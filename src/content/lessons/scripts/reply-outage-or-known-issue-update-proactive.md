<div class="callout callout-intro">

**Purpose:** keep many affected customers informed with one consistent, honest message during an outage, delay or known problem.

**Use it when:** a problem affects many customers at once (site down, payment errors, shipping delays from a warehouse or weather, a widespread bug).

**Goal:** every affected customer gets the same accurate update on a fixed schedule, and the queue does not flood.

**Version:** v1.0

</div>

## Before you send

- [ ]  Tier 2 or the lead has opened a **master ticket** and linked affected tickets
- [ ]  Facts confirmed by the client: what is affected, since when, any workaround, next update time
- [ ]  Wording approved by the client (required for outages and anything public)
- [ ]  Update schedule agreed (for example every 2 hours until fixed)
- [ ]  One person owns sending the updates

## The flow

### 1. First update (as soon as the issue is confirmed)

> **You:** Subject: Update on (ISSUE IN PLAIN WORDS)
Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME),
We're currently having a problem with (WHAT IS AFFECTED) that started at (TIME AND ZONE). Our team is working on it now.
**What this means for you:** (IMPACT, for example "you may not be able to check out" / "your order may arrive 2 to 3 days later than shown").
**What you can do now:** (WORKAROUND, or "nothing is needed from you").
We'll send our next update by (TIME AND ZONE), even if it's not fixed yet.
We're sorry for the disruption.
(YOUR NAME), (COMPANY NAME) Support
> 

*Why it works: what, impact, action and next update time. No guessing, no technical detail.*

### 2. Progress update (on schedule)

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME),
An update on (ISSUE): (PROGRESS IN ONE SENTENCE). (WHAT IS STILL AFFECTED.) Next update by (TIME AND ZONE).
> 

### 3. Resolved

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME),
Good news: (ISSUE) is now fixed as of (TIME AND ZONE). (WHAT THEY SHOULD DO NOW, for example "you can check out as normal" / "your order shipped today, tracking: (TRACKING LINK)").
(If approved:) To say sorry for the trouble, (GOODWILL GESTURE).
Thank you for your patience.
> 

<details class="toggle"><summary>If a customer was financially affected (failed payments, double charges)</summary>

Handle their individual case separately using the Billing Error reply, and link it to the master ticket.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If a customer replies angrily to the update</summary>

Reply personally using STEER. Do not just send the update macro again. Confirm their specific situation and when they will hear next.

</details>

## Objections and tricky replies

| Customer says | You reply |
| --- | --- |
| "When will it be fixed?" | "I don't have a confirmed time yet, and I don't want to guess. You'll get our next update by (TIME AND ZONE)." |
| "What caused it?" | Share only what the client approved: "It was caused by (APPROVED EXPLANATION)." Or: "Our team is still investigating. We'll share more once it's resolved." |
| "I want compensation." | Follow the client's outage policy. Escalate requests outside it. |

## After you send

**Internal note on each linked ticket:**

> Linked to master ticket (MASTER REFERENCE). Update (NUMBER) sent (TIME AND ZONE). Next update due (TIME AND ZONE).
> 

**Tags:** outage or known issue, issue name. **Status:** On-hold until resolved, then Solved after the final update.

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never guess causes or fix times, never blame a supplier or partner by name, and never post or send outage wording the client has not approved. Inconsistent messages during an outage do more damage than the outage.

</div>
