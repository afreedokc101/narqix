<div class="callout callout-intro">

Purpose: find out why an invoice is unpaid or short paid, and get it paid or correctly disputed.

When to use: Side A, when an invoice is past the client's normal payment cycle, was paid short, or has a deduction you do not understand. Side B, adapt it to answer a vendor's payment question with the same calm, factual approach.

Goal of the call: a clear reason and next step for each invoice discussed (payment date, correction needed, or dispute filed).

Version: v1.0

</div>

## Before you dial

- [ ]  AR tracker open, filtered to this client's unpaid and short-paid invoices
- [ ]  For each invoice: number, order number, date submitted, amount, approval date, amount paid, deduction reason if any
- [ ]  Client's payment cycle and dispute window
- [ ]  Proof ready: approval screenshot, photos, bid approval, receipts, submission screenshot
- [ ]  Client's billing contact and preferred channel (portal dispute, email, call)
- [ ]  A maximum of 5 to 10 invoices per call; send a full list by email for more

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never accuse the client of withholding money, never argue about a deduction without proof in hand, and never agree to write off an amount without the vendor owner's approval.

</div>

## The flow

### 1. Opening

> **You:** Hi (BILLING CONTACT NAME), this is (YOUR NAME) calling for (VENDOR COMPANY NAME), vendor number (VENDOR ID). I'm calling about (NUMBER) invoices I'd like to check on. Do you have a few minutes?
> 

*Why it works: telling them how many invoices up front sets the length of the call and shows respect for their time.*

### 2. Start with the oldest

> **You:** The first is invoice (INVOICE NUMBER), for order (ORDER NUMBER), submitted on (SUBMIT DATE) for (AMOUNT). The work was approved on (APPROVAL DATE). I don't see a payment yet. Could you tell me the status?
> 

*Why it works: every detail they need to find it, in the order they search for it.*

### 3. Respond to what you hear

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say &quot;It&#39;s scheduled for the next payment run&quot;</summary>

> **You:** Thank you. Which date is that payment run? ... Great, I'll look for it after (DATE).
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say &quot;It was rejected&quot; or &quot;There&#39;s a problem with it&quot;</summary>

> **You:** I didn't see a rejection notice. Could you tell me the reason? ... Thank you. What do you need from us to fix it: a corrected invoice, a receipt, or photos?
> 

Write down the exact request and the resubmission method.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say &quot;We don&#39;t have that invoice&quot;</summary>

> **You:** No problem. I have a submission confirmation from (SUBMIT DATE). Can I send it to you, with the invoice, right now? ... Where should I send it?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>Short pay: &quot;It was paid, minus a deduction&quot;</summary>

> **You:** Thank you. We received (AMOUNT PAID) on (PAYMENT DATE) against (INVOICE AMOUNT). Could you tell me the reason for the (DIFFERENCE) deduction?
> 

Listen and write down the exact reason. Then:

> **You:** Thank you for explaining. I'll review it with our team. If we have proof that changes it, what's the best way to file a dispute, and what is the deadline?
> 

Do not argue on the call. Review with your Team Lead first.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say &quot;That&#39;s a chargeback from the servicer&quot;</summary>

> **You:** I understand. Could you share the order number and the reason the servicer gave? ... Is there a way for us to respond with our photos and documents?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say &quot;Payments are delayed for everyone right now&quot;</summary>

> **You:** Thank you for being open about it. Is there an expected date when payments will return to normal? ... I'll note that and check back after (DATE).
>

</details>

### 4. Repeat for each invoice, then summarise

> **You:** To summarise: invoice (INVOICE 1) pays on (DATE). Invoice (INVOICE 2) needs (CORRECTION), which I'll send by (TIME). Invoice (INVOICE 3): I'll review the deduction and file a dispute if needed by (DATE). Is that right?
> 

### 5. Close

> **You:** Thank you for your help, (BILLING CONTACT NAME). I'll send a short email now with this summary.
> 

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: billing teams answer polite, organised callers first. Bring the invoice number, order number, dates and amounts every time. A caller who makes the billing contact's search easy gets a faster answer than one who simply asks "where is my money".

</div>

## Objections

| They say | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "Check the portal, don't call." | "Understood. I did check, and these (NUMBER) show no status. I'll use the portal dispute process from now on." |
| "The deduction is final." | "Thank you. Could you tell me where the policy is written, so our team can avoid it in future?" |
| "Your vendor was late on that order." | "Thank you. Could you share the date you have as the completion date? I'll compare it with our submission confirmation." |
| "Send everything by email." | "Of course. I'll send the full list with invoice numbers, amounts and proof today." |

> 

Next step: update the AR tracker for each invoice, send the summary email (Email pack: "Invoice status check" or "Short pay dispute"), and give quality-related deductions to the QC Specialist and Processor.

## Check yourself

<details class="toggle"><summary>1. What details do you give for each invoice?</summary>

Invoice number, order number, submission date, amount, and approval date (plus amount paid and difference for a short pay).

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>2. The billing contact explains a deduction you think is wrong. Do you argue on the call?</summary>

No. Write down the reason, ask how and by when to dispute, review with your Team Lead, and dispute with proof.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>3. Can you agree to write off a small short pay to &quot;keep things friendly&quot;?</summary>

No. Only the vendor owner can approve a write-off.

</details>
