<div class="callout callout-intro">

**Purpose:** answer a furious customer with a reply that calms, takes ownership and starts the fix.

**Use it when:** the message is angry, uses capitals or strong words, mentions repeated contacts, or threatens reviews, chargebacks or leaving.

**Goal:** the customer's next message is calmer, and they believe the problem is being fixed.

**Version:** v1.0

</div>

## Before you send

- [ ]  **Settle:** read the full message twice. Do not reply in the first minute.
- [ ]  Full history read: every past ticket, every promise made, every channel they used
- [ ]  The real problem found in the order, account or payment records
- [ ]  Your fix ready, or the escalation already started
- [ ]  Check for P1 signs: threats of harm, legal action, safety. If present, follow the Escalation Ladder first.
- [ ]  For P2 and above complaints: a second person reviews your draft

## The flow (the STEER Method in writing)

### 1. Tell it back

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME),
You ordered (PRODUCT) on (DATE), it still hasn't arrived, and you've contacted us (NUMBER) times without a clear answer.
> 

*Why it works: when customers see their problem described correctly, they know someone finally listened.*

### 2. Empathize and own

> **You:** I'm sorry. That's not the experience you should have with us, and I'm going to sort it out personally.
> 

*Why it works: one sincere apology and a clear "I" statement of ownership.*

### 3. Execute with a time

> **You:** Here's what I've already done: (ACTION, for example "sent a replacement by express delivery, tracking (TRACKING LINK), arriving Thursday"). (If anything is pending:) I'll email you by (TIME AND ZONE) with (WHAT).
> 

*Why it works: action already taken, with dates, gives back control.*

### 4. A direct path back

> **You:** If anything isn't right, reply to this email and it comes straight back to me.
(YOUR NAME)
> 

<details class="toggle"><summary>If you cannot give them what they demand</summary>

Say it early, once, then move to what you can do:

> **You:** I can't (THEIR DEMAND), but what I can do today is (BEST REAL OPTION). Would that help?
> 

Never hide the "no" at the bottom.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they asked for a manager</summary>

> **You:** I've passed this to our senior team, with everything you've told us, so you won't need to repeat it. (NAME OR "A senior team member") will contact you by (TIME AND ZONE).
> 

Do not refuse and do not argue. Escalate with a full internal note.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the message includes personal insults or abuse</summary>

Respond to the problem, and add one boundary line:

> **You:** I want to help fix this, and I can do that best if we keep things respectful.
> 

If abuse continues, use the Abusive Customer Boundary reply.

</details>

## Objections and tricky replies

| Customer says | You reply |
| --- | --- |
| "I want compensation." | Check the matrix for goodwill options. "I've added (GOODWILL OPTION) to your account for the trouble." If none is allowed: "I've made sure (FIX). I'm not able to add compensation, but I'll personally watch this until it arrives." |
| "I'm reporting you to my bank." | "I understand. You don't need to: (FIX DONE). If you've already started a dispute, let me know so we don't refund twice." Tell Tier 2. |
| "I'm posting this everywhere." | Focus on the fix only. Never bargain or plead about reviews. Flag to Tier 2 if public. |

## Words to cut before sending

"Unfortunately", "as per our policy", "as I said", "calm down", "you should have", "we apologize for any inconvenience", and any sentence that explains why it is not our fault.

## After you send

**Internal note:**

> Issue: complaint, (SUMMARY), contact number (NUMBER). Checked: history, order, tracking. Done: (ACTION). Promised: (WHAT) by (TIME AND ZONE). Next: (WHO) to (WHAT). Customer upset; handle with care.
> 

**Tags:** complaint, root cause, repeat contact. **Priority:** High. **Status:** Open or On-hold until the promise is kept.

Coach's note: write your first draft, then delete every sentence that defends the company. Read what is left. That is usually the right reply.
