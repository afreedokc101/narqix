<div class="callout callout-intro">

**Purpose:** answer questions about using a product, service or feature, accurately and simply.

**Use it when:** a customer asks how to do something, whether a product fits their need, or what a feature does.

**Goal:** the customer can do what they wanted without writing again.

**Version:** v1.0

</div>

## Before you send

- [ ]  Knowledge base and product information checked; answer confirmed from an approved source
- [ ]  If the answer is not in an approved source: ask Tier 2 or the client, do not guess
- [ ]  Customer's product, plan, model or version confirmed
- [ ]  Steps tested yourself, if you have access to a test account or demo
- [ ]  Every question in the message noted

## The flow

### 1. Personal line and the direct answer

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME),
Good question. Yes, you can (DO THE THING). Here's how.
> 

*Why it works: "yes" or "no" first, then the how.*

### 2. Numbered steps, one action each

> **You:**
1. Go to (MENU OR PAGE).
2. Click (BUTTON NAME).
3. Choose (OPTION) and click Save.
> 

*Why it works: one action per step is easy to follow while looking at the screen.*

### 3. Link to more help

> **You:** There's a short guide with pictures here: (KNOWLEDGE BASE LINK)
> 

### 4. Anticipate the next question

> **You:** Tip: once it's on, (WHAT THEY WILL NOTICE NEXT, for example "you'll get a confirmation email within a few minutes").
If anything looks different on your screen, reply with a screenshot and I'll guide you.
Best,
(YOUR NAME)
> 

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the answer is &quot;no, it cannot do that&quot;</summary>

> **You:** Good question. (PRODUCT) can't (DO THE THING) right now. The closest option is (ALTERNATIVE), which (WHAT IT DOES). I've also passed your idea to our product team.
> 

Tag it as a feature request (see Feature Request reply).

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If it is a pre-sale question (&quot;Will this fit / work for me?&quot;)</summary>

Answer only from approved product information (sizes, specifications, compatibility lists). If you are not sure:

> **You:** I want to give you the right answer rather than a guess. I'm checking with our product team, and I'll reply by (TIME AND ZONE).
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If it is a safety question</summary>

Answer only from the client's official safety information. If the customer describes a safety risk (overheating, injury, fire, choking hazard), treat it as Urgent and follow the Escalation Ladder.

</details>

## Objections and tricky replies

| Customer says | You reply |
| --- | --- |
| "I tried that, it didn't work." | "Thanks for trying. Could you send a screenshot of what you see after step 2? That'll show me where it's getting stuck." |
| "Can you just do it for me?" | If your access allows and the client permits it: do it and confirm. If not: offer a call or a step-by-step walkthrough. |
| "Your instructions are confusing." | "Sorry about that. Let me try a simpler way." Rewrite shorter. Tell the knowledge base owner the article needs work. |

## After you send

**Internal note:** only if useful to others (for example "Customer on legacy plan; feature not available, suggested upgrade path").

**Tags:** how-to, product or feature tag. **Status:** Solved.

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never guess about compatibility, safety, medical, legal or technical specifications. A wrong "yes" leads to returns, complaints or harm. "Let me check and reply by (TIME)" is always acceptable.

</div>
