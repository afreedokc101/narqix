<div class="callout callout-intro">

**Purpose:** run a live chat from first message to close, with the right short lines at every stage.

**Use it when:** every live chat conversation.

**Goal:** the customer's issue is solved (or owned) in one chat, and they never wait in silence.

**Version:** v1.0

</div>

## Before you start your chat shift

- [ ]  Chat tool, ticketing system, order system and knowledge base open
- [ ]  Team updates read (known issues, stock, outages)
- [ ]  Concurrency set to your level (2 when new, 3 to 4 after QA sign-off)
- [ ]  Chat macros checked for the client's current wording

## The flow

### 1. Greet within 30 seconds

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME), thanks for reaching out about (TOPIC FROM THEIR FIRST MESSAGE). Let me pull up your order.
> 

*Why it works: name plus topic shows a real person is reading, right away.*

<details class="toggle"><summary>If their first message is just &quot;Hi&quot;</summary>

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME), thanks for reaching out. How can I help today?
>

</details>

### 2. Verify if needed

> **You:** So I can look at your account, could you confirm (VERIFICATION DETAILS)?
> 

Only for account changes or private details, per the client's rules.

### 3. Tell it back

> **You:** Just to check I've got it: your order arrived without the charger, and you need it before Friday. Is that right?
> 

### 4. Hold properly

> **You:** Thanks. Give me about 2 minutes to check stock with our warehouse. I'm right here.
> 

If it takes longer:

> **You:** Still checking, thanks for waiting. About 2 more minutes.
> 

*Why it works: a time estimate turns waiting into trust.*

### 5. Resolve in short messages

> **You:** Good news, I've sent a replacement charger by express.
**You:** It's due Thursday, and here's the tracking: (TRACKING LINK)
**You:** No need to return anything.
> 

One idea per message.

### 6. Close with the outcome

> **You:** So, a new charger arrives Thursday, tracking is in your email too. Anything else I can help with while I'm here?
**Them:** No, that's all, thanks!
**You:** You're welcome, (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME). Have a great day.
> 

<details class="toggle"><summary>Handover to Tier 2 or another team</summary>

> **You:** This needs our billing team, who can approve it. I'm passing them everything we've discussed, so you won't need to repeat anything. They'll (join this chat in a moment / email you by (TIME AND ZONE)).
> 

Write the internal note before handing over. Never say "please hold for a colleague" with no context.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>The customer goes quiet</summary>

> After 3 minutes: **You:** Are you still there, (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME)? I'm here when you're ready.
After 5 more minutes: **You:** I haven't heard back, so I'll close this chat for now. Everything's saved, and you can reply to your email copy anytime.
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>The chat drops or the customer leaves before the fix is confirmed</summary>

Finish the action, then send the result by email from the ticket so the customer has it in writing.

</details>

## Objections and tricky replies

| Customer types | You reply |
| --- | --- |
| "Are you a bot?" | Answer honestly, following the client's disclosure rules: "I'm (YOUR NAME), a member of the support team." (If an AI assistant answered earlier, never claim it was you, and never let an AI claim to be human.) |
| "Hello?? Anyone there??" | "Sorry for the wait, (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME). I'm here. (UPDATE)." Then reduce your concurrency. |
| Long angry paragraph | Read it all. Tell it back in one line. Use STEER in short messages. |

## After the chat

**Internal note:**

> Chat. Issue: (SUMMARY). Done: (ACTION). Promised: (WHAT, WHEN). Next: (NONE OR WHO).
> 

**Tags:** contact reason, outcome. **Status:** Solved, or Open or On-hold with an owner.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: when a chat gets complex or the customer is upset, finish it before accepting a new one. Two good chats beat four rushed ones.
