<div class="callout callout-intro">

**Purpose:** A safe, respectful flow for calming angry callers and setting boundaries with abusive ones.

**When to use:** Any call, outbound or inbound, where the other person is upset, shouting, insulting or threatening.

**Goal of the call:** Bring the conversation to a calm, agreed next step, or end it safely and professionally if it becomes abusive.

**Version:** v1.0

</div>

## Before you dial

Angry calls are rarely planned, but you can be ready:

- [ ]  CRM history checked: previous complaints, promises made, recent contact count
- [ ]  Your authority limits known (what you can offer without approval)
- [ ]  Escalation contacts ready: Campaign Lead, then Operations Manager
- [ ]  Your campaign's emergency protocol open (for threats or a customer in crisis)
- [ ]  Note-taking space ready: you will write while they talk

## The flow

### 1. Let them finish

Do not interrupt, explain or defend. Use short listening signals.

> **You:** "I'm listening." ... "OK." ... "I understand."
> 

*Why it works: most anger needs to be heard before it can be solved. Interrupting restarts the anger.*

### 2. Acknowledge and apologise for the experience

> **You:** "Thank you for telling me. You (WHAT HAPPENED, IN THEIR WORDS). That's really frustrating, and I'm sorry you've had to deal with it."
> 

*Why it works: repeating their words proves you listened. Apologising for the experience is sincere without admitting fault you cannot confirm.*

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they are angry about being called too many times</summary>

> **You:** "I'm sorry about that. That's not the experience we want you to have. Would you like me to remove your number so you don't get any more calls from us?"
> 

If yes, or if they already said "stop", code DNC immediately. See "Stop, Do-Not-Call and Wrong Number Requests".

</details>

### 3. Take ownership and ask to understand

> **You:** "I'd like to help sort this out. Can I ask a couple of quick questions so I get it right?"
> 

Then ask only what you need: dates, order or job number, what they were told, what they want now.

> **You:** "What would a good outcome look like for you?"
> 

*Why it works: asking what they want moves them from venting to solving, and often their request is smaller than you expect.*

### 4. Say what you will do, and when

> **You:** "Here's what I'm going to do. (ACTION). You'll hear from (ME OR ROLE) by (TIME) your time, even if we're still working on it."
> 

<details class="toggle"><summary>If what they want is outside your authority</summary>

> **You:** "I'm not able to approve that myself, but I'm taking it to my manager right now with everything you've told me, so you won't need to repeat it. I'll call you back by (TIME) your time."
> 

Escalate as P2 (or P1 if it involves money at risk, legal threats or safety).

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they ask for a manager</summary>

> **You:** "Of course. I'll make sure a manager gets this. Let me take down the details so they have the full picture and you don't have to explain it all again."
> 

Take full details, read them back, and escalate. Give a realistic callback time. Never promise a manager will call "in five minutes" unless you know it is true.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the answer is no</summary>

> **You:** "I want to be straight with you. I'm not able to (WHAT THEY ASKED), because (ONE-SENTENCE REASON). What I can do is (BEST ALTERNATIVE). Would that help?"
>

</details>

### 5. Set boundaries if the caller becomes abusive

Abuse means personal insults, slurs, sexual comments, or repeated swearing **at you**. A swear word aimed at the situation (for example, cursing about a late delivery) is not abuse; keep helping.

**First boundary:**

> **You:** "I can hear how upset you are, and I want to help sort this out. I can only continue if we keep it respectful."
> 

**Second boundary (clear warning):**

> **You:** "If the language continues, I'll need to end the call. I'd still like to help you."
> 

**Ending the call:**

> **You:** "I'm going to end the call now. Someone from our team will follow up with you. Goodbye."
> 

*Why it works: three calm, predictable steps protect you, give the caller every chance to reset, and show any reviewer that you acted professionally.*

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they threaten violence against you, the client or anyone else</summary>

> **You:** "I'm ending this call now."
> 

End the call. Write down the exact words and time. Report to your Campaign Lead immediately as **P1**. Do not call back.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they threaten a lawsuit, a complaint to a regulator, or the media</summary>

> **You:** "I understand, and I'm sorry you feel that way. I'll make sure your concern goes to my manager today, and I'll note that you don't want further calls if that's what you'd like."
> 

Do not argue, explain the law, or admit fault. If they asked to stop contact, code DNC. Escalate as **P1** the same shift.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say they might harm themselves</summary>

Stay calm and kind. Do not end the call abruptly.

> **You:** "I'm really glad you told me. I care about what happens to you. Are you safe right now?"
**You:** "You can call or text 988 any time to talk to someone right now. They're there to help."
> 

Follow your campaign's emergency protocol and alert your Campaign Lead immediately (**P1**). If they say they are in immediate danger, follow your campaign's emergency steps.

</details>

### 6. Close calmly

> **You:** "Thank you for your patience with me today. To confirm, (NEXT STEP, WHO, DAY, DATE, TIME, YOUR TIME). Is there anything else I should pass on?"
> 

Let them hang up first.

## Objections and hot phrases

| They say | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "You people never listen!" | "I'm listening now, and I want to get this right. Tell me what happened." |
| "This is ridiculous." | "I agree this shouldn't have happened. Here's what I can do." |
| "I want to speak to someone in America." | "I understand. I'll pass this to my manager with all the details, and they'll contact you by (TIME) your time." |
| "Are you even listening?" | "Yes. You said (REPEAT THEIR LAST POINT). Let me fix that." |
| "I'm never using you again." | "I'm sorry we let you down. Before you go, can I at least make sure this is put right?" |
| "Stop calling me!" | "Understood. I'll remove your number right now. Sorry to bother you." (Code DNC.) |

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never raise your voice, argue, use sarcasm or say "calm down". Never hang up without the boundary steps (except for threats of violence). Never mute and comment about the caller. Never make a promise or offer above your authority to end the call faster.

</div>

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Speak a little slower and a little lower than the caller. Write their key words as they speak so you can repeat them exactly. When you hear the first sigh or pause, that is the moment to say "Here's what I'm going to do." Anger usually drops right there.

</div>

## After the call

1. Take one minute: three slow breaths before your next contact.
2. Enter the disposition and a factual note within 5 minutes. Quote abusive or threatening words exactly; never add your opinion.
3. Escalate as required (P1 for threats and crisis, P2 for complaints above your authority).
4. Keep every promise on time.

```
(DATE) (TIME AND ZONE) · (CHANNEL) · (OUTCOME CODE, e.g. ESCALATED P2, DNC, ENDED-ABUSE)
Who: (NAME AND ROLE)
What happened: (FACTS: WHAT THEY WERE UPSET ABOUT, WHAT THEY ASKED FOR)
Key details: (ORDER OR JOB NUMBER, DATES, ANY EXACT THREATENING OR ABUSIVE WORDS QUOTED)
Boundaries used: (NONE, FIRST, SECOND, CALL ENDED AT TIME)
Promised: (WHAT AND WHEN)
Next step: (ACTION) by (OWNER) on (DATE, TIME AND ZONE). Escalated to (ROLE) at (TIME AND ZONE).
```

## Check yourself

1. What are the first two steps with any angry caller?
2. What is the difference between frustrated swearing and abuse?
3. Say the three boundary lines in order.
4. A caller threatens to hurt you. What do you do?
5. What do you write in the CRM about an abusive caller?

<details class="toggle toggle-answers"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Let them finish, then acknowledge and apologise for the experience.
2. Frustrated swearing is about the situation; abuse is insults, slurs, sexual comments or repeated swearing aimed at you.
3. "I can only continue if we keep it respectful." / "If the language continues, I'll need to end the call." / "I'm going to end the call now. Someone from our team will follow up."
4. End the call, write down the exact words and time, and report it to your Campaign Lead immediately as P1.
5. Facts only, with any abusive or threatening words quoted exactly, the boundaries used and the time the call ended. No opinions.

</details>
