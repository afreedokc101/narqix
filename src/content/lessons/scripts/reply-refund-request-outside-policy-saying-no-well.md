<div class="callout callout-intro">

**Purpose:** decline a refund that does not meet policy, honestly and kindly, and offer the best real alternative.

**Use it when:** the request is outside the return window, the item is used or customized, or the product is non-refundable under the client's policy.

**Goal:** the customer understands the decision and accepts an alternative, or knows exactly how it will be reviewed.

**Version:** v1.0

</div>

## Before you send

- [ ]  Policy checked: exact window, conditions, exclusions
- [ ]  Dates checked: order, delivery, request
- [ ]  Refund and Exception Matrix checked: what alternatives can you offer (store credit, exchange, repair, partial)?
- [ ]  Any special circumstance (carrier delay, defect, first-time customer) that the matrix says should go to Tier 2
- [ ]  Every other question in the message noted

## The flow

### 1. Personal line

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME),
Thanks for letting me know the (PRODUCT) didn't work out for you.
> 

### 2. The "no", early and with the reason as a fact

> **You:** I checked your order, and it was delivered on (DELIVERY DATE). That's outside our (NUMBER)-day return window, so I can't refund it to your card.
> 

*Why it works: a clear, early "no" with a fact is kinder than a hopeful paragraph that ends in "unfortunately".*

### 3. The best real alternative

> **You:** What I can do today is add (CREDIT AMOUNT) in store credit to your account. It never expires, and you can use it on anything in the shop. Just reply "yes" and it'll be there within the hour.
> 

*Why it works: "here's what I can do" gives the customer a path forward and a sense of control.*

### 4. Close

> **You:** Best,
(YOUR NAME)
> 

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the matrix allows no alternative</summary>

> **You:** I'm not able to offer a refund or credit for this order. If the (PRODUCT) has a fault, our (WARRANTY OR REPAIR SERVICE) may be able to help. Here's how to start: (LINK OR STEPS).
> 

Point to anything genuinely useful: warranty, repair, how-to help, resale guidance. Never invent options.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If there is a special circumstance (for example, the delivery was weeks late)</summary>

> **You:** I can see the delivery was (NUMBER) weeks late, which cut into your return time. I've asked our senior team to review an exception, and you'll hear back by (DATE AND TIME ZONE).
> 

Escalate to Tier 2 with a recommendation. Do not promise the outcome.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the customer says the item is faulty</summary>

This may not be a return outside policy at all. Faults often fall under warranty or consumer law rights that differ by country and state. Ask for photos or a description, and follow the client's defect process (see Damaged or Wrong Item).

</details>

## Objections and tricky replies

| Customer says | You reply |
| --- | --- |
| "That's ridiculous, it's only a few days late." | "I understand, it's close. The window applies to every order, so I can't refund it to your card, but the store credit is ready right now if you'd like it." (If the matrix gives Tier 2 discretion for near misses, escalate.) |
| "I'll leave a bad review." | "That's your choice, and I'd still like to help. The credit offer stands if you change your mind." Never bargain policy for reviews. |
| "I want to speak to a manager." | "Of course. I'll pass this to our senior team with all the details, and they'll reply by (TIME AND ZONE)." Escalate. |
| "Other stores would refund this." | "I hear you. Our window is (NUMBER) days for everyone. Here's what I can do: (ALTERNATIVE)." |

## After you send

**Internal note:**

> Issue: refund request outside window (delivered (DATE), requested (DATE)). Checked: policy, matrix. Done: declined refund, offered (ALTERNATIVE). Promised: credit within 1 hour if accepted. Next: apply credit on "yes".
> 

**Tags:** refund, outside policy, outcome tag. **Status:** Pending (waiting for the customer's choice).

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never use "as per our policy" as the whole answer, and never grant an exception you are not authorized to give just to end a difficult conversation. Escalate instead.

</div>
