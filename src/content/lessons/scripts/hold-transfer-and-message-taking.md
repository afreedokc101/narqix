<div class="callout callout-intro">

**Purpose:** The Narqix standard for putting callers on hold, transferring them warmly, and taking complete messages.

**When to use:** Any call where you need to check something, connect the caller to someone else, or pass on a message.

**Goal of the call:** The caller never feels forgotten, never repeats their story, and gets a reliable next step.

**Version:** v1.0

</div>

## Before you dial

- [ ]  Your campaign's transfer list: who handles what, their hours, and how to reach them
- [ ]  Your system's hold, mute and transfer functions practised (hold is not the same as mute)
- [ ]  The message-taking fields open in the CRM
- [ ]  Client's business hours and time zone known, so callback promises are realistic

## The flow: hold

### 1. Ask permission and give a reason and time

> **You:** "Let me check that for you. May I put you on a brief hold? It should be about (ONE TO TWO) minutes."
> 

Wait for a yes.

*Why it works: people accept waiting when they know why and for how long.*

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say &quot;No, I can&#39;t hold&quot;</summary>

> **You:** "No problem. I'll find out and call you back by (TIME) your time. Is this the best number?"
> 

Take the details, confirm the callback time, and keep the promise.

</details>

### 2. Check back at least every 60 seconds

> **You:** "Thanks for holding, (NAME). I'm still checking on this. Are you OK to hold a little longer, or would you prefer a callback?"
> 

### 3. Return and thank them

> **You:** "Thank you for waiting. Here's what I found: (ANSWER)."
> 

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never use mute instead of hold, and never talk about the caller while they are on hold or mute. Never leave a caller on hold for more than 60 seconds without checking back. Never put a caller on hold without asking first (except in a genuine emergency).

</div>

## The flow: warm transfer

### 1. Tell the caller who and why

> **You:** "The best person for this is our (ROLE, FOR EXAMPLE SCHEDULING TEAM OR BILLING SPECIALIST). I'm going to connect you now and explain everything so you won't need to repeat it. Is that OK?"
> 

### 2. Brief the receiver before connecting

Place the caller on hold, reach the receiver, and give a 15-second summary:

> **You:** "Hi, it's (YOUR NAME). I have (CALLER NAME) on the line about (REASON). (KEY DETAILS: ACCOUNT OR JOB NUMBER, WHAT YOU ALREADY CHECKED, WHAT THEY WANT). Can you take them now?"
> 

### 3. Introduce and hand over

> **You:** "(CALLER NAME), I have (RECEIVER NAME AND ROLE) on the line. I've shared the details. Thank you for calling, and take care."
> 

*Why it works: a warm transfer saves the caller from repeating their story, which is the most common complaint about transfers.*

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the receiver is not available</summary>

Return to the caller:

> **You:** "Thanks for holding. (ROLE) isn't available right now. I can take a detailed message and make sure they call you back by (REALISTIC TIME) your time. Would that work?"
> 

Then take a full message (below). Never "blind transfer" a caller into an unanswered line or voicemail without telling them.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the caller wants a specific person by name</summary>

> **You:** "Let me check if (PERSON) is available. May I tell them who's calling and what it's about?"
> 

Follow your campaign's rules on which internal names may be shared.

</details>

## The flow: taking a message

### 1. Collect every field

> **You:** "I'll make sure this gets to the right person. Can I start with your full name? ... And how do you spell that?"
> 

| Field | What to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Name | Full name, spelled with the phonetic alphabet if unclear |
| New or existing | New customer, or existing (with account, order, job or file number) |
| Phone | Best callback number, read back in groups |
| Email | If they prefer email, spelled and read back |
| Reason | In their words, one or two sentences |
| For whom | Person or team they want |
| Urgency | Their deadline, and anything time-sensitive (for example, no heating or cooling, a closing date) |
| Best time | Best callback time, **with their time zone** |

### 2. Read it back

> **You:** "Let me read that back. (NAME), at (NUMBER IN GROUPS), about (REASON), for (PERSON OR TEAM). Best time to call is (TIME AND ZONE). Did I get everything right?"
> 

### 3. Set a realistic expectation

> **You:** "I'm sending this to (PERSON OR TEAM) now. You can expect a call back by (REALISTIC TIME, BASED ON THE CLIENT'S HOURS)."
> 

Only promise times you know are realistic. If the office is closed, say so: "Our office opens at 8 AM Central tomorrow, so you'll hear from them in the morning."

### 4. Close

> **You:** "Is there anything else I can help with? ... Thank you for calling (COMPANY NAME). Have a good day."
> 

## Objections

| They say | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "I've already explained this three times!" | "I'm sorry you've had to do that. I'll write it all down and brief the next person myself, so this is the last time." |
| "Just give me their direct number." | "I'm not able to share direct numbers, but I'll make sure they get your message right away and call you back by (TIME)." |
| "How long will this hold be?" | Give an honest estimate. If it may be long, offer a callback. |
| "I need this fixed today." | "I understand. I'm marking this as urgent for (TEAM). Their hours today end at (TIME AND ZONE), so..." (Be honest about what is possible.) |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Your message is only as good as the callback it creates. Ask yourself, "Could the person receiving this call back without asking me anything?" If not, add the missing detail before you send it.

</div>

## After the call

Send the message through your campaign's method within 5 minutes, and log it:

```
(DATE) (TIME AND ZONE) · Inbound call · MESSAGE-TAKEN (or TRANSFERRED)
Who: (CALLER NAME), (NEW OR EXISTING, ACCOUNT OR JOB NUMBER)
What happened: (REASON IN THEIR WORDS)
Key details: Callback (NUMBER), email (IF GIVEN), urgency (DETAILS)
Promised: Callback by (TIME AND ZONE) from (PERSON OR TEAM)
Next step: (PERSON OR TEAM) to call back by (DATE, TIME AND ZONE). Message sent at (TIME AND ZONE) via (CAMPAIGN METHOD).
```

## Check yourself

1. What three things do you say before putting someone on hold?
2. How often do you check back with a caller on hold?
3. What is a warm transfer, and why is it better than a blind transfer?
4. List the eight fields of a complete message.
5. The office is closed until morning. What callback time do you promise?

<details class="toggle toggle-answers"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Ask permission, give the reason, and give an estimated time.
2. At least every 60 seconds.
3. You brief the receiver and introduce the caller, so they never repeat their story. A blind transfer passes the caller with no context.
4. Name, new or existing, phone, email, reason, for whom, urgency, best callback time with zone.
5. A realistic time after opening, said clearly: "Our office opens at (TIME AND ZONE), so you'll hear from them in the morning."

</details>
