<div class="callout callout-intro">

**Purpose:** Make sure every customer knows when the technician is coming, is ready for the visit, and hears about any change before it happens.

**When to use:** Day-before confirmations, morning-of reminders, on-the-way updates, and running-late calls.

**Goal of the call:** A confirmed appointment (or a clean reschedule), with the customer never surprised.

**Version:** v1.0

</div>

## Before you dial

- [ ]  Tomorrow's board (for confirmations) or today's live board (for on-the-way and late calls)
- [ ]  Job record: reason for visit, window, technician, fee disclosed, access notes
- [ ]  Confirmation text template and the client's reply options
- [ ]  Allowed calling hours for the customer's time zone
- [ ]  Cancellation list, in case a slot opens

## Part A: Day-before confirmation

### Step 1: Text first (by 5 PM customer local time)

> Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME), this is (COMPANY NAME) confirming your appointment tomorrow, (DAY), between (WINDOW) for (SERVICE TYPE). Reply YES to confirm or call (OFFICE PHONE) to reschedule. Reply STOP to opt out.
> 

### Step 2: Call anyone who has not confirmed

> **You:** Hi, is this (CUSTOMER NAME)?
**Them:** Yes.
**You:** Hi (CUSTOMER NAME), this is (YOUR NAME) from (COMPANY NAME). I'm calling to confirm your appointment tomorrow between (WINDOW) for (SERVICE TYPE). Does that still work for you?
> 

*Why it works: a quick, specific question gets a clear yes or no, and catches problems while there is still time to fix them.*

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say: &quot;Yes, that works.&quot;</summary>

> **You:** Great. Will an adult be home during that window? And is there anything the technician should know, like a gate code or pets?
**You:** Perfect. The technician will call or text when they're on the way. Have a good evening.
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say: &quot;I need to change it.&quot;</summary>

> **You:** No problem. I have (OPTION 1) or (OPTION 2). Which is better?
> 

Move the job, offer the freed slot to the cancellation list, and note the change.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say: &quot;Actually, it started working again, I want to cancel.&quot;</summary>

> **You:** I'm glad it's working. Sometimes a problem comes and goes, so would you like to keep the appointment and have the technician take a quick look, or would you prefer to cancel? (If cancel:) No problem, I've cancelled it. If it happens again, just call us and we'll get you in.
> 

Note the reason (UB-Fixed or as your client codes cancellations).

</details>

### Voicemail (if no answer)

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER NAME), this is (YOUR NAME) from (COMPANY NAME), calling to confirm your appointment tomorrow between (WINDOW). If that still works, no need to call back. If you need to change it, please call us at (OFFICE PHONE). Thank you!
> 

## Part B: On-the-way update

### Text (when the technician leaves for the home)

> Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME), (TECH FIRST NAME) from (COMPANY NAME) is on the way and should arrive in about (MINUTES) minutes. Reply here if you need anything.
> 

### Call (if the client uses calls, or no text consent)

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER NAME), it's (YOUR NAME) from (COMPANY NAME). Good news, (TECH FIRST NAME) is on the way to you now and should be there in about (MINUTES) minutes. Is everything still good on your end?
> 

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say: &quot;I&#39;m not home yet!&quot;</summary>

> **You:** Thanks for letting me know. How soon can you or another adult be there? (Then:) I'll let (TECH FIRST NAME) know.
> 

Tell the technician straight away. If the delay is long, the dispatcher decides whether to swap the order of jobs.

</details>

## Part C: Running late (before the window closes)

Call, do not just text. Call at least 30 minutes before the window ends.

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER NAME), this is (YOUR NAME) from (COMPANY NAME). I'm calling about your appointment today between (WINDOW). I want to give you an honest update: your technician's earlier job is taking longer than planned. We now expect them between (NEW WINDOW). Does that still work for you, or would you like to move to (ALTERNATIVE)?
> 

*Why it works: the customer hears it first from us, gets a real reason, and gets a choice. Most people say "That's fine, thanks for calling."*

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say: &quot;This is ridiculous, I took the day off.&quot;</summary>

> **You:** You're right to be frustrated, and I'm sorry. You planned your day around us. Let me see what I can do. (Check the board for a faster option.) The best I can do is (OPTION). Would that help?
> 

If the customer is still upset, offer a call from the office manager. Never promise a discount or refund unless the client allows it.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If you cannot reach them</summary>

Leave a voicemail, then send a text:

> Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME), it's (COMPANY NAME). We tried to call you. Your technician is running late and we now expect them between (NEW WINDOW). Please reply or call (OFFICE PHONE) if that doesn't work. We're sorry for the delay.
>

</details>

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never say the technician is on the way if they are not. Never let a window close without the customer hearing from us. Never blame the technician or another customer by name ("the last customer kept him there for hours"). Keep reasons simple and honest.

</div>

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** When a customer is not home and the technician is close, call the customer immediately rather than texting. A two-minute call can save a wasted trip, which costs the client far more than the call.

</div>

## Objections

| They say | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "Can you give me an exact time?" | "I can't promise an exact minute, but the technician will text when they're on the way, about (MINUTES) minutes before." |
| "I forgot about the fee, how much was it?" | Repeat the disclosed fee exactly as noted in the job |
| "Can you send someone else who's faster?" | "Let me check the board." Offer only what dispatch can actually do |

## Check yourself

1. By what time are day-before confirmation texts sent?
2. How long before the window closes should you make a running-late call?
3. A customer says the problem fixed itself. What do you offer before cancelling?
4. Why is a late update a call and not only a text?

<details class="toggle toggle-answers"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. By 5 PM customer local time.
2. At least 30 minutes before the window ends.
3. The option to keep the appointment so the technician can take a quick look, since problems can come and go.
4. A call shows respect, lets the customer choose a new option, and confirms they actually got the message.

</details>
