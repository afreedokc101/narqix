**Purpose:** One method, six power moves and 58 ready answers for the things sellers say that slow a call down.

**Use it when:** A seller pushes back on price, trust, timing, paperwork, family, tenants or anything else.

**Goal of every objection:** Find the real concern, answer it with confidence, and move the call one step forward (or end it kindly).

**Track:** Real Estate  ·  **Role:** All Callers  ·  **Time to complete:** 60 min  ·  **Version:** v1.0

## Why this handbook exists

An objection is not a wall. It is information. When a seller says "your offer is insulting," they are telling you what they fear, what they hope for, and what they need to hear next.

Most callers fail at objections for two reasons. They argue, or they give up. Narqix callers do neither. They use one calm method every time, they pick the right level of firmness for the person in front of them, and they always know the next question.

This handbook gives you:

1. **The STEP Method**, the four moves you use on every objection.
2. **Three firmness levels** (Soft, Steady, Straight) so you can match the seller's mood.
3. **The six power moves** that the best closers use again and again.
4. **58 objections in 10 groups**, each with what it really means, three answers, and the question that moves the call forward.
5. **Rules, drills and a quick-reference table** so you can use this live on a call.

## The STEP Method

Every objection, every time, gets the same four moves. Say them in order. Keep each one short.

| Step | What you do | How long | Sample words |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S: Settle** | Let them finish. Breathe. Agree with the feeling, not always with the claim. | One short line | "That's fair." "I hear you." "That makes sense." |
| **T: Test** | Ask one calm question to find the real concern behind the words. | One question | "Can I ask what's behind that?" "Is it the price, or the timing?" |
| **E: Explain** | Give one clear, confident answer. Three sentences at most. | Three sentences | "Here's how our number works..." |
| **P: Propose** | Ask the question that moves the call one step forward, or close the call kindly. | One question | "Which part feels most off to you?" |

*Why it works: people relax when they feel heard (Settle), they tell you the real reason when you ask (Test), they trust short confident facts (Explain), and calls only move when you ask for the next step (Propose).*

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** The most common mistake is jumping straight to Explain. If you answer before you Settle and Test, you are answering the words, not the worry. Sellers can feel the difference.

</div>

### The STEP Method in one real example

> **Them:** Your offer is a joke. I'm not giving my house away.
**You (Settle):** I hear you. It's lower than you hoped.
**You (Test):** Can I ask, is it the number itself, or how we got to it?
**Them:** I just don't see how it's so low.
**You (Explain):** Fair question. Homes like yours sell for about 250K after repairs. Yours needs about 45K of work, and a buyer also pays holding and selling costs. Our number is what's left after those.
**You (Propose):** Which part feels most off to you: the value, the repairs, or the costs?
> 

## The three firmness levels

Each objection below has three answers. They carry the same message with different firmness. Pick the level that matches the person.

| Level | Use it when | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Soft** | The seller is grieving, older, stressed, emotional, or this is your first contact | Warm, slow, lots of space |
| **Steady** | Your default for most calls | Calm, clear, friendly |
| **Straight** | The seller is busy, direct, business-minded, or the same objection comes back a second time | Short, confident, still kind |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Straight never means rude. It means fewer words. If you ever feel your voice getting faster or louder, drop one level.

## The rules (read before the objections)

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:**

- Never argue, interrupt or raise your voice.
- Never give legal, tax or financial advice. Suggest an attorney, accountant or the title company.

</div>

<div class="callout callout-never">

**A stop request is not an objection.** "Stop calling," "take me off your list," "don't contact me," "remove my number," in any words or language, on a call or a text: say "Understood. I'm removing your number now. Sorry for the bother. Have a good day." End the call. Code it DNC immediately, across every campaign. Never call or text that number again. There is no rebuttal for this, ever.

</div>

**The Two-Try Rule.** You may address the same objection twice. The second time, dig deeper with the deciding-factor question or a takeaway (below). If the seller says it a third time, offer a follow-up, thank them, and log the call.

## The six power moves

These moves show up again and again in the answers below. Learn them by heart. They are what separate a Narqix closer from a script reader.

| Move | What you do | Sample words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The underwriter frame** | You do not set the price. You collect the details and submit them to your underwriters, who decide if the property qualifies. This keeps you on the seller's side of the table. | "I don't come up with the numbers. Once I have the details, I submit them to my underwriters, and if the property qualifies, I'll have a competitive offer for you before we hang up." |
| **The good-news approval** | When a seller pushes on price after the offer, start with the win: the property was approved. Then offer to go back for them. | "The hard part is done. Your property got approved, and that's good news. I'm on your side, and I'm happy to go back to the underwriters. How close to our number could you be comfortable with?" |
| **The volume frame** | Mention how many homes your client buys, so the seller knows you are serious and you do not need this one house. | "We buy 20 to 30 homes every month in this area." |
| **The deciding-factor question** | Ask what would actually decide it, and what would change with more time. | "What would be the deciding factor for you? What would change between today and next week?" |
| **The takeaway** | Calmly step back so the seller has to lean in. | "It sounds like you may not really be looking to sell, and that's completely fine. But if you are, I'd love to help." |
| **The approval hold** | When a seller agrees to sign at a number, put them on a short hold while you get it approved, then come back with good news. | "Perfect. Give me one second while I get that approved." *(hold)* "Good news. We're good to go." |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** The default volume line for seller calls is 20 to 30 homes a month. Dispositions calls to buyers use the 30 to 40 properties a month frame from the Buyer Qualification Call. If your client's fact sheet gives a different number, use theirs. Say it calmly and in passing, as a plain fact, never as a boast.

</div>

## The 58 objections

Each toggle follows the same pattern: **What it really means**, three answers (Soft, Steady, Straight), and **Move it forward**, the question that takes the next step.

### Group 1: Price and value

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PR1. &quot;I get these calls all the time. I&#39;m not taking a lowball offer.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They have been called many times and expect disrespect. But they are still on the line, which means some part of them is curious.

**Soft:**

> **You:** I hear you, and I'd feel the same in your shoes. We're a little different. We're a local buying team in (MARKET), not here to hassle anyone, just looking for the right fit. Can I ask a few questions first? And if the timing isn't right, is it okay if I follow up in a few months?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** I get that a lot, (SELLER NAME). We're one of the bigger buyers in the area, which is why your property was matched with us. I don't come up with the numbers in our office. Once I have the details, I submit everything, and if the property qualifies, I'll have a competitive offer for you by the end of this call.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Fair enough. We buy a lot of homes in this area, and our number comes from our underwriters, not from me guessing. Five minutes of questions, and if the property qualifies, you'll have a real offer before we hang up. Okay?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** To start, how would you describe the condition of the house right now?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PR2. &quot;Just give me a number. Get to the point.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They are busy, testing you, or worried about a long sales pitch. They want control of the call.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Of course. I want to respect your time. A guess right now wouldn't be fair to you. Can I ask four quick questions, then explain how the number is made?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Well, (SELLER NAME), that is the point of this call today. My job is to get a clear picture of your needs, your timeline and the condition of the property. Once I have those details, I submit everything to my underwriters to see if the property qualifies, and we get you a competitive offer.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** If I gave you a number now, it would be a guess, and guesses are usually low. Two minutes of questions, then it goes to underwriting, and you get a real offer instead.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** What would you say the house needs, if anything?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PR3. &quot;You called me, so you must already have a number in mind.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** A fair point from their side. They suspect you are hiding something.

**Soft:**

> **You:** That makes sense to ask. We know your area, but we don't know your house. A new roof or an old kitchen changes the number a lot.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** We reached out because we buy in your area. What we don't know is what's inside. If you were buying a house, you'd want to know its condition first too.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** I know, but I don't know your property's current condition or what you've updated. If you were buying a home, you'd want to know everything about it too. I run my numbers on that information, then I go over it with my partner, we finalize the numbers, and I call you back.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Has anything been updated in the last few years, like the roof or the kitchen?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PR4. &quot;Your offer is insulting. It&#39;s way too low.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** The number is far from what they expected, and they feel disrespected. Handle the feeling first and the numbers second. Sometimes this is also an opening move in a negotiation.

**Soft:**

> **You:** I'm sorry it landed that way. That was never my intent. Can I show you how we got there? If it still doesn't make sense, that's okay.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** I hear you. It's lower than you hoped. Our number starts with the value after repairs, then takes out the repairs and the costs a buyer carries. Can I walk you through those pieces?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** I understand it's not what you wanted. Here's the good news: the hard part is over. Your property was approved. Now, I'm on your side, and I'm happy to go back to the underwriters and see how close I can get you. Tell me what you need.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Which part feels most off to you: the value, the repairs, or the costs?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PR5. &quot;That&#39;s too low. I need at least (SELLER PRICE).&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Their number is anchored to something. It might be a real need (mortgage payoff, next home, a split between siblings) or a hope (an online estimate). Your job is to find out which.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Thank you for telling me your number. That really helps. Can I ask how you arrived at it?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Okay, so we're apart by about (PRICE GAP). Is (SELLER PRICE) based on something you owe or need, or on what you've seen online?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Well, the hard part is over, and the property did get approved, which is good news. Now, (SELLER NAME), I'm on your side, and I'm happy to go back to the underwriters to see if I can get you closer to what you need. How close to our number would you be comfortable with?
> 

**If their number is far above anything a cash buyer can pay:**

> **You:** That's a great price for the open market. But as a cash offer, our numbers are just too far apart. If you listed it, you might get into that range. Just keep in mind you'd have closing costs, fees, commissions and repairs on top of that.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** If we can't reach (SELLER PRICE), what's the lowest number that still solves your situation?
> 

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** If the number is tied to a payoff, ask for the payoff amount. A seller who owes 120K cannot accept 110K, no matter how good your rebuttal is. That is a fact for Acquisitions, not an objection to beat.

</div>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PR6. &quot;The online estimate (or my tax value) says it&#39;s worth more.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They trust a public number. They need to understand why a cash, as-is price is a different product.

**Soft:**

> **You:** I understand your number comes from that estimate. Here's the thing: you could have three different appraisers come to your home, and you'd get three different numbers. It's an opinion. A home is only worth what someone is willing to pay for it. This is a real offer that we're confident taking to the closing table.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Those online estimates aren't always accurate. They pull sales from within a certain distance and give you an average. We go by the year built, the square footage, the beds and baths, and the condition of each home to build our offer.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** You could list the property on the open market, but that could take 60 or 90 days, with closing costs, commissions and all of that. If we buy it for cash, we'd have to be at (OFFER AMOUNT), and we take care of all those fees and headaches for you. Is that something that's important to you?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Would it help to see the recent sales we're using?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PR7. &quot;We put a lot of work into this house.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Pride. They want their effort recognized and they fear you will ignore the upgrades.

**Soft:**

> **You:** It sounds like you've really taken care of it, and that always adds value. We're not here to lowball you. We just want to see if we can find something that works for both sides. Mind if I run some numbers on what you've done?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** That matters, and it should show in the number. What have you updated, and about when?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Good. Upgrades lower the repair estimate, and that raises the offer. Walk me through the list.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Let's start with the big items: roof, heating and cooling, plumbing, electrical. Which ones were updated?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PR8. &quot;I&#39;m not giving it away.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Fear of being taken advantage of.

**Soft:**

> **You:** And you shouldn't. Nobody should. We only want a deal that's fair for you too, or we don't do it.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** I agree. Our offer is based on numbers you can check. If it doesn't help you, you keep your house and we stay friendly.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Of course, and you shouldn't. We make fair, no-pressure offers based on the condition and the current market. Sometimes it just helps to have a number in hand, even if you don't do anything with it yet.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** What would a fair result look like to you?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PR9. &quot;My neighbor&#39;s house just sold for (COMP PRICE).&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They are comparing their house to a renovated, retail sale.

**Soft:**

> **You:** That's good news for your street. Do you know what condition it was in when it sold?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** That sale actually helps us. If it was renovated and sold with an agent, it's a good guide to your home's value after repairs. Then we subtract the work your house needs.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** That price is probably close to your home's value after repairs. Our offer starts from sales like that, then takes out the work and the costs to get there.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Was that house updated inside, or about the same as yours?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PR10. &quot;Why is your number so far below market value?&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** A real question. They want the logic, and they will respect a clear answer.

**Soft:**

> **You:** That's a really good question. Can I explain it simply?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** A cash buyer pays for the house, then the repairs, then taxes and insurance while it's being fixed, then the costs of selling. They also need room for risk. The offer is what's left.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Here's an example. Worth 250K fixed. Needs 45K of work. About 25K in holding and selling costs. Plus the buyer's margin for risk. That's why cash offers sit well below retail. What you get in return is speed, certainty and no repairs.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Right now, is speed and certainty more important to you, or the highest possible price?
>

</details>

### Group 2: Trust and legitimacy

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TR1. &quot;How did you get my number?&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** A privacy worry. Answer it plainly and offer removal. Many sellers relax once they hear the plain answer.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Fair question. Property ownership is public record, and we use a data service that matches owners to phone numbers. If you'd prefer no calls, I'll remove you right now.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** We use public property records and a contact data provider. I'm sorry if the call surprised you. Would you like me to remove your number, or is it okay to ask one quick question?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Public records and a contact data service. Nothing private. Do you want me to remove you, or do you have one minute?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Thanks. Have you ever thought about selling (PROPERTY ADDRESS)?
> 

If they ask to be removed: follow the stop-request rule above. Code DNC.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TR2. &quot;Is this a scam?&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Fear. They have heard about real estate fraud. Do not get defensive. Give them ways to check you.

**Soft:**

> **You:** I'm glad you asked. You always should. I'm (YOUR NAME) with (COMPANY NAME). Nothing happens without a written agreement, and the closing goes through a licensed title company or closing attorney.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Good question. Here's how you stay protected: you never pay us anything, the money and the deed are handled by a title company or attorney, and money only moves at closing. You can look us up at (COMPANY WEBSITE).
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** (SELLER NAME), we don't have the time or the energy to scam anyone out of their property. We have five acquisition managers in our office making offers every single day. If we don't buy your home, we will certainly buy the other homes we're looking at. And we hold a business accreditation with (ACCREDITATION BODY). A scam couldn't have that. It's just not possible.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Would it help if I emailed you our company details so you can check us out first?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TR3. &quot;I&#39;ve read bad reviews. How do I know you won&#39;t back out?&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Fear of wasted time and a sale that falls apart after they have made plans.

**Soft:**

> **You:** That worry makes sense. Can I ask what you read? I'd rather talk about it openly.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** I completely understand, (SELLER NAME). Unfortunately, we can't make everyone happy. That's just the world we live in. What I can tell you is that we will walk hand in hand with you all the way to the closing table. As long as everything you've told us about the home is true, we're confident we can close on this property.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** We buy homes every week, and we close them. As long as the house is what you described, we're closing. That's the whole point of what we do.
> 

**If they still think it's a scam:** go to TR2, Straight.

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** What would you need to see to feel comfortable?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TR4. &quot;How can you make an offer without seeing the house?&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They worry the number is a trick, or that it will drop later.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Fair point. We start with your description and nearby sales. Then we confirm with a walkthrough during the inspection period.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Well, we buy so many properties a month in this area that we know the neighborhood very well. Based on the condition you described to me, our formulas, and the price per square foot in this area, we know what price range we have to be at.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Your description plus recent sales gives a solid number. The walkthrough just checks the facts. Be as accurate as you can, and the number holds.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** So the number is accurate, is there anything about the house we haven't talked about yet, good or bad?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TR5. &quot;Where are you calling from? You&#39;re not local.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Your accent or the caller ID made them curious. They want to know who they are dealing with. Answer with the company and the market, calmly and without hesitation.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Good question. I'm with (COMPANY NAME). We buy homes right here in (MARKET), and our closings happen with a local title company.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** I'm calling from (COMPANY NAME), (CLIENT OFFICE LOCATION). We buy houses all over (MARKET). My job is to learn about your property, and (ACQUISITIONS MANAGER NAME) handles the offer.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** (COMPANY NAME), here in (MARKET). We buy in your area every month. Now, about your house...
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Is it okay if I ask a couple of questions about the house?
> 

<div class="callout callout-tip">

Use your client's approved wording for this answer, word for word. Your answer is always the client's company and the client's market or office. You never mention your own country or where you personally sit.

</div>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TR6. &quot;Are you a real person? Is this call recorded?&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They are tired of robot calls, or they want to know their rights.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Yes, I'm a real person. My name is (YOUR NAME). And yes, calls may be recorded for quality and training.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Real person, yes. I'm (YOUR NAME) with (COMPANY NAME). The call may be recorded so we can keep our service quality high.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Real person, and yes, it may be recorded for quality. Is that okay with you?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Do you have two minutes to talk about (PROPERTY ADDRESS)?
> 

If the seller does not agree to recording, follow your client's policy (usually: offer to end the call or continue without recording if your system allows). Questions like "What's your company's legal name? Send me your do-not-call policy" go to your team lead the same day.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TR7. &quot;Are you an agent? Are you going to list my house?&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They are confused about your business model. Be completely clear. Confusion now becomes a cancelled contract later.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Good question. We're not listing your house. We're a buyer. I'll explain exactly how it works so there are no surprises.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** We're investors, not your agent. We sign an agreement to buy your house. Sometimes we close ourselves, and sometimes we assign our contract to another investor buyer. If that's possible, it's written in the agreement, and it will be explained before you sign.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** No, we're a buyer, not an agent. Our agreement may allow us to assign it to another investor. You'll see that in writing before you sign anything.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Does that answer it, or is there anything else about the process you'd like to know?
> 

<div class="callout callout-tip">

Describe the business model exactly as your client instructs. Several states now require written disclosure that a contract may be assigned or resold. Follow your client's instructions and title company guidance.

</div>

</details>

### Group 3: Timing and motivation

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TI1. &quot;I&#39;m in no rush. I need time to think about it.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** One of three things: a concern they have not said yet, low motivation, or feeling overwhelmed. Find out which.

**Soft:**

> **You:** I completely understand. This is a big decision. What is it that you need to think about that I can answer for you right now over the phone? What would be your deciding factor for selling?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** That makes sense. Usually "think about it" means one thing is still unclear: the price, the timing, or trust. Which one is it for you?
> 

**Straight (when they say "just call me next week"):**

> **You:** Sure. Let me ask you this: what would be the deciding factor between today and next week in selling the property? What would change?
> 

**The takeaway (if they still push it to a later date):**

> **You:** Well, it sounds like you may not really be interested in selling the property. If that's the case, I completely understand. But if you are still interested in selling, I'd love to help you with that.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** If we can answer that question, would you be ready to look at the next step? If not, when should I check back?
> 

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** The takeaway tells you who is real. A motivated seller leans back in and tells you what is holding them up. A seller who agrees with the takeaway was mostly fishing for a number. Code them for long-term follow-up and move on.

</div>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TI2. &quot;Call me back in six months.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Sometimes a polite brush-off. Sometimes a real event is coming (a lease ending, a retirement, a move).

**Soft:**

> **You:** Happy to. Is something happening around then, like a move or a lease ending?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Will do. So I call at the right time, what changes in six months?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Sure. If something changes before then, would you like me to check in sooner, say in (FOLLOW-UP INTERVAL)?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** What's the best number and time of day to reach you then?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TI3. &quot;I have nowhere to go if I sell.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** A real fear. The blocker is logistics, not price.

**Soft:**

> **You:** I can understand. The good thing about our company is that we move at your pace. We often work with homeowners in your exact situation, who need to find a new place before they move.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** As long as we can agree on a price, we can work with you on time and give you a longer closing, up to 90 days. And if you find a home sooner, we can close sooner to fit your needs.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Timing is flexible, up to 90 days. If you choose the date, does the rest of the idea work for you?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Realistically, when do you think you could be ready to move?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TI4. &quot;I think I&#39;ll just rent it out instead.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They are weighing options. Often they are a tired landlord who has not done the math.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Oh, I can completely understand. Just out of curiosity, what made you change your mind from selling to renting it out?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** That makes sense. So what would be your deciding factor between selling it and keeping it as a rental?
> 

**Straight (if they still lean toward renting, break down the rent):**

> **You:** Let's look at it together. If you collect (MONTHLY RENT) a month, that's (YEARLY RENT) a year. To collect the (OFFER AMOUNT) we've offered, and can close on in 30 days, it would take you about (YEARS TO MATCH) years.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Would it help to compare both options side by side?
> 

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Do the math out loud, slowly. Example: 800 a month in rent is 9,600 a year. At that pace it takes nearly 15 years to collect the 150K we've offered to close in 30 days. If after that they still say no, add a note to follow up later, in case things change.

</div>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TI5. &quot;I&#39;ll fix it up myself and sell it for more.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They want the profit. They may underestimate the time, cost and stress.

**Soft:**

> **You:** That can work well if you have the time. How much work do you think it needs?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Yes, you can actually do that. But it might take you a while to get it done. We're talking repairs, and then closing costs, fees and commissions on the back end. If we buy the property as-is, we take care of the closing costs, fees, commissions and everything else.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Are you really going to take the time to do all the repairs, on top of the full-time job you already have? And with the way the market is shifting, who knows if you'll get what you're looking for by the time you're finished.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** If the repairs took twice as long as planned, would that be a problem for you?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TI6. &quot;I live there, and I don&#39;t want to move.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Usually no motivation right now. Respect it.

**Soft:**

> **You:** That's completely fine. Home is home. Thank you for being honest with me.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** I understand. Is there any situation where selling might make sense later, like downsizing?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Got it. I won't push. Can I check back in (FOLLOW-UP INTERVAL) in case things change?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Do you own any other properties you might consider selling?
>

</details>

### Group 4: Agents and listing

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>AG1. &quot;I want to list with an agent.&quot; or &quot;I&#39;ll try listing again.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They want the top price, and they are comparing. Show them the full cost of the listing path.

**Soft:**

> **You:** I think that makes sense. That is an option you can take. But it might sit 30, 60, 90 days on the market, and you've got closing costs, commissions, repairs, and the buyer's financing might fall through.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Look, I'm not here to lowball you. But if a clean, fast, hassle-free offer makes your life easier, I'm happy to explore that with you. We buy as-is, no agent commissions, no repairs, and we close on your timeline, sometimes in as little as 7 to 10 days.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** By working with our company, we have to be at a price that makes sense for both of us. We'd love to help you if you're open to selling, but we have to be in this range: (OFFER RANGE).
> 

**Move it forward (soft close):**

> **You:** Would it be crazy to take ten minutes and see what that kind of offer would look like? No pressure at all, just numbers.
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>AG2. &quot;It&#39;s already listed with an agent.&quot; or &quot;I already have a realtor.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** There is a listing agreement, but a listed seller is often a tired seller. Many value time, ease and certainty more than squeezing out every dollar, even if they will not say it.

**Always ask early on every call:** "Is the property currently listed with an agent?"

**Soft:**

> **You:** Perfect. That usually means you're keeping an eye on things. Just so you know, we buy directly: no fees, no showings, no repairs. Sometimes it's worth comparing options. Would you be open to a quick offer, just to see where it stands?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** No problem. We can loop your agent in, or wait until the listing expires, whichever works better for you. Can I ask what's prompting the move?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** I'm sure your agent will do their best. I've just seen a lot of deals fall apart over small inspection items, and it can drag on for weeks. Is this something you're hoping to get done quickly, or are you holding out for top dollar no matter how long it takes?
> 

**Motivation questions for listed sellers:**

- "What's prompting the move?"
- "Is this something you're hoping to get done quickly, or are you looking for top dollar no matter the wait?"
- "If you could skip the whole showing and inspection process, would that make things easier?"

**The drawbacks to bring up casually:** agent fees (often 5 to 6 percent), closing costs, staging and showings, financing delays, inspection repairs and renegotiations, and buyers falling through.

**If they want to avoid the commission:**

> **You:** If you'd rather not pay a commission, take a look at your listing agreement and ask your agent, in writing, for an early cancellation. Once the listing is cancelled, we can move forward directly.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Would it be crazy to take ten minutes and see what a cash offer would look like next to your listing?
> 

Code it LISTED. Note the listing end date and whether the seller is requesting an early cancellation.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>AG3. &quot;My relative (or friend) is an agent.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They have someone they trust. Welcome that person into the process.

**Soft:**

> **You:** That's great. Having someone you trust helps a lot.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Great. Would you like them to look at our offer with you? We're happy to answer their questions.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Perfect. They can compare our cash offer with a listing. Two options are better than one.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Would you like us to include them on the next call?
>

</details>

### Group 5: Paperwork and process

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PA1. &quot;Just send me an offer in writing.&quot; or &quot;Email it to me.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They want to avoid pressure, or they are comparing, or it is a brush-off. A written number without a conversation is usually ignored or misread.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Of course, I'll send it. A number on paper can be confusing without context, so can we take five minutes to go over it together when it arrives?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Happy to send it. So it's accurate, I need a few details first. And when it arrives, let's look at it together so you can ask questions.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** I will. Paper can't answer your questions, though. Let's book ten minutes to go over it: today at (TIME OPTION 1), or tomorrow at (TIME OPTION 2)?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** What's the best email, and which time works to walk through it?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PA2. &quot;I want my attorney to look at it before I sign.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Caution, or one clause they are still unsure about. Find out which, then make the review fast.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Okay, I can understand that. But is there anything you aren't certain about, since we went through it together?
> 

**Steady (if they still want the review):**

> **You:** No problem. I can make it even easier for you and send it to them directly. What's their email?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** I'll copy them on the agreement now. When can they review it, so we have an answer on whether we're moving forward?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** What's your attorney's email, and what time tomorrow can we have their answer?
> 

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Always get the attorney's contact details and copy them on the agreement in the e-signature tool. Then lock a specific time for the review and the answer. A review with no time on it turns into a lost deal.

</div>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PA3. &quot;Why is there an inspection period in the contract?&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They fear you will use it to lower the price later.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Great question. We need to come out, take pictures and do a quick walkthrough, just to make sure the condition is what you said it was.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** And don't worry, we aren't coming out there to look for things to lower the price. I'm sure that's a concern of yours. If it matches what you told us, the price stays the same.
> 

**Straight (the takeaway, if they push back):**

> **You:** You don't have to worry, (SELLER NAME). We buy 20 to 30 homes each month, and we know exactly what we're getting into. We have every intention of buying this home. The inspection period is there to protect us, so we can come out and make sure it's everything we discussed.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Is there anything we should know now, so there are no surprises at the walkthrough?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PA4. &quot;Will the price change after you see the house?&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Fear of a "re-trade," a planned price cut after signing. Many sellers have heard of this.

**Soft:**

> **You:** I understand that worry. The plan is no. The price is based on what you've told us.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** If the house matches what we discussed, the price doesn't change. Only a major new issue, like a foundation problem nobody knew about, would lead to a conversation. And you'd always have a choice.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Another great question. No, as long as it's what you said it was. We always figure a little worse than described, so the number already has room in it.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** So let's double check: roof, foundation, plumbing, electrical. Any issues there?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PA5. &quot;Why is the earnest money deposit so low?&quot; or &quot;Do you put down a deposit?&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They are testing your commitment. Your answer depends on your client's model. Check the fact sheet: does your client post a deposit, or not?

**If your client does not use deposits:**

> **You:** We haven't posted deposits since we started the business. Our goal is to close the deal, not to have you take our money.
> 

**If your client uses a small standard deposit:**

**Soft:**

> **You:** Good question. The deposit is (EMD AMOUNT), and it's held by (TITLE COMPANY), not by us.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** This is our standard amount, and we put it in on every home. We buy 20 to 30 homes each month, and keeping the deposit standard lets us save our capital for the renovations.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** It's our standard. The real proof of commitment is a closing date in writing, and a company that closes homes every month.
> 

**If they ask for more:**

> **You:** Let me check with my underwriters and my manager on that. I'll come back to you with an answer.
> 

Never promise a higher amount until your manager approves it.

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Is the deposit the main concern, or is there something else?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PA6. &quot;I want to use my own title company.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Trust or habit. Maybe they used that company before.

**Soft:**

> **You:** That's understandable. Can I ask why? Is it someone you've worked with before?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** I understand that. Since we're covering all the closing costs, the title fees and the transfer taxes, we use our preferred title company, (TITLE COMPANY), that we already have a relationship with.
> 

**Straight (if they insist on their own):**

> **You:** I'd have to ask if we can use your title company. And I can't guarantee we would cover all the costs, since it's not one of the preferred companies we work with.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** What's the name of your title company, so I can check?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PA7. &quot;I&#39;m not comfortable with some things in the contract.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Something is unclear or scary. Usually it is one clause.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Thank you for telling me. Let's go through them together. Which parts?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** That's exactly what I'm here for. Tell me the section, and I'll explain what it says in plain words. If I can't, I'll find someone who can.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Let's clear it up now. Which line is bothering you?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Is it the price, the dates, or another section?
> 

Explain what a clause says. Never tell the seller what it means for them legally. For that, suggest their attorney.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PA8. &quot;Why are there so many pages? This feels complicated.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Overwhelm. They need structure, not speed.

**Soft:**

> **You:** I know, it's a lot. We'll go one page at a time. You can stop me whenever you like.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Most of it is standard. There are about five parts that really matter: price, closing date, deposit, inspection period, and who pays which costs. Let's start there.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Five key parts. I'll show you each one, then you can read the rest at your pace.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Shall we start with the price and the closing date?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>PA9. &quot;Why would you list my house on the MLS? I thought you were buying it.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They saw a marketing or listing clause in the agreement, or someone told them about it, and they worry the "cash sale" is really a listing.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Great question. We are buying the home, (SELLER NAME). Nothing about your price or closing date changes.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** We are buying the home. We use the MLS to pre-market the home for our end buyer once the renovation is done. The MLS is the best way to reach agents who may have a client looking for that exact kind of home in that area.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** We're the buyer. The MLS is just our marketing tool for after the renovation. Your price, your date, and your terms stay exactly as written.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Does that clear it up? Then let's keep going with the agreement.
>

</details>

### Group 7: Investor competition

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>CO1. &quot;Another investor offered me more.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** It could be true, or it could be negotiation. Do not panic and do not attack the competitor.

**Soft:**

> **You:** That's good for you. You should take the best option for your family.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Congratulations. Can I ask what terms came with it? Closing costs, inspection period, closing date and deposit all change the real number.

**Straight:**

> **You:** If it's a solid offer, consider it seriously. Just check: is it in writing, and are there conditions like a long inspection period? Sometimes the highest number isn't the one that closes.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Would you like us to compare our offer with theirs, side by side?
>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>TE4. &quot;The tenant pays well. Why would I sell?&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** The rental works for them financially. The motivation may be elsewhere (age, distance, repairs coming).

**Soft:**

> **You:** That's a great position to be in. Good tenants are valuable.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Makes sense. Is anything else making you think about selling, like distance, upcoming repairs, or wanting a break from managing it?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Fair. If the rental is working, there may be no reason to sell now. Is there a big repair coming, like a roof?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** If one thing about owning it could change, what would it be?
>

</details>

### Group 10: Brush-offs and early exits

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>BR1. &quot;Not interested.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Sometimes a reflex. Sometimes a real no. One respectful question tells you which.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Totally understand. Most of the people we speak with aren't actively looking to sell either. We just reach out in case there's a future need. Out of curiosity, have you ever thought about what number would make you at least consider it?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Understood. Is that a "not ever," or a "not right now"?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Got it. One last question: is that for this property only, or do you own others too?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Would it be alright to check back in (FOLLOW-UP INTERVAL), or would you prefer I don't?
> 

If they say they'd prefer you don't, that is a stop request. Code DNC.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>BR2. &quot;It&#39;s not for sale.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Right now it isn't. That can change.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Totally understood. Thank you for telling me.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** That's fine. Has selling ever crossed your mind, even for later?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Understood. If a fair cash offer arrived one day, would you at least look at it?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** We like to stay in touch in case life ever takes a turn. Is it okay if I leave my number and check back in (FOLLOW-UP INTERVAL)?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>BR3. &quot;I already sold it.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** The lead is closed. Stay friendly and update the CRM.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Congratulations on the sale! Thanks for letting me know.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Great news. I'll update our records so you don't get more calls about it.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Got it, I'll update that now.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Do you own any other property you might sell one day?
> 

Code it SOLD.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>BR4. &quot;I&#39;m busy right now.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** Bad timing, or an easy way out. Offer a real callback.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Sorry for catching you at a bad time. When would be better?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** No problem. It's a two-minute call. Is later today or tomorrow better?
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Understood. Ten seconds: are you open to an offer on (PROPERTY ADDRESS) at all? If yes, I'll call back when it suits you.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** What time works best, and is this the best number?
> 

Set the callback in the seller's local time, inside legal calling hours.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>BR5. &quot;Wrong number.&quot; or &quot;I&#39;m not the owner.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** The data is wrong, or the person is protecting someone.

**Soft:**

> **You:** I'm so sorry for the bother. Thank you for telling me.
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** My apologies. I'll remove this number from this property right away.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Sorry about that. I'll update our records now.
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** Have a good day.
> 

Code WRONG. Do not ask for the owner's real number unless the person offers it. Do not keep calling to "check."

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>BR6. &quot;Just text me.&quot; or &quot;Send me something.&quot;</strong></summary>

**What it really means:** They prefer text, or they want off the phone. Texting needs their clear permission.

**Soft:**

> **You:** Of course. Is it okay if I text this number about your property?
> 

**Steady:**

> **You:** Happy to. Just to confirm, you're okay with me texting you at this number about (PROPERTY ADDRESS)? You can reply STOP any time.
> 

**Straight:**

> **You:** Sure. I'll text you one short message. Before I go, when's a good time to talk for two minutes?
> 

**Move it forward:**

> **You:** What's the one thing you'd like to see in that message?
> 

Log the consent in the CRM with the date and time. Texts go only through your client's approved texting platform.

</details>

## When objections stack up

Sometimes a seller gives you three objections in one breath: "It's too low, my wife won't agree, and I don't trust investors." Do not answer all three.

> **You:** I hear three things: the price, your wife, and trust. Those are all fair. Which one matters most to you right now?
> 

Answer that one with STEP. Then come back to the others. The one they pick first is usually the real one.

## Tone and delivery

- **Slow down after an objection.** Your heart rate goes up. Your speaking speed should go down.
- **Pause before you Settle.** One full breath. It shows you are listening, not reacting.
- **Drop your tone at the end of facts.** A fact that ends with a rising voice sounds like a question, and sounds unsure.
- **Lift your tone at the end of your Test and Propose questions.** It sounds curious and safe.
- **Say numbers clearly, then repeat them.** "One hundred thirty thousand. 130K." Many callers lose deals over "thirteen" versus "thirty." If needed, say "one-three-zero thousand."
- **Use the seller's name, but not in every line.** Two or three times per call is warm. Every line is salesy.

### Five tone styles

How you say it matters as much as what you say. Practice these until you can switch between them on purpose.

| Style | When to use it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rising tone** | To sound curious and non-threatening | "Would it be crazy to ask...?" (voice goes up at the end) |
| **Falling tone** | To make a confident, authoritative statement | "We specialize in helping homeowners like you." (voice drops slightly) |
| **Low, quiet tone** | To sound sincere and private. Lower your voice a little. It builds trust and closeness. | "Between you and me, most sellers in your spot feel the same way." |
| **Curious tone** | To sound genuinely interested and open | "What's your timeline looking like for something like this?" |
| **Assumptive tone** | To lower resistance by speaking as if the next step is already happening | "Let's find a time that works. Are mornings or afternoons better for you?" |

### Six decision triggers

These are the mental and emotional hooks that build rapport and move a seller toward a decision.

| Trigger | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Emotional trigger** | Touches the seller's feelings: pain, relief, fear, desire | "We work with a lot of people who are just tired of dealing with repairs and tenants. I'm not sure if that's your situation, though?" |
| **Logic trigger** | Gives a logical reason to keep listening | "I don't know if this is a fit, but if we handled everything, inspections, closing, even the clean-out, would that be worth a quick chat?" |
| **Pattern break** | Breaks the seller's automatic "no" | "Hey, I'm sure I caught you at a bad time. Real quick, though..." |
| **Framing** | Sets you up as helpful, not salesy | "This may or may not be for you. I just wanted to reach out in case the timing made sense." |
| **Echo and name** | Repeat their last one to three words with a rising tone, then name the feeling | "Too low?" ... "Sounds like that's been pretty frustrating." |
| **Small yeses** | Collects small agreements on the way to the big one | "Does that sound fair?" "Would it be okay if I asked...?" |

## Practice drills

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>Drill 1: Three levels, one objection (10 minutes, in pairs)</strong></summary>

Your partner reads one objection. You answer it three times: Soft, Steady, Straight. Your partner scores each answer: Was it confident? Was it short? Did it end with a question? Switch roles after five objections.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>Drill 2: Find the real concern (10 minutes, in pairs)</strong></summary>

Your partner picks a secret real concern (for example: "I owe 140K on the mortgage") and gives you a surface objection ("Your offer is too low"). You may only use Settle and Test. You win when you uncover the secret concern in three questions or fewer.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>Drill 3: The stop-request reflex (5 minutes, solo)</strong></summary>

Write ten different ways a person might ask you to stop calling (for example "I'm good, lose my number," "don't bother me again," "please remove me"). Read each one aloud and answer with the exact stop line. The goal is zero hesitation and zero rebuttal.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>Drill 4: The numbers out loud (5 minutes, solo)</strong></summary>

Record yourself saying: "Homes like yours sell for about 250K after repairs. Yours needs about 45K of work. That's why our offer is 130K." Listen back. Are the numbers clear? Is your tone going down at the end of each fact? Repeat until both are yes.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>Drill 5: Approval and takeaway (10 minutes, in pairs)</strong></summary>

Your partner plays a seller who pushes back on price, then says "I need to think about it." You must use, in order: the good-news approval, the deciding-factor question, and the takeaway. Your partner scores whether each one sounded calm and natural. Then run the approval hold: "Give me one second while I get that approved," a short hold, and "Good news. We're good to go."

</details>

## After the call

Log every objection in the CRM notes the same day, in this format:

- **Objection code:** for example PR4
- **Their exact words:** short quote
- **Level used:** Soft, Steady or Straight
- **Real concern found:** for example "owes 140K, needs payoff covered"
- **Outcome:** resolved, partly resolved, or not resolved
- **Next step:** what happens, who owns it, and the date and time (seller's local time)
- **Disposition code:** HOT, FU-WARM, CB, NI, DNC, LISTED, SOLD, WRONG

Hand off to Acquisitions the same day when the objection is about price after an offer, the contract, the deposit or title.
