<div class="callout callout-intro">

Purpose: give the client's coordinator a clear, honest status on one or more orders, and ask for what you need (an extension, access, or instructions).

When to use: Side A campaigns, when an order is at risk, blocked (occupied, no access, wrong scope), or when the coordinator asks for status. On Side B, use the same structure when your campaign allows you to update the servicer's contact.

Goal of the call: the coordinator knows the true status and gives a decision or approval you can record.

Version: v1.0

</div>

## Before you dial

- [ ]  Order open: number, address, tasks, due date in the client's time zone
- [ ]  Exact status from the tracker and the latest crew update
- [ ]  Proof ready to send: photos of occupancy signs, locked gate, hazard, or damage
- [ ]  What you are asking for, in one sentence (for example "a 2-day extension to (NEW DATE)")
- [ ]  Reason for the request, in one sentence
- [ ]  The client's preferred extension or update method (portal message, email, call)
- [ ]  Your campaign's recording disclosure line, if calls are recorded

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never say work is "done" or "on the way" unless the tracker and the crew confirm it. Never blame the crew, the weather or the client to cover a desk mistake. Coordinators remember who told them the truth.

</div>

## The flow

### 1. Opening

> **You:** Hi (COORDINATOR NAME), this is (YOUR NAME) calling for (VENDOR COMPANY NAME), vendor number (VENDOR ID). I'm calling about order (ORDER NUMBER) at (PROPERTY ADDRESS). Do you have a minute?
> 

*Why it works: vendor name, vendor ID and order number let the coordinator open the right screen before you finish your sentence.*

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they ask &quot;Where are you located?&quot;</summary>

> **You:** I'm with (VENDOR COMPANY NAME). We handle work orders all over (MARKET), and I take care of our order updates with your team.
> 

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** How you answer location questions depends on the client. Use the client's approved wording. Never mention your own country.

</div>

</details>

### 2. Status in one line

Choose the status that matches the truth.

> **On track:** "The order is on schedule. The crew completed it today, and the update will be submitted by (TIME AND TIME ZONE)."
> 

> **At risk:** "The order is at risk of missing the (DUE DATE) due date. I'm calling before the due date to explain and ask for an extension."
> 

> **Blocked:** "The crew could not complete the order because (REASON). We need your instructions."
> 

*Why it works: the coordinator hears the headline first, then the detail. Busy people decide faster that way.*

### 3. The reason and the proof

> **You:** Here's what happened: (SHORT FACTUAL REASON). I've sent you photos showing (WHAT THE PHOTOS SHOW) through (PORTAL MESSAGE OR EMAIL).
> 

<details class="toggle"><summary>Example: weather delay</summary>

> **You:** Heavy rain in (COUNTY NAME) yesterday and today made the grass too wet to cut safely. The crew is scheduled for tomorrow morning.
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>Example: property appears occupied</summary>

> **You:** The crew arrived at (TIME) and saw signs the house may be occupied: a car in the driveway, lights on, and fresh trash at the curb. They did not enter. Photos from the street are attached.
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>Example: no access</summary>

> **You:** The rear gate has a padlock that is not on the client's key code, so the crew could not reach the back yard. Front and side yards are done. Photos of the locked gate are attached.
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>Example: scope larger than ordered</summary>

> **You:** The order is for a re-cut, but the crew found about (QUANTITY) cubic yards of new debris in the back yard. They photographed and measured it. A bid is attached to the update.
>

</details>

### 4. The ask

> **You:** Can we have an extension to (NEW DATE)?
> 

or

> **You:** How would you like us to proceed: should the crew return, or should we close it as not completed?
> 

*Why it works: one clear question gets one clear answer. Never make the coordinator guess what you need.*

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say &quot;Yes, extension approved&quot;</summary>

> **You:** Thank you. Could you please note it in the portal as well, so our records match? ... I'll confirm completion by (NEW DATE).
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say &quot;No extension. It must be done today.&quot;</summary>

> **You:** Understood. I'll check with the crew right now and call you back within (MINUTES) minutes to confirm whether today is possible.
> 

Call the crew and Team Lead immediately. Call back on time, even if the answer is no.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they say &quot;Send it in writing&quot;</summary>

> **You:** Of course. I'll send it through (PORTAL MESSAGE OR EMAIL) within 15 minutes, with the photos.
> 

Use the "Extension request" template from the Email pack.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If they are upset about repeated delays</summary>

> **You:** You're right to be concerned, and I'm sorry. Here's what we are changing so it doesn't happen again: (SPECIFIC FIX). For this order, the new completion time is (TIME).
> 

Tell your Team Lead the same day.

</details>

### 5. Confirm and close

> **You:** So to confirm: (DECISION), and I'll (YOUR NEXT ACTION) by (TIME). Thank you, (COORDINATOR NAME).
> 

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: call before the due date, not after. An extension asked for the day before, with photos and a reason, is usually a simple "yes". The same request the day after is a late mark on the scorecard.

</div>

## Objections

| They say | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "Why didn't you tell us earlier?" | "That's fair. We should have flagged it sooner. From now on, we'll alert you as soon as an order is at risk." |
| "Your vendor is always late in this county." | "Thank you for telling me. I'll share this with our manager today and come back with a plan for that county." |
| "Just do the work, no extension." | "Understood. Let me confirm with the crew right now and call you back in (MINUTES) minutes." |
| "I don't have time for calls. Use the portal." | "Of course. I'll send all updates through the portal from now on. Thank you." |

## After the call

> **(DATE) (TIME YOUR TZ) / (TIME CLIENT TZ) · Status update to client · (YOUR NAME)**
Order: (ORDER NUMBER) · Coordinator: (COORDINATOR NAME)

Next step: (ACTION) by (TIME)
> 

Next step: put the decision in the tracker and the order notes, tell the crew, and add any new due date to the dashboard.

## Check yourself

<details class="toggle"><summary>1. What are the first three pieces of information you give the coordinator?</summary>

Your name and the vendor you represent, the vendor ID, and the order number with address.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>2. When is the best time to ask for an extension?</summary>

As soon as you know the order is at risk, always before the due date, with a reason and proof.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>3. The coordinator approves an extension on the phone. What else do you ask?</summary>

That they also note it in the portal (or confirm in writing), so the records match.

</details>
