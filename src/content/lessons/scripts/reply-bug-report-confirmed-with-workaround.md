<div class="callout callout-intro">

**Purpose:** confirm a product or website problem honestly, give a workaround, and commit to an update.

**Use it when:** you (or Tier 2) have reproduced the problem, or it matches a known issue the client has confirmed.

**Goal:** the customer can keep working, knows we take it seriously, and knows when they will hear more.

**Version:** v1.0

</div>

## Before you send

- [ ]  Problem reproduced, or matched to a known issue in the team updates
- [ ]  Exact steps, device, browser type, app version and time noted
- [ ]  Workaround found and tested (from the knowledge base or Tier 2)
- [ ]  Bug reported to the client's product contact using the agreed path, with a reference
- [ ]  If many customers are affected, check for a master ticket and link to it

## The flow

### 1. Thank and confirm

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME),
Thanks for the clear steps. I was able to reproduce the problem where (SHORT DESCRIPTION), so this is on our side, not something you did.
> 

*Why it works: confirming it is not their fault removes frustration and self-doubt.*

### 2. Workaround

> **You:** While our team fixes it, this gets you the same result:
1. (STEP ONE)
2. (STEP TWO)
3. (STEP THREE)
> 

### 3. What happens next, with a date

> **You:** I've passed it to our product team under reference (BUG REFERENCE). I'll update you by (DATE) even if the fix isn't ready yet.
Best,
(YOUR NAME)
> 

*Why it works: an update date, kept even with no news, is what customers remember.*

<details class="toggle"><summary>If there is no workaround</summary>

> **You:** I'm sorry, there isn't a way around this yet. Our team has it as a priority under reference (BUG REFERENCE). I'll update you by (DATE). (If the matrix allows: I've also (GOODWILL OPTION, for example extended your subscription by one week).)
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the bug cost them money (failed orders, wrong charges)</summary>

Fix the money first, then the bug. Check the order and payment records and follow the Billing Error reply. Escalate to Tier 2 if needed.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>When the fix goes live</summary>

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME), good news: the problem with (SHORT DESCRIPTION) is now fixed. You can go back to (NORMAL WAY). Thanks again for reporting it so clearly. It helped us fix it for everyone.
>

</details>

## Objections and tricky replies

| Customer says | You reply |
| --- | --- |
| "When exactly will it be fixed?" | "I don't have a date from the team yet, and I don't want to guess. I'll update you by (DATE) with whatever I know." |
| "This is unacceptable, I pay for this." | Use STEER. "You're right to expect it to work. Here's what I've done (WORKAROUND, REPORT) and when you'll hear next." |
| "Can I get a refund for the time it didn't work?" | Check the matrix (pro-rated credit, goodwill). Offer what is allowed or escalate. |

## After you send

**Internal note:**

> Issue: bug, (SHORT DESCRIPTION). Checked: reproduced on (DEVICE, BROWSER TYPE), steps in note. Done: reported to product, ref (BUG REFERENCE); workaround sent. Promised: update by (DATE AND ZONE). Next: chase product team on (DATE).
> 

**Tags:** bug, product area. **Status:** On-hold (waiting on product team). Link to the master ticket if one exists.

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never promise a fix date the product team has not given you, and never blame "the developers" or "the system". It is "our team" and "on our side".

</div>
