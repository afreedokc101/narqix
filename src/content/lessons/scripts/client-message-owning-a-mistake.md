<div class="callout callout-intro">

**Purpose:** tell the client about an error you made, quickly and honestly, with the fix already in motion.

**Use it when:** you discover your own mistake (wrong data, missed task, wrong booking, wrong file shared).

**Goal:** the client hears it from you first, sees it is under control, and trusts you more, not less.

**Version:** v1.0

</div>

## Before you send

- [ ]  **Is it a security incident?** (Data sent to the wrong person, a phishing click, a lost device.) If yes, stop. Report to your Narqix lead within 15 minutes first. Narqix handles client notification for incidents. See the Security Standard.
- [ ]  Contain the damage if you safely can (cancel a wrong invite, recall a draft, pause a task)
- [ ]  Know the facts: what happened, when, what it affected
- [ ]  Have the fix ready, or the steps already started
- [ ]  Tell your Narqix lead for anything more than minor

## The flow

### 1. Say it plainly, first line

> **You:** Hi (CLIENT FIRST NAME), I made a mistake I need to tell you about.
> 

*Why it works: no build-up, no hiding. Clients respect directness.*

### 2. What happened, in facts

> **You:** In yesterday's invoice batch, I entered the (SUPPLIER NAME) bill as 4,650 USD instead of 4,560 USD. I found it this morning during my control total check.
> 

### 3. What you already did

> **You:** I have corrected the entry, and the batch total now matches the invoices. The payment has not been sent yet, so nothing went out wrong.
> 

### 4. How you will prevent it

> **You:** From today, I am running the double entry check on every invoice batch before I submit it, not after.
> 

### 5. Close without excuses

> **You:** I am sorry for the error. Let me know if you would like me to walk you through the fix.
(YOUR NAME)
> 

*Why it works: facts, fix, prevention, one apology. No excuses, no blaming tools or people.*

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the mistake affected someone outside (a customer, vendor, or meeting guest)</summary>

Add what you propose to send them and ask the client first:

> **You:** (CONTACT NAME) received the invite with the wrong time. I suggest sending this short correction: "Apologies, the correct time is 2:00 PM CT, not 2:00 PM ET. The invite is updated." OK to send?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the client is upset</summary>

Listen, do not defend. Repeat the fix and the prevention step. Offer a short call. Tell your Narqix lead so they can support you and the client.

> **You:** You are right to be frustrated. The fix is done, and the new check is in place. Would a 10-minute call help to go through it?
>

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If you are not sure yet how big the problem is</summary>

> **You:** I have found an error in the CRM import from this morning and paused all work on it. I am checking how many records are affected and will update you by 11:00 AM ET with the full picture and the fix.
> 

Tell early, update on time. Do not wait until you know everything.

</details>

## Phrases to avoid

| Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- |
| "The system did it" | Blaming tools sounds like hiding |
| "Small issue, nothing to worry about" | Let the client judge the size |
| "Sorry sorry, very sorry" | Over-apologizing shifts attention to you |
| Waiting until the weekly report | Late news feels like hidden news |

## After you send

- Log the mistake and the fix in your end of day report.
- Add the prevention step to the task card in the Client Workflow Record.
- If it was caused by an unclear process, propose an SOP change to your lead.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: at Narqix, a mistake you report early is a coaching moment. A mistake you hide is a conduct issue. Clients almost never fire a specialist for one honest error. They do leave when they find errors nobody told them about.
