<div class="callout callout-intro">

**Purpose:** keep helping while setting a calm, firm limit on abusive language.

**Use it when:** a customer moves beyond anger into personal insults, slurs, harassment or repeated abusive language toward you or the team.

**Goal:** either the conversation becomes respectful and continues, or it ends safely and is handed to Tier 2.

**Version:** v1.0

</div>

## Before you send

- [ ]  **Anger or abuse?** Anger at the situation ("This is a joke!") is not abuse. Insults at people, slurs and threats are.
- [ ]  **Any threat of violence or self-harm?** Stop. That is P1. Follow the Escalation Ladder and the client's emergency process now.
- [ ]  Client's policy on abusive customers checked (warnings, ending chats, blocking)
- [ ]  Take two minutes away if the message upset you
- [ ]  Tier 2 or your lead reviews the reply before sending

## The flow

### Step 1: Help, plus one boundary

> **You:** Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME),
I want to get (ISSUE) sorted for you, and here's where it stands: (FACTS AND NEXT STEP).
I'm happy to keep helping, and I'll need us to keep the conversation respectful to do that.
> 

*Why it works: help comes first, so the boundary is not a punishment. One calm sentence, no lecture.*

### Step 2: If abuse continues, a clear final notice

> **You:** I've noted your concerns about (ISSUE), and (NEXT STEP) is still happening. Because of the language in your messages, I'm going to pass this conversation to our senior team, who'll contact you by (TIME AND ZONE).
> 

*Why it works: the customer still gets the fix. Only the conversation with you ends.*

### Step 3: Hand over and end per policy

- Assign to Tier 2 or your lead with a note.
- On chat: end the chat after the final notice, per the client's policy.
- On email: do not reply to further abusive messages yourself.

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the customer apologizes or calms down</summary>

> **You:** Thank you, I appreciate that. Let's get this fixed. (CONTINUE WITH THE SOLUTION.)
> 

Move on fully. Do not mention it again.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>If the message contains a threat</summary>

Do not reply with the boundary script. Do not argue or joke. Save the message, follow the Escalation Ladder immediately (P1), and let your Narqix lead and the client decide the response. If the threat is about self-harm, follow the client's emergency guidance and escalate at once.

</details>

## Objections and tricky replies

| Customer says | You reply |
| --- | --- |
| "I'll speak however I want, I'm the customer." | Do not debate. Use Step 2. |
| "Put me through to someone who isn't useless." | "I'll ask our senior team to take over. They'll contact you by (TIME AND ZONE)." |
| Insults about your accent, name or country | Do not respond to the insult itself. Use Step 1, then Step 2 if it continues. Report it to your lead. |

## After you send

**Internal note:**

> Issue: (ORIGINAL ISSUE). Customer used abusive language (quote one example). Boundary set (TIME). (Continued / stopped.) Done: (FIX STATUS). Handed to (TIER 2 OR LEAD) (TIME). Promised: senior contact by (TIME AND ZONE).
> 

**Tags:** abusive, original issue tag. **Priority:** High. **Status:** Open (assigned to Tier 2).

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never insult back, use sarcasm, threaten the customer, or quietly ignore them. Never stop the actual fix because the customer was rude. We set boundaries on behavior, not on service.

</div>

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: abuse is never your fault, and you do not have to absorb it. After a conversation like this, tell your lead, take a short break if needed, and remember that the customer was attacking a situation, not you.

</div>
