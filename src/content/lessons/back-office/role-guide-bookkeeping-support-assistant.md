Track: Back Office & Admin  ·  Role: Bookkeeping Support Assistant  ·  Time to complete: 20 min

## What this role is (and is not)

You prepare and organize financial data so the client's qualified bookkeeper, accountant or the owner can review it and make decisions. You make their work faster and cleaner.

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: you never give tax, accounting, legal or financial advice. You do not decide how something is treated for tax, you do not file returns, you do not approve or send payments, and you do not move money. If anyone asks "Can I deduct this?" or "How should we book this?", the answer is: "That is a question for your accountant. I can prepare the information they will need."

</div>

| You do | You do not |
| --- | --- |
| Collect and match receipts to transactions | Decide tax treatment of anything |
| Enter bills and invoices from source documents | Approve, schedule or release payments |
| Suggest categories using the client's written rules, marked for review | Finalize categories that the rules do not cover |
| Prepare reconciliation worksheets for review | Sign off a reconciliation |
| Send polite payment reminders using approved templates | Negotiate payment terms or write off debts |
| Keep a missing-documents list | Give opinions on the client's finances |
| Organize month-end folders | File anything with a tax authority |

## What you own

- **Receipt capture and matching:** every transaction has its receipt or is on the Missing list.
- **Bills entry:** supplier bills entered with date, supplier, amount, due date and reference, exactly as the source shows.
- **Invoice prep:** customer invoices drafted from approved rate sheets for the client to send or approve.
- **Category suggestions:** applied only from the client's written category rules, everything else marked "For review".
- **Accounts receivable reminders:** friendly reminders on the client's schedule using approved wording.
- **Month-end pack:** a folder and checklist ready for the bookkeeper by the agreed date.

## Your rhythm

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Daily | Capture new receipts and bills from the inbox and shared drive. Enter bills. Match yesterday's transactions. |
| Twice a week | Chase missing receipts with one batched message to the client or staff. |
| Weekly | Accounts receivable aging check; reminders sent per the approved schedule; summary to client. |
| Month-end | Month-end checklist completed; pack handed to the bookkeeper; open questions listed. |

## How you are measured

| KPI | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Amount accuracy on entered bills and invoices | 100% |
| Batches where your control total matches the source total | 100% |
| Transactions with a matched receipt or a "Missing" status | 100% |
| Month-end pack delivered by the agreed date | 100% |
| Items categorized outside the written rules without a "For review" flag | Zero |
| QA score on sampled work | 90% or more |

## The control total habit

Every batch of money data gets a **control total**. Add up the amounts on the source documents. Add up the amounts you entered. They must match to the cent before you submit.

**Example:** you enter 22 supplier bills. The source PDFs add up to 18,406.75. Your entries add up to 18,046.75. The difference is 360.00, which is a classic swap of digits (406 versus 046). You find bill 14, fix it, and both totals now read 18,406.75. Only then is the batch done.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: if a difference between two totals divides exactly by 9, look for two swapped digits first. 360 divided by 9 is 40. This old bookkeeper's trick finds most transposition errors in seconds.

</div>

## Payment fraud protection

Bookkeeping support staff are a favorite target for fraudsters because they sit close to money. Learn these red flags by heart:

1. A supplier "changes" their bank details by email.
2. An urgent request "from the owner" to pay someone new today.
3. A request to buy gift cards for any reason.
4. An invoice from a supplier the client has never used.
5. A sender address that is almost right (one letter different).

**Your response is always the same:** do not act, do not reply to the email, and verify through a channel you already know, such as the client's team channel or a phone number already in the client's records. Report it to your Narqix lead as a possible security incident (see the Security Standard).

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never update a supplier's bank details in any system based on an email alone. Bank detail changes need the client's confirmation through a second, known channel, every time.

</div>

## Tools (generic)

Accounting system (entry or limited access only), spreadsheet, shared drive with a restricted finance folder, receipt capture inbox, password manager, task tracker, team channel, your campaign's time tracker.

## Handoffs

- **You receive:** receipts, bills, bank and card transaction lists, category rules, rate sheets, and answers to your questions.
- **You pass on:** entered and matched data, a For review list, a Missing list and the month-end pack to the client's bookkeeper or accountant.

## Quality standards

1. **Enter exactly what the source says.** If it looks wrong, flag it, do not fix it.
2. **Double entry or control totals** on every money batch (see Data Entry Accuracy).
3. **Never type full card or bank numbers** into notes, chats or sheets. Use the last four digits only.
4. **Finance files live only in the restricted finance folder** shared with named people.
5. **Mark uncertainty.** "For review" is a respected status, not a failure.

## Escalation rules

- **P1 to your Narqix lead within 15 minutes:** suspected fraud, a payment you think was made to the wrong account, or financial data sent to the wrong person.
- **Same day to the client:** a duplicate bill, a supplier overcharge, an overdue amount past the client's alert threshold.
- **Weekly summary:** missing receipts, aging receivables, category questions.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Choosing a category because it "seems right" | Use only the written rules; flag the rest |
| Entering the bill date as the due date | Read every date label on the bill |
| Mixing currencies | Note the currency on every line; never convert without instructions |
| Chasing staff one receipt at a time | One batched list, twice a week |
| Answering "is this deductible?" | Refer to the accountant, offer to prepare information |
