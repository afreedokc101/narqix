For anyone who manages a client's email inbox. After reading you will be able to set up an inbox, sweep it with the Six Outcomes method, write a daily digest, and keep nothing waiting too long.

Track: Back Office & Admin  ·  Role: Executive and Admin Assistant, all back office roles  ·  Time to complete: 25 min

</div>

## The idea in one line

**The inbox is a waiting room, not a to-do list.** Every email that enters gets one clear outcome during a sweep, and then it leaves the inbox.

## Set up the inbox (first week on the account)

Do this once with the client and record it in their Client Workflow Record.

- [ ]  **Access:** delegated access to the client's inbox (your own login with permission to their mailbox). If the client can only share a password, it goes through the password manager. See the Security Standard.
- [ ]  **Folders or labels:** create the six Narqix folders below.
- [ ]  **VIP list:** people whose email always reaches the client fast (partners, top customers, family, investors, lawyers).
- [ ]  **Never-reply list:** people or topics you must never answer on the client's behalf.
- [ ]  **Sign-off:** agree how you sign. Default: you sign in your own name as part of the client's team, for example "(YOUR NAME), Office of (CLIENT NAME)". You never sign as the client unless they approve that type of email in writing.
- [ ]  **Digest time:** the time the client wants the daily digest, in their time zone.
- [ ]  **Sweep times:** at least three sweeps per shift (start, middle, end).
- [ ]  **Approved auto-actions:** for example "you may unsubscribe me from promotions".

### The six Narqix folders

| Folder | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| 01 Decide | Needs the client's decision or personal attention |
| 02 Drafts for You | You wrote a reply in the client's voice; it waits for their send |
| 03 Waiting On | Someone else owes a reply or action |
| 04 Read Later | Newsletters and updates the client wants to read |
| 05 Finance | Receipts, invoices, statements |
| 06 Reference | Keep for the record, no action needed |

Numbers in front keep the folders in the same order on every screen.

## The Six Outcomes

During a sweep, open each email once, read it fully, and give it exactly one outcome.

| Outcome | When | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Done** | You can fully handle it within your authority (scheduling, confirmations, simple requests, receipts) | Handle it now, then archive (receipts go to 05 Finance) |
| **2. Decide** | It needs a client decision | Move to 01 Decide; add one line to the digest with your recommended answer |
| **3. Draft** | It needs a reply in the client's voice | Write the draft in the thread; move to 02 Drafts for You; mention it in the digest |
| **4. Delegate** | Someone else should handle it (client's staff, vendor) | Forward with a one-line note; move to 03 Waiting On; add a follow up date |
| **5. Defer** | Information only | 04 Read Later or 06 Reference; unsubscribe from promotions if approved |
| **6. Danger** | Phishing, suspicious payment request, strange attachment | Do not click or reply. Report it as phishing. Tell your Narqix lead. Note it in your end of day report. |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: the two-minute test. If an email can be fully handled in under two minutes, do it during the sweep. If it needs more, turn it into a task in the tracker and archive the email. Your task tracker holds work; the inbox holds nothing.

</div>

### Worked example: a 9:00 AM sweep with 23 new emails

| Count | Type | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | Meeting requests and reschedules | Done (handled within calendar rules) |
| 4 | Receipts and invoices | Done, filed in 05 Finance, logged in finance sheet |
| 3 | Questions only the client can answer | Decide, with recommendations in the digest |
| 2 | Partner emails needing a personal reply | Draft, in 02 Drafts for You |
| 2 | Requests for the client's operations manager | Delegate, forwarded, in 03 Waiting On |
| 5 | Newsletters | Defer, 04 Read Later |
| 1 | "Invoice overdue, click here" from an unknown sender | Danger, reported |

23 in, 23 out. Inbox: zero unprocessed.

## The daily digest

Send the digest once a day at the client's chosen time. It replaces twenty separate messages with one.

> **Inbox digest, (DATE)**
**Needs your decision (3)**
1. (SENDER NAME), (COMPANY): asks to move the contract review to next week. My suggestion: yes, Tuesday 2:00 PM ET is free.
2. (SENDER NAME): wants a quote for 40 units. My suggestion: use last month's price sheet. Reply "yes" and I will send it.
3. (SENDER NAME): conference invite, 1,200 USD ticket. My suggestion: decline, it clashes with your board meeting.
**Drafts ready for you to send (2)**
1. Reply to (PARTNER NAME) about the renewal. Needs your voice because it discusses pricing.
2. Thank-you note to (CUSTOMER NAME).
**Handled for you (14):** 6 scheduling, 4 receipts filed, 4 vendor confirmations.
**Waiting on others (5):** oldest is (VENDOR NAME), 4 days. I followed up today.
> 

Rules for the digest:

1. Decisions first, because they block work.
2. Every decision has your recommendation.
3. Counts for what you handled, so the client sees your value.
4. Keep it under one screen. Details live in the emails, not the digest.

## Follow up cadence for 03 Waiting On

| Day after you sent it | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Day 2 | Polite nudge in the same thread |
| Day 4 | Second nudge, shorter, with a clear question |
| Day 6 | Tell the client in the digest and ask how to proceed |

Some contacts need faster follow up (deadlines, deals). Set the follow up date based on the deadline, not the calendar.

## Inbox health standards

| Measure | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Unprocessed emails after a sweep | 10 or fewer (target zero) |
| Items in 01 Decide older than 2 business days | Zero without a reminder to the client |
| VIP emails seen within | Your next sweep, or alert at once if urgent |
| Items in 03 Waiting On without a follow up date | Zero |

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never delete an email the client has not seen, unless it is clear spam and the client approved that rule. Never open attachments or links in suspicious emails. Never reply to legal, HR, press, investor or complaint emails without the client's explicit instruction.

</div>

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never mark an email as read and leave it in the inbox. A read email with no outcome is the most common way important messages get lost.

</div>

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Checking the inbox every few minutes | Sweep on schedule; set alerts only for VIPs |
| Moving emails without telling the client | The digest shows every Decide and Draft |
| Long forwarded chains with no context | One line on top: what it is, what you need, by when |
| Writing drafts that sound like you, not the client | Study 20 of the client's sent emails first; note their greetings, length and sign-off |
| Letting 03 Waiting On grow forever | Follow up cadence, then escalate to the client |

## Check yourself

1. Name the Six Outcomes.
2. A partner on the VIP list emails asking for a call today. The client is offline for 3 hours. What do you do?
3. What are the four rules for a good digest?
4. A vendor has not replied for 4 days. What is your next action?
5. An email says "Your mailbox is full, click here to verify". Which outcome is it, and what are your steps?
