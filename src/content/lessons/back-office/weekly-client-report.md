For every back office specialist who reports to a client. After reading you will be able to write a weekly report in under 30 minutes that shows outcomes in numbers, surfaces decisions and risks, and proves the value of the work.

Track: Back Office & Admin  ·  Role: All back office roles  ·  Time to complete: 20 min

</div>

## Why the weekly report matters

Daily reports (start of day and end of day, see the Reporting Standard in Narqix Standards) keep the client informed. The **weekly report** does something different: it shows the **pattern**. What got done, what improved, what is at risk, and what the client needs to decide. It is also the main way a client sees the value of Narqix beyond "hours worked".

A client who reads a good weekly report every Friday rarely asks "What does my VA actually do?"

## When and how

- **When:** last working day of the client's week, before the end of their day, in their time zone.
- **Where:** the channel the client prefers (email or team channel), with the full numbers in a shared sheet if needed.
- **Length:** one screen. The client should read it in two minutes.
- **Time to write:** under 30 minutes if your daily reports are good, because you collect the numbers from them.

## The structure: six blocks

| Block | Answers | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Headline | What is the one thing to know this week? | One sentence |
| 2. Numbers | How much, how fast, how accurate? | Compare with last week and target |
| 3. Wins | What improved or got finished? | Outcomes, not effort |
| 4. Issues and risks | What went wrong or might? | Each with what you did and what is next |
| 5. Decisions needed | What do you need from the client? | Each with your recommendation |
| 6. Next week | Top three priorities | Specific and dated |

## A full example

<div class="callout callout-note">

The example below is the report exactly as the client receives it.

</div>

**Weekly report, (CLIENT NAME), week ending (DATE)**

**Headline:** Invoice backlog is cleared, and supplier payments are now entered within 1 business day.

**Numbers**

| Measure | This week | Last week | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emails processed | 412 | 389 | All |
| Inbox unprocessed at end of each sweep (average) | 4 | 9 | 10 or fewer |
| Meetings booked and confirmed | 23 | 19 | All confirmed |
| Invoices entered (double entry) | 64 | 41 | All received |
| Data accuracy, QA sample | 100% money, 99.7% contact | 100%, 99.4% | 100%, 99.5% |
| CRM duplicates merged | 37 | 52 | Falling |

**Wins**

- Backlog of 38 old invoices cleared; all control totals match.
- New vendor onboarding checklist created; first two vendors onboarded in 2 days instead of 6.

**Issues and risks**

- Website form is creating duplicate CRM records (29 of 37 this week). Fix proposed below.
- (VENDOR NAME) has not sent their insurance certificate after 3 reminders. Their job starts (DATE).

**Decisions needed**

1. Turn on email matching in the website form so it updates existing records. My suggestion: yes. It stops most duplicates.
2. (VENDOR NAME) certificate: pause their start until it arrives? My suggestion: yes, as your policy requires it.

**Next week**

1. Quarterly CRM tag audit, proposal by Wednesday.
2. Travel for the (CITY) conference held and ready for approval by Tuesday.
3. Hiring: 6 interviews scheduled for the dispatcher role.

## Writing rules

1. **Numbers over adjectives.** Not "lots of emails", but "412 emails".
2. **Compare.** A number alone means little. Show last week and the target.
3. **Outcomes over activity.** Not "worked on invoices", but "cleared 38 old invoices".
4. **Bad news early and plainly.** Every issue has what you did and what happens next.
5. **Every decision has a recommendation** so the client can reply "yes to both".
6. **No surprises.** Nothing in the weekly report should be the first time the client hears about a serious problem. Urgent issues go through the Escalation Ladder the day they happen.
7. **Honest numbers only.** Never round up, never estimate without saying so. If a number is missing, write "not measured this week" and why.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: keep a running "report notes" document during the week. Every time something good, bad or decision-worthy happens, add one line. On Friday, the report almost writes itself, and you never forget the win from Monday.

</div>

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never invent, guess or "improve" a number in a report. A report with one made-up number makes every other number worthless. This is a serious conduct issue at Narqix.

</div>

## Before you send

- [ ]  Headline is one sentence and is the most important fact
- [ ]  Every number checked against its source (tracker, CRM, sheet)
- [ ]  Comparison with last week and target
- [ ]  Every issue has an action and a next step
- [ ]  Every decision has a recommendation
- [ ]  Client's spelling and date style
- [ ]  Fits on one screen
