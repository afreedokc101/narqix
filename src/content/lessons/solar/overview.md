## What this track is

The Solar Track trains Narqix appointment setters, confirmers, live transfer agents, lead managers and QA reviewers who work for US solar installers. You will not sell solar. You will create something the installer values even more: a qualified homeowner who shows up to a consultation with every decision maker present.

Everything in this track follows one idea:

<div class="callout callout-tip">

**Our product is the sit, not the set.** A set is a booking on a calendar. A sit is a real meeting that happens with the right people. Clients judge Narqix on sits, so every page in this track is built to protect them.

</div>

## How residential solar is sold in the US

Most US home solar is sold through a simple chain. Each link hands work to the next one.

1. **Lead generation.** The installer (or a lead company working for it) finds interested homeowners through online ads, web forms, door knocking, referrals, events and purchased lead lists.
2. **Setting.** A setter calls or texts the homeowner, checks whether the home is a fit, and books a consultation. **This is where most Narqix VAs work.**
3. **Confirming.** A confirmer makes sure the appointment still happens: written confirmation, a personal call a day or two before, a reminder on the day, and recovery if the homeowner goes quiet.
4. **The consultation.** A US-based sales representative (often called an energy consultant or closer) meets the homeowner at home or on a video call. The consultant reviews the electric bill, the roof and the financing options, then presents a proposal. This meeting usually takes 45 to 90 minutes.
5. **The agreement.** If the homeowner says yes, they sign with the installer. Many agreements carry a short legal cancellation window, which the consultant explains.
6. **Operations.** The installer's office team takes over: site survey, system design, permits, installation, inspection, and finally the utility's **Permission to Operate (PTO)**, which is the day the system can switch on.

### Who is who

| Party | What they do | How you deal with them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homeowner | Owns and lives in the home, pays the electric bill | The person you call. Treat them with patience and respect |
| Installer (your client) | The solar company that sells, designs and installs | The company you represent on every call. You say its name |
| Energy consultant (closer) | US sales rep who runs the consultation and signs the deal | You book their calendar and pass them clean notes |
| Setter | Qualifies homeowners and books consultations | Usually you |
| Confirmer | Protects each booking until it is held | You, or a teammate |
| Lead generator | Collects homeowner interest, sometimes sells it | Source of many of your leads. Their consent records matter |
| Utility (power company) | Supplies electricity and connects the solar system | Never claim to be them or to work with them |
| Financing partner | Lender or leasing company behind loans, leases and PPAs | The consultant handles all of this |

### How homeowners pay, in simple words

You must know which payment options your client offers so you can answer basic questions honestly. You never quote prices, payments, rates or savings. That is the consultant's job.

| Option | Who owns the panels | How the homeowner pays | What you can say |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cash** | Homeowner | Pays the full price up front | "The consultant can walk you through a cash option if that interests you." |
| **Loan** | Homeowner | Borrows the money and pays a monthly loan payment. Lenders check credit | "Many homeowners finance it. The consultant explains the options and what is needed to qualify." |
| **Lease** | A third-party company | Pays a fixed monthly fee to use the system | "There are options where you do not own the system and pay monthly. The consultant can compare them." |
| **PPA (power purchase agreement)** | A third-party company | Pays for the solar power the system produces, at an agreed rate per kilowatt-hour | Same as lease. Do not quote rates. |

Leases and PPAs together are called **third-party owned (TPO)** systems.

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** quote a price, a monthly payment, a savings amount, a savings percentage, or anything about taxes, credits, rebates or incentives. The homeowner federal solar credit ended for systems finished after December 31, 2025. Rules and incentives change often and vary by state, so all of it belongs to the licensed consultant. Your line is always: "The consultant will go through the real numbers and options for your home."

</div>

### Three facts about solar sales right now

1. **Incentives changed in 2026.** Homeowners hear old advertising and ask about it. You stay neutral and pass every money question to the consultant.
2. **Utility credit rules differ by state.** How a utility pays for extra solar power sent back to the grid (net metering or net billing) is generous in some states and small in others. You do not explain it. You only collect the utility name so the consultant can model it.
3. **Solar is one of the most sued industries for calling and texting.** Installers can be held legally responsible for calls made on their behalf. That means **your conduct is the client's legal risk.** Read the compliance page before your first dial.

## Why the sit matters so much

Leads are expensive. As a rough 2026 guide, a shared solar lead can cost about USD 25 to 100, an exclusive lead USD 100 to 250, and a booked appointment USD 150 to 500. When a booked appointment does not happen, the client loses the lead cost and a consultant's evening. That is why good clients pay for sits, and why Narqix measures you on sits and quality, not raw bookings.
