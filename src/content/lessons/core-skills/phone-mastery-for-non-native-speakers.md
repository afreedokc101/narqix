<div class="callout callout-intro">

For everyone who speaks with US and other English-speaking customers by phone. After reading this page and doing the drills, you will speak at a clear pace, sound warm and confident, listen actively, use mirroring and pauses well, and pronounce common US names, places, numbers and addresses correctly.

Track: All tracks  ·  Role: All Callers  ·  Time to complete: 40 min, plus daily practice

</div>

## The goal is not an American accent

You do not need to sound American. You need to be **easy to understand and pleasant to talk to**. Customers in the US talk with people from every country every day. What frustrates them is not an accent. It is speaking too fast, swallowing word endings, unclear numbers and not listening.

Clear, calm, warm and accurate beats any accent.

## 1. Pace

Most people speak too fast when they are nervous, and non-native speakers often speed up to sound fluent. Slow down.

- **Target pace:** about 130 to 150 words per minute in conversation. Slightly slower for numbers, addresses, names and anything important.
- **Self-test:** read the paragraph in the drill section below out loud and time yourself. It has about 150 words. Aim for 60 to 70 seconds.
- **Match the customer, gently.** If an older customer speaks slowly, slow down further. If a busy professional speaks fast, keep your words short but do not rush your pronunciation.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Speed comes from fewer words, not faster words. "Is now an OK time for a quick question?" is fast to say and easy to hear. A long sentence said quickly is neither.

</div>

## 2. Tone

People decide whether they like you in the first few seconds, mostly from your tone.

- **Smile while you speak.** It changes the shape of your mouth and listeners hear it as warmth. Try it with a recording.
- **Sit or stand upright.** Slouching makes your voice flat and tired.
- **End statements with a slightly downward tone.** It sounds confident: "I can help with that."
- **Use a rising tone only for real questions.** If every sentence rises, you sound unsure.
- **Match energy, one step calmer.** If the customer is excited, be warm and positive. If they are upset, be calm and steady. Never louder or faster than them.
- **Be human, not scripted.** Know the script so well you can say it like a conversation.

## 3. Clarity

Common habits that make Bengali speakers (and many South Asian speakers) harder to understand, with fixes:

| Habit | Example | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dropping word endings | "I ask" instead of "I asked"; "two hundred dollar" | Finish every word, especially -ed, -s and -t endings: "asked", "dollars", "first" |
| V and B or W mixed up | "bery good", "wery good" | V: top teeth touch the lower lip and buzz. W: round lips, no teeth. Practise "very well", "vacant", "west", "value" |
| Z said as J | "jero", "jip code" | Z is a buzzing S, with the tongue behind the teeth. Practise "zero, zone, ZIP code, size, business" |
| Extra vowel before S clusters | "ischool", "estreet", "ispeak" | Start straight on the S: "school", "street", "speak", "state", "spring" |
| S and SH mixed | "sheet" for "seat" | S: tongue behind teeth, thin hiss. SH: lips pushed forward |
| TH said as T or D | "tank you", "dis" | Tongue lightly between teeth: "thank you", "this", "three", "Thursday" |
| Flat stress on every syllable | "pro-PER-ty", "in-for-MA-tion" said evenly | Learn the stressed syllable: PROP-er-ty, in-for-MA-tion, ap-POINT-ment |
| Very soft volume at the end of sentences | Last words fade away | Keep your voice steady to the last word, especially names and numbers |

Note on stress: "appointment" is said ap-POINT-ment. "Property" is PROP-er-tee. "Address" is AD-dress (noun, US) or ad-DRESS. "Available" is a-VAIL-a-ble. "Interested" is IN-trest-ed (three syllables in US speech).

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never fake an American accent or use American slang you are unsure of ("y'all", "dude", "buddy"). It sounds forced, can confuse listeners, and can make customers feel you are pretending to be local.

</div>

## 4. Active listening

Customers can tell when you are waiting for your turn to talk instead of listening.

- **Let them finish.** Count one second of silence after they stop before you speak.
- **Use short listening signals** while they talk: "I see." "Got it." "OK." "That makes sense." "Mm-hm." Not after every sentence, just enough to show you are there.
- **Take notes during the call**, in short words, so you can repeat details back correctly.
- **Answer the question they asked**, not the one in your script.
- **Summarise before moving on:** "So the house is vacant, it needs a new roof, and you would like to sell within three months. Did I get that right?"
- **Listen for feelings, not just facts.** "My mother passed away last year and the house is just sitting there" is a fact about a property and a feeling about a loss. Respond to both.

## 5. Mirroring and labelling

Two simple techniques make people feel heard and encourage them to share more.

**Mirroring:** repeat the last one to three important words the person said, with a gentle curious tone, then pause.

> **Them:** "We've just had a lot going on with the tenants."
**You:** "A lot going on with the tenants?"
**Them:** "Yeah, they stopped paying in March and now..."
> 

**Labelling:** name the feeling you are hearing, starting with "It sounds like..." or "It seems like..."

> **You:** "It sounds like it's been a really stressful few months."
> 

Rules:

- Use mirroring two or three times in a call, not after every sentence.
- Never mirror in a mocking way or copy their accent.
- After a mirror or label, **stop talking** and let them fill the silence.

## 6. The power of pausing

Silence feels long to the speaker and short to the listener. Use it on purpose.

| Pause | Where | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 second | After your greeting and name | Lets the person register who you are |
| 2 to 3 seconds | After asking a question | Gives them time to think; do not answer for them |
| 1 second | Before important information | Signals "listen to this" |
| Short breaks | Inside numbers and addresses | Makes them easy to write down |
| 2 seconds | After a mirror or label | Invites them to say more |

## 7. Pronouncing US names

Names matter more than anything. Get a name right and people relax. Get it wrong and they stop trusting you.

- **Check before you dial.** If you are unsure, use a pronunciation resource your campaign allows, or ask politely: "Could you help me say your name correctly?" Then write the pronunciation in the CRM.
- **When unsure on a call, use the full name less**, and use "sir" or "ma'am" politely until confirmed.

## 9. Numbers

Numbers cause more mistakes on calls than anything else.

**Teens versus tens:** "fifteen" and "fifty" sound similar on a phone line.

| Number | Say | If it must be clear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 13 | thir-TEEN (stress the end) | "one-three" |
| 30 | THIR-tee (stress the start) | "three-zero" |
| 15 | fif-TEEN | "one-five" |
| 50 | FIF-tee | "five-zero" |
| 18 | eigh-TEEN | "one-eight" |
| 80 | EIGH-tee | "eight-zero" |

**Phone numbers:** say them in groups of 3, 3 and 4, with a short pause between groups.

> "That's four-zero-five ... five-five-five ... zero-one-three-four."
> 

Americans often say "oh" for zero in phone numbers. Both are fine. Be consistent.

**Money:** say the full amount clearly and confirm.

| Written | Say |
| --- | --- |
| USD 185,000 | "one hundred eighty-five thousand dollars" |
| USD 1,250 | "one thousand two hundred fifty dollars" or "twelve hundred fifty dollars" |
| USD 12.50 | "twelve dollars and fifty cents" |

Americans may say "one eighty-five" meaning 185,000 in a real estate or price context. Always confirm: "Just to confirm, that's one hundred eighty-five thousand?"

**Dates:** Americans say month then day: "October eighteenth". Always confirm the day of the week too: "That's Tuesday, October twentieth, at 2 PM your time."

## 10. Addresses

US addresses are read in a specific way.

| Written | Say |
| --- | --- |
| 1234 Oak St | "twelve thirty-four Oak Street" |
| 1005 Pine Ave | "ten oh five Pine Avenue" |
| 200 Main St | "two hundred Main Street" |
| 4 Elm Ct | "four Elm Court" |
| 15802 County Rd 9 | "fifteen eight-oh-two County Road Nine" |
| 77 W 3rd St, Apt 5B | "seventy-seven West Third Street, Apartment five B" |
| ZIP 02134 | "zero-two-one-three-four" (always digit by digit) |

**Common abbreviations:** St (Street), Ave (Avenue), Blvd (Boulevard), Dr (Drive), Ln (Lane), Rd (Road), Ct (Court), Cir (Circle), Pl (Place), Pkwy (Parkway), Hwy (Highway), Ter (Terrace), Apt (Apartment), Ste (Suite), N, S, E, W (North, South, East, West), NE (Northeast). Always say the full word.

**Always read back** an address, phone number, email or amount, and spell names using the phonetic alphabet.

## 11. Spelling with the phonetic alphabet

When spelling names, emails and street names, use these words:

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A, Alpha | H, Hotel | O, Oscar | V, Victor |
| B, Bravo | I, India | P, Papa | W, Whiskey |
| C, Charlie | J, Juliet | Q, Quebec | X, X-ray |
| D, Delta | K, Kilo | R, Romeo | Y, Yankee |
| E, Echo | L, Lima | S, Sierra | Z, Zulu |
| F, Foxtrot | M, Mike | T, Tango |  |
| G, Golf | N, November | U, Uniform |  |

> "That's M as in Mike, A as in Alpha, R as in Romeo, Y as in Yankee."
> 

Many Americans use everyday words instead ("B as in boy, D as in dog"). That is fine too. Just be consistent and clear.

**Email addresses:** say "at" for @, "dot" for the period, and "underscore", "dash" or "hyphen" for symbols. Read it back slowly in groups.

## Daily drills (15 minutes)

1. **Pace drill (3 minutes):** read this paragraph aloud and time it. Target 60 to 70 seconds.

> Good afternoon, and thank you for taking my call. My name is (YOUR NAME), and I am calling on behalf of (COMPANY NAME). Just so you know, this call may be recorded for quality. I am reaching out because you recently asked about our services, and I wanted to make sure you got the information you needed. I only need a few minutes of your time. First, I would like to confirm a couple of details so I can point you in the right direction. Could you tell me a little about what you were looking for and what timeline you have in mind? There is no pressure at all. If now is not a good time, I am happy to call back at a time that works better for you this week. What would you prefer?
> 
1. **Ending sounds (3 minutes):** say clearly: asked, worked, first, dollars, months, needs, helped, streets, lists.
2. **Number drill (3 minutes):** say ten random phone numbers, addresses and amounts in the correct groups.
3. **Name and place drill (3 minutes):** practise ten names and places from tomorrow's list.
4. **Record and review (3 minutes):** record one practice call opening. Listen back once for pace, once for tone.

## Check yourself

1. What is the target conversational pace in words per minute?
2. How do you say the phone number 405-555-0134 on a call?
3. A customer says "We'd want about one fifty for it." What do you say next?
4. What is mirroring, and what do you do right after you mirror?
5. How do you say "1005 Pine Ave, Apt 2C, ZIP 02134"?
