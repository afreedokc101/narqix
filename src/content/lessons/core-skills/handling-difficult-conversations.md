For everyone who speaks or writes to customers, clients or vendors. After reading this page you will be able to calm an upset person, deliver bad news, say no without damaging the relationship, set boundaries with abusive people, and know exactly when to escalate.

Track: All tracks  ·  Role: Everyone  ·  Time to complete: 30 min

</div>

## What makes a conversation difficult

Most difficult conversations have one of these at the centre:

- **Anger:** something went wrong and they want it fixed now.
- **Fear or stress:** money trouble, a home at risk, a broken air conditioner in a heat wave.
- **Grief:** a death in the family, often behind an inherited property or a cancelled service.
- **Confusion:** they do not understand the process and feel lost.
- **Disappointment:** you have to say no, or the news is bad.
- **Abuse:** the person attacks you personally.

The skill is the same in every case: **stay calm, make them feel heard, move to what can be done, and follow through.**

## The L.A.N.D. method

Use these four steps to bring any difficult conversation to a safe landing.

| Step | What you do | What it sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **L: Let them finish** | Do not interrupt, defend or explain. Take notes. In writing, read the whole message twice. | (Silence, then) "I'm listening." |
| **A: Acknowledge** | Name what happened and how they feel, in their words. Apologise for the experience when it is right to. | "You were told someone would call on Tuesday and nobody did. That's really frustrating, and I'm sorry." |
| **N: Navigate to what you can do** | Say clearly what you will do and when. If you cannot do what they want, say so early and offer the best alternative. | "Here's what I'm doing right now: ... You'll hear from me by 3 PM your time." |
| **D: Document and deliver** | Write the note, escalate if needed, and keep every promise, on time. | (CRM note, then the follow-up exactly when promised) |

## Phrases that help

- "Let me make sure I understand. You were charged twice and haven't heard back in three days?"
- "You're right to expect better than that."
- "I'd feel the same way in your situation."
- "Here's what I can do right now."
- "I can't do (X), but I can do (Y) today."
- "I don't have that answer yet. I'll find out and call you back by (TIME AND ZONE)."
- "Thank you for telling me. That helps me fix this."

## Phrases that make it worse

| Avoid | Why | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Calm down." | Nobody has ever calmed down because they were told to | "I can hear how frustrating this is." |
| "As I already said..." | Sounds impatient and blaming | "Let me put it another way." |
| "That's our policy." (as the whole answer) | Feels like a wall | "Our refund window is 30 days. Here's what I can offer instead." |
| "You should have..." | Blames the customer | "Going forward, the fastest way is..." |
| "I'm just the assistant." | Sounds powerless and unhelpful | "Let me get this to the right person, and I'll stay on it." |
| "Unfortunately, there's nothing I can do." | Ends hope | "Here's what I can do." |
| "No problem!" (after a complaint) | Minimises their problem | "I'm glad you told me." |
| Over-apologising | Sounds scripted and weak | One sincere apology, then action |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Lower your voice and slow down when the other person speeds up. People unconsciously match the pace of the person they talk to. If you stay slow and steady, most angry callers calm down within 60 to 90 seconds.

</div>

## Saying no

You will often need to refuse a request: a refund outside policy, a price you cannot offer, a date you cannot meet.

1. **Say it early.** Do not build hope you will have to break later.
2. **Give the real reason briefly.** One sentence.
3. **Offer the best alternative** you are allowed to offer.
4. **Let them choose.**

> "I'm not able to refund this one, because it was delivered 45 days ago and our window is 30 days. What I can do today is add a store credit for the full amount, which never expires. Would that help?"
> 

## Delivering bad news

1. **Prepare.** Know the facts, the impact and the next steps before you call or write.
2. **Lead with the news.** Do not hide it after small talk.
3. **Be honest about impact.** Do not soften it until it is untrue.
4. **Say what happens next** and when they will hear from you.
5. **Follow through** exactly as promised, even if there is nothing new.

> "I'm calling with an update on your closing. The title company found a lien from 2019 that needs to be cleared first. That will likely move closing by about two weeks. They're already working on it, and I'll update you every Tuesday and Friday until it's resolved, starting this Friday."
> 

## Grief and stress

Many customers you speak with are going through something hard: a parent's death, a divorce, job loss, a home in foreclosure.

- **Slow down.** Their situation is not a task on your list.
- **Acknowledge simply:** "I'm so sorry for your loss." Then pause. Do not rush to the next question.
- **Never use their pain to push.** No pressure, no false urgency.
- **Offer control:** "Would you rather we talk another day?"
- **Note it sensitively** in the CRM so the next person knows: "Owner's mother passed in June. Be gentle; prefers calls after 5 PM CT."

## Abuse: setting boundaries

You are not required to accept insults, slurs, threats or sexual comments. Narqix will always support you when you end an abusive conversation the right way.

1. **First time:** acknowledge their frustration and set a calm boundary.

> "I want to help sort this out, and I can only continue if we keep it respectful."
> 
1. **Second time:** give a clear warning.

> "If the language continues, I'll need to end the call. I'd still like to help."
> 
1. **Third time:** end politely.

> "I'm going to end the call now. Someone from our team will follow up. Goodbye."
> 
1. **Afterwards:** note the call factually, tell your Campaign Lead, and take two minutes to reset before your next contact.

See the Script Library entry "Handling Abusive or Angry Callers" for the full flow.

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never insult, mock, argue with or hang up on a customer without warning (except for threats of violence, which you report immediately). Never mute and complain about a customer while they can still hear. Never record personal opinions about them in the CRM.

</div>

## When to escalate immediately (P1)

- Threats of violence against anyone
- A customer says they might harm themselves
- Threats of a lawsuit, regulator complaint or media story
- Mentions of discrimination or harassment by the client's staff
- Anything involving a child's safety

**If a customer says they may harm themselves:** stay calm and kind, keep them talking, and do not end the call abruptly. Follow your campaign's emergency protocol and alert your Campaign Lead immediately. In the US, people can call or text **988** to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline; you may share this number gently: "I care about what happens to you. You can call or text 988 any time to talk to someone right now." If they say they are in immediate danger, follow your campaign's emergency steps.

## Difficult conversations in writing

- Read the whole message twice before replying.
- Answer every point they raised, in order.
- First line shows you read it: "I'm sorry the table arrived broken, especially as it was a gift."
- Write the reply, wait one minute, reread it, and delete anything defensive.
- Complaint replies are scored at a higher pass mark (90%). For a P2 complaint, ask your lead or a Senior to review before sending.

## Looking after yourself

Difficult calls are draining. After a hard one:

1. Take three slow breaths (in for 4 seconds, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4).
2. Write the note while it is fresh, then let it go.
3. Remind yourself: their anger is about their situation, not about you.
4. If you need a minute before the next contact, tell your lead.
5. If a call stays with you, talk about it at your next one-to-one.

## Check yourself

1. What do the four letters in L.A.N.D. stand for?
2. Rewrite: "Calm down, sir. That's our policy."
3. A customer swears at you personally for the second time. What do you say?
4. A customer tells you they "don't see the point anymore". What do you do?
5. Why should you say no early instead of late?
