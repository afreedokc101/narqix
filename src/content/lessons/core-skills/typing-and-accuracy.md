## Accuracy first, then speed

A fast typist who makes errors creates work for everyone: wrong phone numbers, misspelled names, amounts off by a zero. In data work, **one wrong digit can send money to the wrong place or a technician to the wrong house.** At Narqix, accuracy always comes first. Speed is built on top of it.

## The targets

| Area | Minimum | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typing speed, all roles | 40 words per minute at 97% accuracy | 55 words per minute at 98% or better |
| Typing speed, back office, support and transaction roles | 50 words per minute at 98% accuracy | 65 words per minute at 99% |
| Financial data (amounts, account numbers) | 100% accurate | 100% accurate |
| Contact data (names, phones, emails, addresses) | 99.5% accurate | 99.9% accurate |
| Status, tag and dropdown fields | 99% accurate | 100% accurate |

You will take a typing test during your first week and again at each level review.

## Why "I looked it over" is not a check

Research on data entry shows that simply looking over your own work catches far fewer errors than people think. Your eyes see what you **expect** to see. The methods below catch errors that a quick look misses.

## Methods that catch errors

**1. Double entry or control totals (for financial data)**

- Enter the data twice (or have two people enter it) and compare. Any difference is checked against the source.
- Or use a **control total:** add up the amounts in the source batch and in your entered batch. If the totals do not match exactly, find the difference before you continue.

**2. Batch checks (for any list)**

Work in batches of 25 to 50 records. After each batch, check:

- [ ]  Record count matches the source
- [ ]  Amount totals match (if any amounts)
- [ ]  US phone numbers have exactly 10 digits
- [ ]  Emails have one @ and a valid ending
- [ ]  ZIP codes are 5 digits, stored as text (leading zeros kept)
- [ ]  One date format throughout
- [ ]  No extra spaces before or after values

**3. Read-back (for anything spoken)**

Read back every name, number, address, email and amount to the customer before you save it.

**4. Source discipline**

- Always enter from the **original source**, not from a copy of a copy.
- If the source is unclear (blurry, handwritten, conflicting), **do not guess.** Put the item in a "Held" list with the reason, and ask.

**5. Change of eyes**

For important documents, read the final version in a different way than you wrote it: out loud, backwards for numbers, or printed to a different view on screen. The change forces your brain to actually read.

## The most common typing errors

| Error | Example | How to catch it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transposed digits | 4053 typed as 4035 | Read numbers in pairs; check against source |
| Missing or extra zero | 18,500 vs 185,000 | Control totals; say the number in words |
| Wrong similar name | Steven vs Stephen; Ann vs Anne | Copy names from the source; ask spelling |
| Wrong state code | MI vs MN, AL vs AK | Check the city matches the state |
| Homophones | their/there, your/you're, its/it's | Spell-check does not catch these; reread |
| Autocorrect changes | The surname "Wren" changed to "When" | Check every name after typing |
| Copy-paste carries extra spaces | " Sam Sample" | Trim spaces; batch check |

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never guess a missing digit, letter or amount. Never "fix" data you think is wrong without checking the source and noting what you changed. Never type card numbers or passwords into notes or chat.

## Working smart

- **Learn touch typing.** Typing without looking at the keyboard is the single biggest speed gain. It feels slower for two weeks, then much faster forever.
- **Use keyboard shortcuts** for copy, paste, undo, search and switching windows. Seconds saved per action add up to hours per month.
- **Use approved text snippets or saved replies** for common phrases, but always personalise and check them.
- **Use spell-check and grammar tools** approved for your campaign, but never trust them with names, numbers or meaning.
- **Over about 50 records of repetitive entry?** Ask your lead if an import or automation is possible. Suggesting it is a sign of a Senior.

## Posture and comfort

- Wrists straight and floating, not bent up on the desk edge.
- Elbows at about 90 degrees, shoulders relaxed.
- Screen at eye level, about an arm's length away.
- Short stretch for hands and wrists every hour.

## Daily practice plan (15 minutes)

| Minutes | Practice |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Touch-typing drill on an approved practice site or tool, focus on accuracy, not speed |
| 5 | Number drill: type 20 phone numbers, ZIP codes and amounts from a sample sheet, then check every one |
| 5 | Note drill: write one CRM note in the Narqix format from a practice scenario, then check it against the source |

Track your speed and accuracy once a week. Most people gain 10 to 15 words per minute in two months with daily practice.
