For everyone working remotely on evening or overnight shifts. After reading this page you will be able to plan your shift, protect your focus, manage your energy through the night, and bounce back from rejection and hard moments.

Track: All tracks  ·  Role: Everyone  ·  Time to complete: 20 min

## Nobody is watching, and everybody can see

Working from home at night, there is no manager walking past your desk. That freedom is also the challenge. Your results, reports and tracker data tell the story of every shift. The people who succeed here are not the ones with the most willpower. They are the ones with the best **systems**: a routine that makes the right action automatic.

This page is about focus and productivity. For sleep, food and health, read Night Shift Health and Wellbeing in Narqix Standards.

## 1. Start the shift the same way every time

A fixed start routine tells your brain "work mode now".

1. **10 minutes before:** pre-shift check (power, internet, headset, logins). See Your Setup.
2. **Check in** with your campaign's check-in method; start the tracker with a clear task note.
3. **Read** overnight messages, handover notes and the CRM tasks due today.
4. **Plan:** pick your top 3 priorities and post your start-of-day report.
5. **Begin with something concrete:** your first call, first ticket, first record. Not "checking things".

## 2. Plan the shift in blocks

| Block | Best for | Tips |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First third** (fresh) | Hardest work: high-value calls, complex files, writing | Protect it from small tasks |
| **Middle** (steady) | Volume work: dialling blocks, ticket queues, data entry | Work in focused sprints with short resets |
| **The dip** (often about 3 AM to 5 AM local time) | Routine work with built-in checks | Stand, stretch, cold water; double-check numbers |
| **Last 45 minutes** | Clean-up, notes, follow-ups, end-of-day report, handover | Never start something big you cannot finish or hand over |

On calling campaigns, the calling windows are set by the customers' local times and compliance rules, so plan the rest of your work around them.

## 3. Focus sprints

- Work in **sprints of 25 to 50 minutes** on one type of task, then a 2 to 5 minute reset (stand, water, stretch, eyes away from screen). These short resets are separate from your logged breaks and do not mean leaving your work.
- **One task type per sprint.** Switching between calls, emails and data entry every few minutes costs time and causes errors.
- **Batch the small stuff.** Check your inbox or team channel at set points (for example at the end of each sprint), unless your role requires live monitoring or something is urgent.

## 4. Remove distractions

- **Personal phone:** silent, face down, out of reach. Agree with family how to reach you in an emergency.
- **Tracked time is work time.** No personal browsing, social media or personal chat. Clients can see tracker data.
- **One screen for work, one for reference.** Close everything you are not using.
- **Household:** a clear "working" signal (closed door, headset on) that your family respects.

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never leave the tracker running while you are away from your desk, and never use tools or tricks to look active. Never do personal tasks during tracked time. It is dishonest, and it is visible.

</div>

## 5. Prioritise with "Must, Should, Could"

When everything feels urgent, sort your list:

| Category | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Must** | A promise, deadline or compliance requirement today | Callbacks promised for tonight, a closing document due, a customer waiting for an update |
| **Should** | Important for KPIs, not time-critical tonight | Dialling block, CRM cleanup, research task |
| **Could** | Nice to do if time allows | Improving a template, organising files |

Do every **Must** first. If a Must is at risk, tell your lead early, not at the end of the shift.

## 6. Energy, not just time

- Eat a proper meal before your shift and light snacks during it.
- Water at your desk; drink through the shift.
- Caffeine early in the shift only.
- Stand and stretch every hour.
- Use your logged breaks fully. Working through breaks makes the last hours worse.

## 7. Handling rejection (callers especially)

On outbound calls, most people say no. That is normal and it is not about you.

- **Know the numbers.** If your campaign needs about 20 conversations for one qualified lead, every "no" is one step closer. Count conversations, not just wins.
- **Reset after every call.** One breath, one quick note, next dial. Do not carry one rude person into the next conversation.
- **Celebrate the process:** a clean disposition, a perfect note, a polite exit after a "no". These are wins.
- **Review, do not ruminate.** At the end of the shift, pick one call you could improve and one you handled well. Then stop thinking about it.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Set "process goals" alongside results goals. "Every note in format, every DNC coded instantly, every callback on time" is fully in your control. Results follow good process, and good process protects you on nights when results are slow.

</div>

## 8. End the shift cleanly

1. Finish or hand over every open customer conversation.
2. Update every record you touched.
3. Write your end-of-day report with real numbers.
4. Write tomorrow's top 3 so you start fast next shift.
5. Stop the tracker and check out.
6. **Switch off.** Close the work profile, dim the screen, and begin your wind-down for sleep.

## 9. Weekly reflection (10 minutes)

At the end of your work week, answer three questions in your notes:

1. What worked well this week, and why?
2. What got in the way, and what will I change?
3. What one skill will I practise next week?

Share the answers at your weekly scorecard review. Your lead can help with anything that got in the way.

## Check yourself

1. What are the five steps of the shift start routine?
2. Which part of the shift should hold your hardest work, and why?
3. Sort these into Must, Should, Could: improving an email template; a callback promised for 8 PM ET tonight; a 2-hour dialling block.
4. What do you do in the last 45 minutes of a shift?
5. What is a "process goal"? Give one example.

<details class="toggle toggle-answers"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Pre-shift check, check in and start the tracker, read messages and handover, plan top 3 and post the start-of-day report, begin with something concrete.
2. The first third, when you are freshest and make the fewest mistakes.
3. Must: the promised callback. Should: the dialling block. Could: improving the template.
4. Clean-up: notes, follow-ups, handover, end-of-day report, and tomorrow's top 3. Nothing big you cannot finish.
5. A goal fully in your control. Examples: every note in format, every DNC coded instantly, every callback on time.

</details>
