For every support specialist. After reading you will be able to move any ticket from arrival to closure correctly: triage it, set priority, own it, use the right status, hand it over cleanly and close it with confidence.

Track: Customer Support  ·  Role: Support Agent, CSR  ·  Time to complete: 25 min

</div>

## The lifecycle at a glance

| Stage | What happens | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Arrive | Customer contacts by email, chat, social, phone or form. Automatic acknowledgment tells them when to expect a reply. | Ticketing system |
| 2. Triage | Read, tag the topic, set priority, check history and order | Tier 1 |
| 3. Own | One named owner from now until solved or handed over | Tier 1 or Tier 2 |
| 4. Work | Resolve within policy and authority, or escalate with a full note | Owner |
| 5. Wait (if needed) | Pending (customer owes us) or On-hold (internal team or vendor owes us) | Owner |
| 6. Resolve | Customer has the answer or the fix; next steps confirmed | Owner |
| 7. Solve and survey | Status Solved; satisfaction survey sent automatically | Owner |
| 8. Close or reopen | Closes after the client's set period; a customer reply reopens it | Ticketing system |
| 9. Learn | Root-cause tag feeds the weekly top reasons report | Owner, Tier 2 |

## Stage 2: Triage in 60 seconds

For every new ticket, before you write anything:

- [ ]  Read the whole message (and the whole thread if it is a reply)
- [ ]  Check the customer's previous tickets and notes
- [ ]  Open the order, account or booking
- [ ]  Tag the contact reason from the approved list
- [ ]  Set the priority
- [ ]  Check for linked tickets (the same customer writing through two channels)

### Priority levels

| Priority | Examples | First response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Urgent** | Safety risk, legal threat, suspected account takeover, payment taken but no order, public complaint spreading, VIP customer | Immediately; follow the Escalation Ladder |
| **High** | Order past its promised date, product unusable, billing error, event-date gift at risk | Ahead of Normal, within target |
| **Normal** | How-to, return request, order change, general questions | Within target, oldest first |
| **Low** | Feedback, feature ideas, compliments | Within target, after Normal |

Priority is about **impact on the customer and the business**, not about how loud the message is. An angry message about a 5 USD shipping fee is still Normal. A polite message saying "I was charged three times" is High.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: always check for a second contact. Upset customers often email, then chat, then post on social. If you answer one without linking the others, two agents may give two different answers. Merge or link first, then reply once.

</div>

## Stage 3: Ownership

1. **If you touch it, you own it.** Once you reply, the ticket is yours until it is solved or handed over formally.
2. **A formal handover** means an internal note (below) and a reassignment, plus telling the customer if a new person will reply.
3. **Shift change:** any ticket you own that will need action before your next shift gets a handover note to the next shift or your lead.

## Stage 4: The internal note

Write an internal note whenever someone else might pick up the ticket. Use this format:

> **Issue:** (one line)
**Checked:** (what you looked at and found)
**Done:** (what you did)
**Promised:** (what you told the customer, and by when, with zone)
**Next:** (what needs to happen, and who)
> 

Example:

> **Issue:** Order not delivered, tracking shows "delivered" 3 days ago.
**Checked:** Address matches the order. Carrier shows delivery photo at a different door.
**Done:** Opened carrier claim, reference (CLAIM NUMBER).
**Promised:** Update by Thursday 12:00 PM ET.
**Next:** If no carrier answer by Thursday, Tier 2 to approve resend (over my limit).
> 

## Stage 5: Statuses (use them honestly)

| Status | Means | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **New** | Nobody has looked at it | Never leave a ticket here longer than the first response target |
| **Open** | We owe the next action | You are working on it or will soon |
| **Pending** | The customer owes the next action | You asked a question or need a photo, document or confirmation |
| **On-hold** | Someone inside the business or a vendor owes us | Waiting on warehouse, carrier, client, product team |
| **Solved** | The issue is resolved, or the reply is complete and nothing more is owed | After the final answer |
| **Closed** | Locked by the system after the client's set period | Automatic |

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never set a ticket to Pending when we owe the next step, just to stop a response timer or clean your queue. It hides work, breaks reports and leaves customers waiting. It is a QA fail.

</div>

### Pending follow ups

If a Pending ticket gets no reply, follow the client's timing. A common pattern: a friendly reminder after 2 business days, a last check after 4, then Solved with a note that the customer can reply anytime (see the No Reply Follow Up template).

### On-hold follow ups

On-hold does not mean "forget". Check every On-hold ticket daily. If an internal answer is late, chase it, and send the customer the promised update even if there is no news.

## Stage 6 and 7: Resolving and solving

A ticket is ready to solve when:

- [ ]  Every question the customer asked is answered
- [ ]  Any action promised is done (refund issued, label sent, account fixed)
- [ ]  The customer knows what happens next (for example "the refund appears in 5 to 10 business days")
- [ ]  Tags, notes and fields are complete
- [ ]  The root-cause tag is set

## Stage 8: Reopens

A reopen means the customer was not fully helped. Treat it as a learning signal:

1. Read why it reopened.
2. Fix the issue properly this time.
3. If the first reply missed something, note the lesson for yourself.
4. If the process or macro caused it, tell your lead.

## Stage 9: Learning from tickets

At the end of every week, Tier 2 or the lead sends the client the **top 5 contact reasons**, with numbers and a suggested fix for each. For example: "112 of 640 tickets (18%) asked where their order was. 70% of those were within the normal delivery window. Suggestion: add the delivery estimate to the order confirmation email."

This is where support stops being a cost and starts improving the client's business.

## Check yourself

1. List the six triage checks.
2. A polite customer says they were charged three times. What priority, and why?
3. What is the difference between Pending and On-hold?
4. Write an internal note for a ticket where you are waiting for the warehouse to confirm stock.
5. A ticket is On-hold and you promised an update today, but the warehouse has not replied. What do you do?
