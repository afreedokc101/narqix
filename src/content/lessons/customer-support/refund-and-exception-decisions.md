For every support specialist who handles refunds, credits, replacements and policy exceptions. After reading you will be able to make the right decision every time using the client's matrix, run the pre-refund checks, spot abuse, and escalate with a clear recommendation.

Track: Customer Support  ·  Role: Support Agent, CSR, Tier 2  ·  Time to complete: 30 min

</div>

## The principle

Refunds are the client's money. You do not decide what feels fair. You apply the client's written rules, consistently, to every customer. When the rules do not cover a case, you escalate with a recommendation.

## The Refund and Exception Matrix

Every client has a **Refund and Exception Matrix** in their Client Workflow Record. If you cannot find it, ask your lead before your first refund. It looks like this:

| Situation | Tier 1 can | Tier 2 can | Client decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Damaged or wrong item, reported within 7 days with photos | Replace or refund up to 100 USD | Replace or refund up to 300 USD | Above 300 USD |
| Return inside the 30-day window, unused | Return label and refund on receipt | Same |  |
| Return outside the window | Offer store credit if allowed, up to 50 USD | One-time exception up to 150 USD | Above 150 USD |
| Lost in transit (after carrier confirms) | Resend or refund up to 100 USD | Up to 300 USD | Above 300 USD |
| Late delivery, no loss | Shipping fee refund | Small goodwill credit |  |
| Subscription charge after cancellation request | Refund the last charge if the request was before renewal | Refund up to 2 charges | More than 2 |
| Chargeback filed | Do not refund; escalate | Prepare evidence | Decides |

Amounts here are examples only. **Always use your client's real matrix.**

## The decision path

Work through these questions in order, every time:

1. **Is there a chargeback or dispute already open?** If yes, do not refund. Escalate to Tier 2 (refunding plus a chargeback means the client pays twice).
2. **Does the order exist and is it paid?** Check the order and payment status.
3. **Was it already refunded, partly refunded, or replaced?** Check the order history and past tickets.
4. **Does it meet the policy?** Window, condition, proof (photos, tracking, serial number).
5. **Any abuse flags?** See below.
6. **Is it within your authority?** Check the matrix amount for your tier.
7. **Decide:** if all checks pass and it is within your authority, act. If not, escalate with a recommendation.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: run the checks before you reply, not after. Telling a customer "I've approved your refund" and then finding it was already refunded last week creates a much harder conversation than taking two extra minutes to check.

</div>

## Abuse and fraud signals

Most customers are honest. A few are not. Watch for:

- Several "item not received" claims in a short time
- Claims on items delivered with signature or photo proof
- A request to refund to a **different** card, account or person
- A new account with a large first order and an immediate claim
- Pressure to act "right now" with threats of bad reviews to skip checks
- Photos that look like they come from the internet or another order

If you see a flag, **do not accuse the customer**. Stay polite, follow the normal process, and escalate to Tier 2 with what you noticed in an internal note.

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never refund to anything other than the original payment method, and never send money to an account the customer names. If a customer says the original card is closed, the card issuer usually still passes the refund to the customer's account; tell them so and follow the client's process for exceptions.

</div>

## Telling the customer

**Approved:**

> "I've refunded (AMOUNT) to the card ending in (LAST FOUR DIGITS). Banks usually show it within 5 to 10 business days."
> 

**Declined, with an alternative:**

> "Your order was delivered on (DATE), outside our 30-day window, so I can't refund it. What I can offer is (ALTERNATIVE). Would that work?"
> 

**Escalated:**

> "This needs approval from our senior team. I've sent them everything, including your photos, and you'll hear from us by (TIME AND ZONE)."
> 

**Rules:**

1. Honest timing. Never say "instantly" unless it is. Refund times depend on the customer's bank.
2. Never promise approval you do not control.
3. Name the payment method by the last four digits only.

## Escalating with a recommendation

When a case is above your authority, write an internal note the approver can decide on in one minute:

> **Refund approval needed (P3)**
**Order:** (ORDER NUMBER), 412 USD, delivered (DATE)
**Issue:** lamp arrived with a broken base; photos attached
**Policy:** damage within 7 days, reported day 1. Meets policy.
**Checks:** paid, no previous refunds, no chargeback, no abuse flags
**Options:** full refund; replacement (in stock); partial refund 100 USD if kept
**Recommendation:** replacement, customer is happy with that option
**Promised update:** today, 4:00 PM ET
> 

## Goodwill gestures

Some clients allow small goodwill credits (for example a shipping refund or a discount code) when service went wrong even if policy was followed. Use them only when the matrix allows, and only for real service failures. Never use goodwill to make an angry customer go away when the client's policy says no; escalate instead.

## The exception log

Every exception (anything outside standard policy) is logged:

| Date | Ticket | Customer | Exception | Amount | Reason | Approved by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (DATE) | (TICKET REFERENCE) | (CUSTOMER NAME) | Refund outside window | 85 USD | Delivery delayed 3 weeks by carrier | (APPROVER NAME) |

Monthly exception totals go into the client report. If the same exception happens often, the policy may need to change. Suggest it.

## Chargebacks in short

- Do not refund once a chargeback is open (unless the client instructs).
- Do not argue with the customer about it.
- Tier 2 prepares the evidence pack: order, payment, delivery proof, messages, policy.
- The client decides and responds to the bank.
