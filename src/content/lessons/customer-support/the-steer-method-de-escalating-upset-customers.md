## Why customers get angry

Most angry customers are not angry at you. They are angry because something went wrong, often more than once, and they feel nobody is listening. Under the anger is usually one of three things:

1. **Lost time:** "I've already contacted you three times."
2. **Lost money or a lost moment:** "The gift was for her birthday."
3. **Lost control:** "Nobody tells me what's happening."

Your job is to give them back what they lost: **attention, a plan, and control**.

## The STEER Method

STEER is the Narqix five-step method for turning an upset customer into a resolved one. You steer the conversation from emotion to action.

| Step | What you do | On email and chat | On the phone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S: Settle** | Let them finish. Take in everything before responding. | Read the full message twice. Do not reply in the first minute. | Do not interrupt. Short sounds ("I see", "mm-hm") show you are there. |
| **T: Tell it back** | Restate the problem in their words, so they know you understood. | "So you were charged twice on 3 October and haven't heard back in three days." | "Let me make sure I have this right…" then pause for their yes. |
| **E: Empathize and own** | One sincere sentence about how it feels, and one sentence taking ownership. | "That's frustrating, especially when you've already written twice. I'm going to sort this out." | Same, said slowly and calmly. |
| **E: Execute with a time** | A specific action and a specific time. If their wish is not possible, say so early and offer the best real option. | "I've refunded the duplicate charge. You'll see it in 5 to 10 business days. I'll email you the confirmation in the next 10 minutes." | Describe the action while you do it, then confirm. |
| **R: Record the root** | Tag the root cause and note what failed, so it does not happen again. | Root-cause tag, internal note, flag repeat issues. | Same, in the call log. |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: the "Tell it back" step does most of the work. When customers hear their own problem described correctly, their tone usually drops by half. Many agents skip it because it feels slow. It is the fastest step in the whole method.

</div>

## Phrases that help

| Purpose | Say |
| --- | --- |
| Show you understood | "Let me make sure I've got this right…" |
| Validate | "You're right to expect better than this." |
| Take ownership | "I'm going to take care of this for you." |
| Give control back | "Here's what I'm doing now, and here's when you'll hear from me." |
| Say no with a yes | "I can't do X, but I can do Y today." |
| Slow things down on the phone | "I want to get this right for you, so let me check one thing." |

## Phrases that make it worse

| Never say | Why |
| --- | --- |
| "Calm down." | Tells them their feelings are wrong |
| "As I already said…" | Sounds impatient |
| "That's our policy." (as the whole answer) | Feels like a wall |
| "You should have…" | Blames them |
| "There's nothing I can do." | Rarely true, always hopeless |
| "I understand how you feel." (alone) | Sounds scripted; name the actual feeling or problem instead |
| Jokes or sarcasm | Tone is easily misread, especially in writing |
| Long technical explanations | They want a fix, not a lecture |

## A full example (email)

<details class="toggle"><summary>See the example</summary>

**Customer:** "This is RIDICULOUS. Third email. You charged me twice for the same order and NOBODY answers. I want my money back NOW or I'm reporting you to my bank."

**STEER reply:**

"Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME),

You were charged twice for order (ORDER NUMBER) on 3 October, and you've written to us three times without a proper answer. I'm sorry. That's not the service you should get from us.

I've just refunded the duplicate charge of 84.90 USD to your original card. Banks usually show refunds within 5 to 10 business days. Your confirmation is attached.

I've also added a note to your account so this is clear to anyone on our team, and I've reported the double charge so we can stop it happening to others.

If you don't see the refund by (DATE), reply to this email and it comes straight to me.

(YOUR NAME)"

**What each line does:** Tell it back (charged twice, three emails), Empathize and own (I'm sorry, not the service you should get), Execute with a time (refunded now, 5 to 10 days, confirmation attached), Record the root (note, reported), plus a direct path back.

</details>

## When their wish is not possible

Say it early, say it once, and move straight to what you can do.

> "I can't refund an order that's outside the 30-day window. What I can do today is (BEST REAL OPTION). Would that help?"
> 

Never hide a "no" at the end of a long paragraph. Never let the customer hope for something you know will not happen.

## Abusive customers: one boundary

Customers may be angry. They may not abuse you. Abuse includes insults about you personally, slurs, threats and harassment.

1. **Acknowledge once and set a boundary:**

> "I want to help sort this out, and I can do that if we keep the conversation respectful."
> 
1. **If it continues, warn once more and explain what will happen:**

> "I'm not able to continue if the language continues. I'll end the chat now and our senior team will follow up by email."
> 
1. **End per the client's policy,** log it, and hand the ticket to Tier 2 or your lead.

**Threats of violence or self-harm are always P1.** Stay calm, do not argue, and follow the Escalation Ladder immediately. If the client has an emergency process, follow it.

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never argue back, match the customer's tone, or send a reply written while you are upset. If a message makes you angry, step away for two minutes and ask Tier 2 to review your draft. For P2 and above complaints, a second person always reviews before sending.

</div>

## When to hand over

Hand over to Tier 2 or your lead when:

- The customer is still upset after one full STEER reply or call
- The fix needs authority you do not have
- The customer asks for a manager (do not refuse; offer a senior team member and a time)
- It is the third contact about the same issue
- Abuse continues after a boundary

> "I'd like a senior member of our team to take this over so you get the fastest answer. They'll have everything we've discussed, and they'll contact you by (TIME AND ZONE)."
> 

## Look after yourself

Difficult conversations are tiring. After a hard one: take a short breath, stand up if you can, and tell your lead if it stays with you. Leads check in with agents who handled abuse. That is normal and expected at Narqix.
