For every support specialist who writes to customers. After reading you will be able to write replies that are clear, warm, complete and easy to read for customers in the US, UK and Australia, and to turn a weak reply into a strong one.

Track: Customer Support  ·  Role: Support Agent, CSR  ·  Time to complete: 30 min

## The Answer-First reply

Every Narqix reply follows five parts, in this order:

| Part | Purpose | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Personal line** | Prove you read their message | "A late birthday gift is the worst timing, and I want to fix this today." |
| **2. The answer** | The outcome they care about, in the first two sentences | "Your order arrives tomorrow, Thursday, by 8:00 PM." |
| **3. The details** | Only what they need; steps numbered | Tracking link, what to do if it does not arrive |
| **4. The next step** | Who does what, by when | "I will check tracking tomorrow at noon and email you if anything changes." |
| **5. Sign-off** | Human and simple | "Best, (YOUR NAME)" |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: imagine the customer reads only your first two sentences on their phone while walking. Would they know the outcome? If not, move the answer up.

</div>

## Before you write: the three-question check

1. **What did they actually ask?** List every question, including the ones in the last line or in a P.S.
2. **What do they really want?** "Where is my order?" often means "Will it arrive before Saturday?"
3. **What can I do?** Check policy, order data and your authority before you promise anything.

## Plain English rules

1. Short sentences. One idea each.
2. Short paragraphs: one to three sentences.
3. Numbered steps for instructions.
4. Everyday words: "use" not "utilize", "help" not "assist", "buy" not "purchase", "about" not "regarding".
5. Active voice: "I have refunded your order" not "Your order has been refunded".
6. Positive framing: say what you **can** do.
7. Contractions are fine and sound friendly ("I'll", "you're", "we've").

## Words and phrases to replace

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| "We apologize for the inconvenience" | "I'm sorry your order is late" (name the real problem) |
| "Unfortunately, we cannot…" | "Here's what I can do…" |
| "As per our policy" | "Our return window is 30 days" (state the fact) |
| "Kindly" | "Please" |
| "Do the needful" | "Please (specific action)" |
| "Please revert" or "revert back" | "Please reply" |
| "Valued customer" | Their first name |
| "You have to" or "You must" | "The next step is" or "Could you" |
| "Actually" | Delete it |
| "Please find attached" | "I've attached" or "Here's" |
| "Your good name" | "Your name" |
| "Same to you" (as a reply to thanks) | "You're welcome" |
| "Prepone" | "Move earlier" |
| "Dear Sir or Madam" | "Hi (FIRST NAME)" |

## Empathy that sounds real

Empathy should match the size of the problem. Too little sounds cold. Too much sounds fake.

| Situation | Too little | Right size | Too much |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Asks how to reset a password | (none needed) | "Happy to help with that." | "I'm so terribly sorry you're having this awful experience!" |
| Order 3 days late | "Your order is delayed." | "I'm sorry it's running late, especially when you were expecting it this week." | Three paragraphs of apology |
| Charged twice | "A refund has been processed." | "Being charged twice is stressful, and I've already fixed it." | "I cannot imagine how devastated you must be." |

## US, UK and AU writing

|  | US customers | UK and AU customers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spelling | color, organize, center, check | colour, organise, centre, cheque |
| Dates | Oct 18, 2026 or 10/18/2026 | 18 Oct 2026 or 18/10/2026 |
| Safe choice | Write the month as a word | Write the month as a word |
| Greeting | "Hi (NAME)" | "Hi (NAME)" (AU often more casual, UK often slightly more formal) |
| Sign-off | "Best," or "Thanks," | "Kind regards," or "Thanks," (AU: "Cheers," from the customer is normal; you can stay with "Thanks,") |

Follow the client's style guide when it differs.

## Before and after

<details class="toggle"><summary>Example 1: Where is my order</summary>

**Customer:** "Ordered on the 2nd, still nothing, and it's for my son's party on Saturday. Also can you add a gift note?"

**Weak reply:**

"Dear valued customer, we apologize for the inconvenience. As per our records your order has been shipped. Kindly check the tracking link. Regards."

Problems: no personal line, no answer about Saturday, gift note question ignored, stiff phrases.

**Strong reply:**

"Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME),

I know Saturday matters, so I checked right away: your order is with the carrier and due Friday by 8:00 PM, a day before the party. Here's the tracking link: (TRACKING LINK)

About the gift note: the parcel has already left our warehouse, so I can't add a printed note. I've emailed you a gift message card instead, ready to print or forward.

I'll check the tracking on Friday afternoon and email you if anything changes.

Best,

(YOUR NAME)"

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>Example 2: Saying no</summary>

**Customer:** "I want a refund, I bought this 50 days ago and don't like it."

**Weak reply:**

"Unfortunately we cannot refund as per our 30 day policy."

**Strong reply:**

"Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME),

Thanks for telling me the (PRODUCT) isn't right for you. Your order was delivered on (DATE), which is outside our 30-day return window, so I can't refund it to your card.

What I can do today is add (CREDIT AMOUNT) store credit to your account, which never expires. Just reply 'yes' and it'll be there within the hour.

Best,

(YOUR NAME)"

(Only offer credit if the client's Refund and Exception Matrix allows it.)

</details>

## Chat replies are shorter

Chat follows the same order, in smaller pieces:

> "Hi (NAME), thanks for waiting. Your order shipped yesterday."
"Here's the tracking: (LINK). It's due Thursday."
"Anything else I can check while I'm here?"
> 

One idea per message. No long sign-offs.

## The final check before sending

- [ ]  Every question answered
- [ ]  The answer is in the first two sentences
- [ ]  Names, order numbers, amounts and dates checked against the system
- [ ]  No placeholder or blank field left from a macro
- [ ]  The next step has an owner and a time with zone
- [ ]  Spelling style matches the customer's country
- [ ]  Read once more as the customer would

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never send an AI-drafted or macro reply without reading every line and personalizing it. Wrong order details or a blank field in a reply are QA failures and break customer trust.

</div>
