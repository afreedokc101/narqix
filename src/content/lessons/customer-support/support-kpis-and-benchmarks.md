## Why Narqix targets beat the market average

Clients compare us with their old support, with other outsourcing firms, and with what they read online. Industry surveys put the average first reply to a support email at around 12 hours. Narqix aims for under 2 hours in business hours. That gap is a big part of why clients choose us.

But numbers are only useful if they are honest. A fast reply that does not help, or a ticket closed too early, makes the number look good and the customer feel worse.

## The metrics

| KPI | What it measures | How it is calculated | Market reference | Narqix target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **First response time, email** | How long until a person first replies | Time from ticket creation to first public reply (internal notes and automatic replies do not count) | Around 12 hours on average; 4 hours is good; 1 hour is excellent | Under 2 hours in the client's business hours; under 8 hours outside them |
| **First response time, chat** | How long until an agent first speaks | Time from chat start to first agent message | About 40 to 45 seconds is typical | Under 30 seconds |
| **First response time, social** | Reply to public posts and messages | Time to first reply | 1 hour is excellent | Under 1 hour in business hours |
| **Phone service level** | How quickly calls are answered | Calls answered within 20 seconds divided by calls offered | 80% in 20 seconds is the long-standing standard | 80% in 20 seconds |
| **Abandon rate** | Callers who hang up before an answer | Abandoned calls divided by calls offered | Under 5 to 8% is common | Under 5% |
| **Full resolution time** | Total time to solve | Creation to final Solved, including reopens | Depends on business | Tier 1 median under 24 hours |
| **One-touch resolution** | Tickets solved in one reply | Tickets solved with one agent reply divided by tickets solved | Depends on business | 60% or more on Tier 1 topics |
| **Reopen rate** | Tickets not truly solved | Reopened tickets divided by solved tickets |  | Under 5% |
| **CSAT** | Customer satisfaction | Good ratings (4 or 5 out of 5) divided by all ratings | About 80% for chat on average, wide range by industry | 90% or more |
| **QA score** | Quality against the scorecard | Average QA Scorecard result | 85 to 90% is a common pass range | 88% or more |
| **Backlog age** | Old unsolved tickets | Open tickets older than 48 hours divided by all open tickets |  | Under 5% |
| **Average handle time** | Time spent per contact | Total handling time divided by contacts | Very different by business | Tracked only, until the client has a baseline |

## Read metrics in pairs

One number alone can mislead. Always read them together:

| Pair | What a problem looks like |
| --- | --- |
| First response time + CSAT | Fast replies but low CSAT: agents rushing or sending empty replies |
| Handle time + reopen rate | Low handle time but high reopens: tickets closed before they are solved |
| One-touch resolution + escalation rate | High one-touch but many escalations later: issues pushed away, not solved |
| Backlog age + first response time | Fast first replies but old backlog: easy tickets picked, hard ones left |

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never game a metric. Examples of gaming: sending "We're looking into it" just to stop the first response timer, setting Pending while we owe the next step, solving a ticket before the customer has their answer, cherry-picking easy tickets, or rushing callers to cut handle time. Gaming is a QA fail and a conduct issue.

</div>

## How to improve each number honestly

| To improve | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| First response time | Work oldest first within priority; use macros for common topics (always personalized); stagger breaks |
| One-touch resolution | Read the full history; answer every question; anticipate the next question ("You'll get a tracking email within 24 hours") |
| Reopen rate | Check that every question is answered; give clear next steps; confirm actions are actually done |
| CSAT | Personal first line; answer first; keep every promise; STEER for upset customers |
| Backlog age | Daily check of tickets older than 24 hours; ask for help early |
| QA score | Score yourself with the scorecard before sending |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: one-touch resolution is the metric that improves all the others. When you answer the next question before the customer asks it, there is no second message, no reopen, and a happier customer. Ask yourself after every reply: "What will they ask me next?" Then answer it now.

</div>

## Where your numbers appear

- **End of day report:** tickets handled, solved, escalated, and top contact reasons (see the Reporting Standard).
- **Weekly client report:** KPIs against target, top 5 contact reasons with suggested fixes, QA average.
- **Monthly review:** trends, CSAT comments, exception totals, improvement ideas.

## A worked example

<details class="toggle"><summary>See a week of numbers and what they mean</summary>

| KPI | This week | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First response time, email | 1 h 40 min | Under 2 h |
| CSAT | 84% | 90% |
| Reopen rate | 9% | Under 5% |
| One-touch resolution | 48% | 60% |

**Reading it:** first response is on target, but CSAT, reopens and one-touch are all weak. The pattern says replies are fast but incomplete. **Action:** QA reviews 10 reopened tickets to find the common miss. Result: agents were not answering the "when will my refund arrive?" follow up question. Fix: add the refund timing line to the refund macro and coach the team. Next week: reopens fall to 4%, CSAT rises to 91%.

</details>
