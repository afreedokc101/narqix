For senior Narqix support specialists who take over complaints, exceptions, billing disputes and complex problems. After reading you will know your authority, how to take over a ticket cleanly, how to build a case for the client, and how to stop the same problem coming back.

Track: Customer Support  ·  Role: Tier 2 and Escalations Specialist  ·  Time to complete: 25 min

## What this role is

Tier 2 handles the tickets that need judgment: the angry customer, the exception, the double charge, the bug, the chargeback. You are also the safety net for Tier 1 (reviewing their hardest replies) and the early warning system for the client (spotting patterns before they become crises).

## What you own

| Area | Your job |
| --- | --- |
| Escalated tickets | Full ownership from handover to resolution |
| Complaints | De-escalation using the STEER Method, resolution, follow through |
| Exceptions | Decisions within your matrix limit; clear recommendations above it |
| Billing disputes | Investigation, correction requests, honest timelines |
| Technical troubleshooting | Reproduce, document, workaround, report to the client's product contact |
| Chargebacks | Evidence pack prepared for the client; never argue with the customer |
| Incidents | Master ticket, linked tickets, update schedule during outages |
| Tier 1 support | Review emotional or high-risk replies before they are sent; answer Tier 1 questions |
| Root causes | Patterns reported weekly with suggested fixes |

## Taking over a ticket: the clean handover

When a ticket comes to you:

1. **Read the full history:** every message, every internal note, past tickets, order and payment records.
2. **Check the internal note from Tier 1:** what happened, what was checked, what was promised and by when.
3. **Contact the customer quickly,** introduce yourself, and show you already know the story:

> "Hi (CUSTOMER FIRST NAME), I am (YOUR NAME) from the senior support team. I have read everything you have shared with (TIER 1 AGENT NAME), so you do not need to repeat anything. Here is what I am doing next."
> 
1. **Keep the promise Tier 1 made,** or if you cannot, tell the customer before the promised time.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: the words "you do not need to repeat anything" calm more angry customers than any apology. Having to tell the same story twice is one of the most frustrating parts of support. Make sure they never have to.

</div>

## Your authority

Tier 2 authority comes from the client's **Refund and Exception Matrix**. A typical setup: refunds and credits up to a higher limit than Tier 1, one-time goodwill exceptions within written rules, and the right to approve Tier 1 requests within your own limit. Everything else goes to the client with a recommendation (see Refunds and Exceptions).

## Building a case for the client

When a decision is above your authority, send the client one complete message:

> **P2 escalation: (TICKET REFERENCE)**
**Customer:** (CUSTOMER NAME), customer since 2023, 14 orders, no previous refunds.
**Issue:** dining table delivered with a cracked leg. Photos in the ticket.
**Policy:** damage claims within 7 days get a refund or replacement. Reported on day 2.
**Ask:** full refund of 612 USD. Above my 250 USD limit.
**Options:** 1) full refund, 2) replacement (in stock, ships in 3 days), 3) 150 USD partial refund if they keep it.
**My recommendation:** option 2 if the customer agrees; option 1 if not. Low abuse risk.
**Promised update to customer:** today, 2:00 PM ET.
> 

## Bugs and outages

1. **Reproduce** the problem if you can. Note the exact steps, device, browser type and time.
2. **Workaround:** find a way for the customer to get the result now, if one exists.
3. **Report** to the client's product contact using the agreed path, with steps and examples.
4. **Outage mode:** open one master ticket, link every affected ticket to it, use one update macro, and send updates on a fixed schedule (for example every 2 hours) until resolved.

## Chargebacks

A chargeback is when a customer asks their bank to reverse a payment. You do not decide it, and you never argue with the customer about it.

1. Collect: order details, payment record, shipping and delivery proof, all messages with the customer, the policy they agreed to.
2. Put it into the client's evidence template.
3. Hand it to the client before the bank's deadline.
4. If the customer contacts support, stay polite and factual. Follow the client's instructions on whether to resolve directly.

## Escalation rules

- **To the client:** anything above your matrix, legal threats, press, safety, policy changes, product defects affecting many customers.
- **To your Narqix lead at once:** threats of harm, data incidents, suspected fraud rings, anything P1.
- **To Tier 1 as coaching:** patterns in the escalations you receive (for example the same question escalated five times means Tier 1 needs a macro or training).

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never promise an outcome you do not control ("I will make sure you get a full refund") before the client approves it. Promise the action you will take and when you will update them.

</div>

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never refund to a different card, account or person than the original payment, whatever the customer's reason. Requests to "refund to my new card" or "send it to my friend's account" are common fraud patterns.

</div>

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Re-asking the customer for details already in the ticket | Read the full history first |
| Escalating to the client with no recommendation | Options plus your recommendation, always |
| Fixing the ticket but not the cause | Root-cause tag and weekly pattern report |
| Defensive replies to angry customers | STEER Method; a second person reviews P2 and above complaints |
| Letting escalations sit overnight without an update | Hand over with a note, or update the customer before shift end |
