For every support specialist on live chat or phone. After reading you will be able to run a smooth chat or call, sound natural and professional to customers in the US, UK and Australia, and avoid the small habits that make customers lose confidence.

Track: Customer Support  ·  Role: Support Agent, CSR  ·  Time to complete: 30 min

</div>

## Part 1: Live chat

### The seven chat habits

1. **Greet fast and specifically.** Within 30 seconds, use their name and their topic: "Hi (NAME), thanks for reaching out about your order. Let me pull it up."
2. **Never go silent.** Before you check something, say how long it will take ("about 2 minutes"). Come back within that time, even if only to say you are still checking.
3. **Read the whole message, then reply once.** Do not reply to half a sentence while the customer is still typing.
4. **One idea per message.** Short messages. Use numbered steps or a link instead of a wall of text.
5. **Say "let me find out" instead of guessing.** A confident wrong answer is worse than a short wait.
6. **Hand over warmly.** If you transfer, pass on the name, order and everything tried. The customer should never repeat themselves.
7. **Close with the outcome.** Summarize what was done and what happens next, then ask: "Anything else I can help with while I'm here?"

### Holding messages

| Situation | Message |
| --- | --- |
| Checking something | "Thanks, (NAME). Give me about 2 minutes to check this with the warehouse. I'm right here." |
| Taking longer | "Still checking, thanks for your patience. About 2 more minutes." |
| Need to escalate | "This needs our senior team. I'll pass on everything we've discussed so you won't need to repeat it, and you'll get an email by (TIME AND ZONE)." |

### Chat concurrency

New agents handle **2 chats at a time**. After QA sign-off, 3 to 4. When a chat is complex or the customer is upset, finish it before taking a new one. Quality first.

### When the customer goes quiet

> After 3 minutes: "Are you still there, (NAME)? I'm here when you're ready."
After 5 more minutes: "I haven't heard back, so I'll close this chat for now. Everything we discussed is saved, and you can reply to the email copy anytime."
> 

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: in chat, customers judge your speed by the gaps between messages, not the total time. Three short messages over four minutes feel faster than one perfect message after four minutes of silence.

</div>

## Part 2: Phone

### The shape of every call

1. **Greet:** "Thank you for calling (COMPANY NAME), this is (YOUR NAME). How can I help today?"
2. **Disclose recording** if the client requires it.
3. **Listen and note.** Let them finish.
4. **Verify** identity with the client's method before discussing account details.
5. **Tell it back** and agree what you will solve.
6. **Resolve or route.** Holds and transfers done properly.
7. **Summarize** what was done and what happens next.
8. **Close:** "Is there anything else I can help with?" Thank them. Let them hang up first.
9. **Log** within 5 minutes.

### Holds

> "Could I place you on a short hold while I check with the warehouse? It should take about 2 minutes."
> 

Wait for a yes. Check back at least every 60 seconds:

> "Thank you for holding. I'm still checking. Are you okay to hold another minute?"
> 

### Transfers

Warm transfers only. Tell the caller who and why, then brief the receiver before connecting:

> **To the caller:** "I'm going to connect you with (NAME) on our billing team. They can approve this. I'll explain everything to them first, so you won't need to repeat it."
**To the receiver:** "I have (CALLER NAME) on the line about a double charge on order (ORDER NUMBER). Verified. They'd like the duplicate refunded today."
> 

### Clear speech for international callers

| Habit | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Speak a little slower than feels natural | Accents plus headsets reduce clarity |
| Say numbers in small groups: "four eight two, one three" | Easier to write down |
| Say teen numbers as digits: "one, five" for 15 | Prevents 15 and 50 confusion |
| Use a phonetic alphabet for spelling: "M as in Mike" | Letters like B, D, P and T sound alike on phones |
| Pause after key facts | Gives the caller time to write |
| Read back emails and addresses | Most callback failures are wrong emails |

### Phonetic alphabet (use the standard one)

Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India, Juliett, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango, Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee, Zulu.

## Part 3: US, UK and AU differences

The basics are the same everywhere: be clear, warm and honest. These differences help you sound natural.

|  | United States | United Kingdom | Australia |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Overall style** | Friendly, upbeat, direct. Speed is valued. | Polite, slightly more reserved. Understatement is common. | Relaxed, informal, direct. Dislikes stiff formality. |
| **Names** | First names right away | First names are normal; some older callers prefer "Mr" or "Mrs (SURNAME)". Follow their lead. | First names right away |
| **What "fine" may mean** | Usually fine | "It's a bit disappointing" can mean "I'm very unhappy". Take understatement seriously. | Usually honest and direct |
| **Things you will hear** | "Awesome", "Sure thing" | "Cheers" (thanks), "Brilliant", "Sorry?" (please repeat) | "No worries", "Cheers", "Reckon" (think) |
| **Spelling in writing** | US spelling | UK spelling | UK spelling |
| **Dates** | Month first (10/18/2026) | Day first (18/10/2026) | Day first (18/10/2026) |
| **Money** | USD | GBP (pounds and pence) | AUD (dollars and cents). Say "Australian dollars" if there could be confusion. |
| **Time zones** | ET, CT, MT, PT | GMT in winter, BST in summer | AEST and AEDT on the east coast; several zones across the country |

**Understand their phrases, but do not copy their slang.** You do not need to say "no worries" or "cheers" to an Australian customer. Clear, warm, standard English always works. Fake slang sounds forced.

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never put on a fake accent, claim to be in the customer's country, or use a name the client has not approved. Be honest, clear and professional. That is what builds trust.

</div>

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never use phrases that read oddly to US, UK and AU customers: "do the needful", "kindly revert", "your good name", "prepone", "same to you", "Dear Sir or Madam". See Writing Great Replies for replacements.

</div>
