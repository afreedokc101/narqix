## Why the lifecycle matters

A work order is not finished when the grass is cut. It is finished when the money is in the vendor's bank account and the payment is matched to the invoice. Between those two points there are many places where an order can stall, get rejected, get paid short, or simply be forgotten.

Most lost money in this industry is not lost in the field. It is lost on the desk: an update submitted late, a photo in the wrong slot, a bid without measurements, an invoice never sent. Know the 12 stages and you will always know where every order is and what it needs next.

## Before it reaches you: the trigger

Something upstream starts every order. You do not control it, but you should recognise it because it tells you how urgent the order is.

| Trigger | What happened | What it usually creates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First time vacant | An inspector confirmed the house is empty for the first time | Initial secure: locks, lockbox, photos, winterization, grass, debris check |
| Ownership transfer after foreclosure | The house became lender-owned | Initial services, cleaning, repairs to make it marketable |
| Recurring cycle | A monthly or seasonal service came due | Re-cut, monthly inspection, re-winterization check, snow removal |
| Damage found | An inspector or crew reported a problem | Bid request, repair order, roof tarp, board-up |
| Approved bid | The client approved a bid you sent | A new order to do the approved work |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: first time vacant orders carry the tightest investor clocks. The investor deadline is usually much longer than the vendor's due date because the preservation company keeps time for its own review and billing. Always work to the due date on the order, never to the investor deadline you may have read about.

</div>

## Stage by stage

### Stage 1: Assignment received

**What happens:** the preservation company issues a work order in its portal or app. It lists the address, the investor or loan type, the tasks, special instructions, the due date, and sometimes the price for each task.

**Side A desk actions:**

- Check every client portal at the start of shift and at least every two hours after that. Some campaigns ask for every hour.
- Bring each new order into the vendor's work order platform (automatic import or manual entry).
- Log the time the order was received.

**Side B desk actions:**

- Create the order, or confirm it arrived from the servicer correctly.
- Choose the vendor by coverage area, service type, score and current workload.

**Output:** every new order is visible in one tracker within the check cycle.

### Stage 2: Intake check

Read the order before anyone drives to the house. This five-minute check prevents most wasted trips.

- [ ]  Address complete and inside the vendor's coverage area (county and ZIP)
- [ ]  Not a duplicate of an open order for the same address and task
- [ ]  Investor or loan type noted, because the rules change with it
- [ ]  Due date realistic for the tasks and travel distance
- [ ]  Key code, lockbox code or access instructions present when needed
- [ ]  Special instructions read in full (for example "do not enter", "occupied", "contact broker first")
- [ ]  Price per task present, or the price list for this client is known
- [ ]  Any task the vendor cannot do (no licence, no equipment) flagged

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never accept an order you have not read to the last line. Special instructions often sit at the very bottom, and they override the standard checklist.

</div>

### Stage 3: Accept and commit

**What happens:** the vendor accepts or declines inside the client's window. Many portals also ask for a commit date, which is the vendor's promise of when the work will be complete.

- Accept only what the vendor can deliver on time.
- Set a commit date you can actually meet. A missed commit date hurts the score more than an honest later date.
- If you must decline, decline quickly and give a clear reason (outside coverage, no qualified crew). A fast decline lets the company reassign without losing time.

**Side B:** confirm that the vendor accepted. If an order sits unaccepted past the window, call or message the vendor, then reassign by the company's rule.

### Stage 4: Dispatch and crew packet

**What happens:** the order is assigned to a field crew, and the crew receives everything needed to do it right the first time.

**The crew packet contains:**

- Address and map pin
- Tasks in plain words
- Access: key code, lockbox code, gate code, contact person
- Photo checklist for each task (from the Order Types Guide)
- Measurements to take (lot size, cubic yards, opening sizes, roof area)
- Special instructions copied word for word
- Due date and the time the desk needs photos back

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: crews read on a phone, in a truck, in the sun. Keep the packet short. Put the three things most likely to cause a rejection at the top in capital letters, for example "YARDSTICK IN EVERY BEFORE PHOTO".

</div>

### Stage 5: Field visit support

**What happens:** the crew is at the property. Things go wrong in real time: the house is occupied, the key does not work, a dog is in the yard, the damage is bigger than the order.

**Desk actions:**

- Stay reachable on the campaign's team channel during field hours.
- Answer crew questions within minutes, not hours.
- When the scope is wrong or the house is occupied, tell the crew to stop, take proof photos, and wait. Then contact the client's coordinator.
- Record every change in the order notes with the time.

| Situation in the field | What the crew does | What the desk does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| House looks occupied (cars, people, fresh food, lights) | Do not enter. Photograph signs of occupancy from outside. | Report to the client as possibly occupied. Ask for instructions. |
| Cannot get in (gate locked, key fails) | Photograph the obstacle and the address | Report no access. Ask whether a trip charge applies. |
| More debris or damage than ordered | Photograph and measure. Do not do extra work without approval. | Prepare a bid. Check whether the client allows "complete then bid" for small amounts. |
| Safety hazard (exposed wires, gas smell, animals) | Leave the area. Call emergency services if needed. | Report the hazard to the client the same hour. |

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never tell a crew to enter a house that may be occupied, and never tell a crew to ignore a safety hazard to meet a due date. A due date can be extended. A person cannot be un-hurt.

</div>

### Stage 6: Photo intake

**What happens:** photos arrive from the crew's app, a shared drive, or a team channel.

- Download every photo into a folder named with the order number.
- Remove blurry, dark, sideways and accidental photos.
- Check that the date stamp matches the visit date.
- Match photos to tasks.
- If anything is missing, message the crew immediately, while they may still be near the property.

### Stage 7: Internal QC

**What happens:** before anything leaves the desk, a second set of eyes checks the order against the Photo QC Standard and scorecard.

- Before, during and after from the same angle for every task
- Address verification photos present
- Proof items visible (yardstick, tape measure, antifreeze, tags, gauge reading)
- Answers to questions match what the photos show
- No duplicates, no reused photos

**Output:** the order is marked "QC passed" with the checker's initials, or sent back with a reason.

### Stage 8: Update and submit

**What happens:** the Processor completes the update in the client's portal.

- Answer every required question (occupancy, utilities, debris, damage, violations, code notices).
- Mark each task completed, or not completed with a clear reason.
- Place each photo in the right slot. Most portals check that a slot has a photo but not that it is the right photo. That gap is where most rejections come from.
- Write short, factual comments.
- Submit, then take a screenshot of the confirmation and save it in the order folder.
- Update the tracker status and time.

**Side B:** the vendor submits. You review the submission against the same standard before it goes to the servicer.

### Stage 9: Bids

**What happens:** any work outside the order, or above the allowable price, needs a bid.

- Use the crew's measurements and photos.
- Write each line with location, size, cause, scope, materials, price and reason.
- Flag items that may need a second bid or prior approval.
- Submit with the update or as the client requires.
- Log the bid in the bid tracker and follow up until it is approved, denied or changed.

Full detail is in Bids and Estimating.

### Stage 10: Client review and corrections

**What happens:** the preservation company reviews the update. It approves, or returns it as a rejection, kickback, reopen or "pending vendor".

- Check the rejection queue at least three times per shift.
- Read the reason in full. Fix the actual problem, not only the words in the note.
- If photos are missing and the crew must go back, arrange it the same day.
- Resubmit within the same shift whenever possible.
- Log the rejection reason code in the tracker. Patterns in rejections are how the desk improves.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: a rejection is a gift of information. Every one tells you which rule the reviewer cares about. Keep a short "reviewer preferences" note per client and read it before each shift.

</div>

### Stage 11: Invoice

**What happens:** approved work is invoiced, usually inside the same portal.

- Invoice only completed and approved tasks and approved bids.
- Match every line to the price list or bid approval.
- Match quantities to the photos (cubic yards, united inches, square feet).
- Attach receipts where needed (dump fees, materials).
- Target: invoice every approved order within one business day of approval (Narqix starting target).

### Stage 12: Payment and reconciliation

**What happens:** the preservation company pays on its cycle, often weekly or every two weeks, and sometimes with deductions.

- Match each payment to its invoices by payment reference or check number.
- Log short pays and deductions with a reason code.
- File disputes through the client's process, with proof, inside its time limit.
- Report the aging of unpaid invoices every week.

**Side B:** the same stage runs in reverse. You pay vendors after the company is satisfied with the work, and you answer vendor questions about deductions.

## After stage 12: recurring work

Many properties come back again and again: monthly inspections, grass re-cuts every two to three weeks in season, snow events, re-winterization checks. Keep a recurring calendar per property so the next order is expected, not a surprise. Some clients expect the vendor to keep a recurring grass cut list even when no order is open.

## Status names to use in the tracker

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| New | Received, not yet checked |
| Accepted | Intake check done, order accepted, commit date set |
| Dispatched | Crew packet sent |
| In field | Crew on site or scheduled for today |
| Photos received | Photos in the order folder |
| QC hold | Missing or failed items, waiting on crew |
| QC passed | Ready to submit |
| Submitted | Update sent to the client, screenshot saved |
| Rejected | Returned by the client, fix in progress |
| Approved | Client accepted the update |
| Invoiced | Invoice submitted |
| Paid | Payment received and matched |
| Short paid | Payment received with a deduction, dispute open or closed |

## Common lifecycle mistakes

| Mistake | What it costs | Prevention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orders left in "New" overnight | Late acceptance, lost orders, lower score | Portal checks every two hours, end-of-shift sweep |
| Commit date set without checking crew schedule | Missed promise | Confirm crew capacity before committing |
| Crew sent without the photo checklist | Missing photos, second trip | Packet template with checklist built in |
| Update submitted without screenshot | No proof of on-time submission | Screenshot is part of the submit step |
| Rejections checked once a day | Orders go late while waiting | Three checks per shift minimum |
| Approved orders not invoiced | Money never collected | Daily "approved but not invoiced" report |
| Payments not matched | Short pays go unnoticed | Weekly reconciliation by reference number |

## Check yourself

<details class="toggle"><summary>1. An order arrives with a due date of tomorrow and a note at the bottom: &quot;Property may be occupied. Exterior photos only.&quot; What do you do at intake?</summary>

Read and copy the instruction into the crew packet at the top in capital letters. Make sure the crew knows not to enter. Accept only if the crew can reach the property by tomorrow.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>2. Which stage protects the vendor from paying for a second trip to the property?</summary>

Stage 6 (photo intake) and Stage 7 (internal QC), because missing photos are caught while the crew is still nearby. Stage 4 also helps, because a good crew packet prevents missing photos in the first place.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>3. A portal accepted your update with a photo in every slot. Why can it still be rejected?</summary>

The portal only checks that each slot has a photo, not that it is the right photo. A human reviewer checks the content, angle, date and proof items later.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>4. What is the Narqix starting target for invoicing approved work?</summary>

Within one business day of approval.

</details>
