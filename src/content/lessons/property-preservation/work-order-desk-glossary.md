## How to use this glossary

Terms are grouped by topic. Use your browser's search to find a word fast. Some short forms mean different things to different clients. When a client uses a term differently, the client's meaning wins: add it to your campaign cheat sheet.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: when you see a new term in an order or rejection, do not guess. Look it up here, then ask your Team Lead if it is not listed. A wrong guess about a term like "personal property" can cost far more than a two-minute question.

## 1. The industry and its players

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Property preservation | Securing, maintaining and protecting empty homes tied to defaulted or lender-owned loans |
| Field services | The wider name for preservation, inspections and related work done at properties |
| Investor | The owner of the loan or its risk. Writes the rules, price limits and deadlines |
| Insurer | A government program or company that insures the loan and sets its own preservation rules |
| GSE | Government-sponsored enterprise. A large investor that buys and guarantees home loans and publishes preservation guidelines |
| FHA | Federal Housing Administration. A US government program that insures home loans. FHA loans follow their own preservation and conveyance rules |
| HUD | The US Department of Housing and Urban Development, which runs FHA. FHA properties are often conveyed (handed over) to HUD after foreclosure |
| VA and USDA | Other US government loan programs with their own rules |
| Private investor or trust | A non-government owner of loans with its own guideline |
| Servicer | The company that manages the loan for the investor and must protect empty properties |
| National preservation company | A large company that receives orders from servicers and sends them to local vendors across many states |
| Regional company | A smaller preservation company covering a few states, sometimes working under a national company |
| Vendor | A local contractor company that does preservation work |
| Field crew | The people who visit properties and do the work |
| Coordinator | The client's staff member who manages orders in an area and answers vendor questions |
| Back office | The team that processes orders, photos, bids and invoices. The Narqix Work Order Desk is a back office |

## 2. Property and loan status

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Default | The borrower stopped paying the loan as agreed |
| Foreclosure | The legal process where the lender takes the property back |
| Deed in lieu | The borrower gives the property to the lender to avoid foreclosure |
| REO | Real estate owned. A property now owned by the lender or investor after foreclosure |
| Pre-sale or pre-foreclosure | A property still owned by the borrower but in default; access may be limited |
| FTV | First time vacant. The date a property is first confirmed empty. Often starts investor deadlines |
| Occupied | Someone lives in the property. Crews must not enter without instructions |
| Vacant | Nobody lives there, confirmed by evidence |
| Abandoned | Vacant and left in poor condition, often with personal property inside |
| Conveyance | Handing over a property to the insurer (for FHA loans, to HUD) in exchange for the insurance claim |
| Conveyance condition | The required state of a property before handover: secured, winterized if needed, clean, lawn maintained, no unrepaired damage |
| Reconveyance | The insurer sends a property back because it did not meet conveyance condition |
| Mortgagee neglect | Damage caused by failure to preserve the property. Usually not reimbursed |
| Code violation | A local government notice that the property breaks a local rule (tall grass, trash, unsafe structure) |

## 3. Orders and workflow

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Work order (WO) | The instruction for one property: tasks, due date, special notes |
| Order number | The client's unique number for a work order |
| Client's portal | The client's website or app where orders arrive and updates are submitted |
| Work order platform | The vendor's own system that gathers orders from several clients in one place |
| Assignment | The client sends an order to a vendor |
| Acceptance | The vendor agrees to do the order |
| Commit date | The vendor's promised completion date |
| Due date | The date by which the update must be submitted |
| Dispatch | Sending an order to a specific field crew |
| Crew packet | The instructions, access codes and photo checklist sent to a crew |
| Update | The completed report: answers, photos, comments and bids |
| Completion | The order is fully done and submitted |
| Not completed | A task could not be done; must be reported with a reason and proof |
| Extension | A new, later due date granted by the client before the original date passes |
| Recurring order | A service repeated on a cycle (re-cuts, monthly inspections) |
| Grass cut list | A list of properties due for recurring cuts, kept by the vendor |
| Trip charge | A fee for a visit where the work could not be done (no access, occupied) |
| Special instructions | Notes on the order that override the standard checklist |

## 4. Securing

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Initial secure (initial services) | The first full visit after vacancy: locks, lockbox, photos, securing, winterization, grass, damage and debris check |
| Lock change | Replacing a lock with a new one |
| Rekey | Changing the pins inside an existing lock so a new key works |
| Key code | The client's standard key pattern so agents and contractors can enter |
| Lockbox | A combination box holding the property key |
| Padlock | A separate lock used on gates, sheds or hasps |
| Hasp | A metal plate and loop used with a padlock to secure a door or gate |
| Board-up | Covering a broken opening with plywood |
| Clear-board | Covering a broken opening with a clear polycarbonate panel |
| Opening | Any window, door or gap that could allow entry |
| Pool securing | Covering, treating and fencing a pool to prevent accidents |
| Sump pump | A basement pump that removes groundwater |

## 5. Winter work

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Winterization | Draining and protecting plumbing so it does not freeze |
| Dry winterization | Winterization of a normal water system with no boiler heating |
| Wet or steam winterization | Winterization that includes a boiler and hot water or steam heating |
| Radiant winterization | Winterization of radiant or in-floor heating, using glycol in the heating loop |
| Re-winterization | Repeating a winterization that was broken |
| Dewinterization | Reversing a winterization so the plumbing works again |
| Pressure test | An air test with a gauge to check pipes for leaks; gauge photos before and after |
| Antifreeze | Non-toxic plumbing antifreeze poured into every trap, visible by its colour in photos |
| Trap | The curved pipe under a drain that holds water; must get antifreeze |
| Winterization tag | A sticker on fixtures and panels warning not to turn on water |
| Freeze damage | Burst pipes or fixtures caused by freezing |

## 6. Yard and cleaning

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Initial cut | The first grass cut at a property, often of long grass |
| Re-cut | A repeat grass cut on the client's cycle |
| Yardstick photo | A photo with a measuring stick in the grass or snow to prove height or depth |
| Edging | Trimming grass along walks, driveways and borders |
| Shrub trim | Cutting bushes back from the house, windows and walks |
| Snow removal | Clearing snow and ice from walks, steps and driveways |
| Maid service | Cleaning a property, often to "broom swept" standard |
| Sales clean | A deeper clean to "marketable condition" for listing |
| Broom swept | Floors swept and loose debris removed |

## 7. Debris and personal property

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Debris | Trash and items with no value |
| Personal property | Items that may have value to the former occupant. Handled by client rules and state law |
| Trash-out (clean-out) | Removing debris (and personal property when instructed) from a property |
| Hazardous materials | Paint, chemicals, fuel, batteries, tyres and similar items needing special disposal |
| Disposal receipt (dump receipt) | Proof of where and how much debris was disposed |
| Load | One trip of a trailer or truck to the disposal site, numbered in photos |
| Inventory | A list and photos of personal property found |

## 8. Measurements

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| CYD (cubic yard) | Volume unit for debris: 27 cubic feet (3 ft x 3 ft x 3 ft) |
| UI (united inches) | Width plus height of an opening in inches, used for boarding |
| Square feet (sq ft) | Area: length times width |
| Square (roofing) | 100 square feet of roof |
| Linear feet (LF) | Length only, used for fences, gutters, rails |
| Lot size | The area of the land, in square feet or acres |
| Acre | 43,560 square feet |

## 9. Bids and money

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Bid | A priced request for work not on the order or above the price limit |
| Allowable | The maximum amount the investor will reimburse for a task |
| Over-allowable | An amount above the allowable that needs approval |
| Price list (price matrix) | The client's list of prices it pays the vendor per task |
| Line item | One task on a bid or invoice |
| Second bid | An independent bid from another contractor, required for some items |
| Complete and bid after | Client rule allowing small overages to be done first and bid with proof after |
| Bid first | Client rule requiring approval before larger or riskier work |
| Approval | The client agrees to a bid, in full or part |
| Denial | The client refuses a bid |
| Counter-offer | The client approves a bid at a lower price or quantity |
| Invoice | The bill for approved, completed work |
| Remittance | The payment details showing which invoices were paid |
| Short pay | A payment smaller than the invoice |
| Deduction | An amount taken off a payment, for example for lateness |
| Chargeback | An amount taken back after payment, often weeks or months later |
| Dispute | A formal request to reverse a deduction, with proof |
| Aging report | A list of unpaid invoices grouped by how long they have been unpaid |
| Accounts receivable (AR) | Money owed to the vendor that has not yet been paid |

## 10. Quality and review

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| QC | Quality control. Checking work against the standard before or after submission |
| Before, during, after | The three photo stages that prove each task |
| Same angle | Before and after photos taken from the same spot and direction |
| Address verification | Photos of the house front, house number and street sign |
| Elevation | One side of the house (front, left, right, rear) |
| Media slot (photo slot) | The place in the portal where a specific photo must be placed |
| Date stamp | The date and time printed on or stored in a photo |
| Geotag | Location data stored in a photo |
| Rejection (kickback) | The client returns an update for correction |
| Reopen | The client reopens an approved order because a problem was found later |
| Pending vendor | The order is waiting for the vendor to supply something |
| First-pass approval | An update approved the first time, with no rejection |
| Scorecard | The client's grade of a vendor's quality and timeliness. Higher grades usually mean more orders |
| Discoloration | Industry word for suspected mould, used because mould is not confirmed without testing |
| Property condition report (PCR) | A structured form describing the condition of a property |

## 11. Vendor compliance

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Onboarding | Collecting and checking documents before a vendor can receive orders |
| COI (certificate of insurance) | A document from the insurer showing the vendor's coverage, limits and dates |
| General liability | Insurance for damage or injury caused by the vendor's work |
| Workers' compensation | Insurance for injuries to the vendor's workers |
| Errors and omissions (E and O) | Insurance for professional mistakes, such as reporting errors |
| Additional insured | The client named on the vendor's policy so it is also protected |
| Named insured | The company the policy covers; must match the vendor's legal name |
| Background screening | A check on the vendor's people before they enter properties |
| Coverage area | The counties and ZIP codes a vendor serves |
| Hold | A status that stops new orders to a vendor until an issue is fixed |
| Recertification | Periodic renewal of a vendor's approval and documents |

## 12. Other short forms you may see

| Short form | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| BPO | Broker price opinion: a property value estimate by a real estate agent. Not "business process outsourcing" in this industry |
| ETA | Estimated time of arrival |
| N/A | Not applicable |
| W/D | Window or door, depending on the client; confirm |
| B/A | Before and after |
| B/D/A | Before, during and after |
| ICC | Used differently by clients (for example conveyance-related repairs, or "initial cut and clean"). Always confirm the client's meaning |

## Check yourself

<details class="toggle"><summary>1. What is the difference between debris and personal property?</summary>

Debris is trash with no value. Personal property may have value to the former occupant and must be handled by the client's rules and state law.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>2. What does &quot;over-allowable&quot; mean?</summary>

An amount above the maximum the investor will reimburse for a task. It needs approval.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>3. A client note says &quot;BPO needed&quot;. Does this mean outsourcing?</summary>

No. In this industry BPO usually means broker price opinion, a value estimate by a real estate agent.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>4. What is a short pay, and how is it different from a chargeback?</summary>

A short pay is a payment smaller than the invoice. A chargeback is money taken back after a payment was already made.

</details>
