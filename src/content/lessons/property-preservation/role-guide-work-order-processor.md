Track: Property Preservation  ·  Role: Work Order Processor  ·  Time to complete: 30 min

## What you own

You are the heartbeat of the vendor's back office. Every order passes through your hands at least twice: when it arrives and when it goes back to the client. You own:

- **Order intake:** pulling new orders from every client portal into the vendor's work order platform and tracker
- **Order review:** the intake check on every order before it is accepted
- **Acceptance and commit dates:** accepting inside the client's window with realistic dates
- **Crew packets:** sending field crews everything they need
- **Field support:** answering crew questions during field hours
- **Updates:** completing and submitting updates in the client's portal
- **Rejections:** working the rejection queue until it is empty
- **Status accuracy:** every order in the tracker shows its true status at all times

You do not own photo QC sign-off (the Photo QC Specialist does), bid writing (the Bid Writer does), or invoicing (the Invoice and AR Assistant does). You do make sure each of them receives clean, complete work on time.

## Your handoffs

| You receive from | What | You pass to | What |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Client portals | New orders, rejections, client messages | Field crews or vendor Dispatcher | Crew packet, access info, photo checklist |
| Field crews | Photos, notes, measurements, problems | Photo QC Specialist | Photo folder ready for QC |
| Photo QC Specialist | QC passed or fix list | Bid Writer | Crew notes and damage photos for bids |
| Bid Writer | Finished bids to attach | Invoice and AR Assistant | Approved orders ready to invoice |
| Team Lead | Priorities, client rules, extensions | Team Lead | Escalations, end-of-shift report |

## Tools you use (generic)

- Each client's portal (one login per person, never shared)
- The vendor's work order platform
- The campaign tracker (shared sheet or platform view)
- Shared drive with one folder per order
- Team channel for crews and the desk
- Email and, when the campaign allows, phone

## Daily rhythm

Your campaign sets your actual shift. This is the shape of a typical Narqix processor shift.

| Time in shift | Focus | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 15 min | Check in by your campaign's method. Read handover notes and the client rules sheet. | You know every open issue from the last shift |
| 15 to 60 min | Sweep every client portal for new orders and rejections. Intake check and accept. | Zero unaccepted orders older than the check cycle |
| Hour 1 to 2 | Send crew packets for today and tomorrow. Confirm crews received them. | Every dispatched order has a confirmed crew |
| Hours 2 to 6 | Updates from photos that passed QC. Field support on the team channel. Rejection queue check 1. | Due-today orders submitted first |
| Every 2 hours | Portal sweep for new orders and rejections | Logged in tracker |
| Mid shift | Rejection queue check 2. Due tomorrow review. | Every at-risk order has a plan |
| Hours 6 to 8 | Remaining updates. Rejection queue check 3. Extension requests for anything at risk. | No order goes late without a documented extension request |
| Last 20 min | Tracker cleanup. Handover note. End-of-shift report to Team Lead. | Next shift can start without asking you anything |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: work the queue by due date, not by order of arrival. A simple re-cut due today beats a big initial secure due in three days. Sort your tracker by due date every time you open it.

</div>

## KPIs

These are Narqix starting targets. Your campaign may adjust them after the first month with the client.

| KPI | Narqix starting target | How it is measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New orders accepted inside client window | 100 percent | Accept time minus receive time |
| Simple updates per shift (re-cut, inspection) | 40 to 80 | Tracker count |
| Complex updates per shift (initial secure, trash-out with bids) | 10 to 25 | Tracker count |
| Photos received to update submitted | Same US business day; within 4 hours for due-today orders | Tracker timestamps |
| On-time update rate | 98 percent or more | Submitted before due date |
| First-pass approval rate | 95 percent or more (aim for 98) | Approved without rejection |
| Rejections fixed and resubmitted | Same shift | Rejection log |
| Tracker accuracy (spot check) | 100 percent | Team Lead audit |

## Writing a clean update

1. Open the order instructions next to the portal update screen.
2. Answer every required question. Use the photos to answer, not memory or the crew's text alone.
3. Mark each task completed, or not completed with a reason.
4. Place photos into each required slot in the right order: address set first, then each task before, during, after.
5. Write comments in short factual sentences.
6. Read the whole update once more from top to bottom.
7. Submit. Screenshot the confirmation. Save it to the order folder.
8. Update the tracker status and time.

**Good comment examples:**

> "Re-cut completed front, sides and rear. Edged walkway and driveway. Clippings removed. Lot approx 7,200 sq ft. No new debris. No violations posted."
> 

> "Lock change completed on rear door to client key code. Lockbox installed on rear door handle, code entered per order. Old lockset left in kitchen drawer."
> 

> "Initial secure partially completed. Front door glass broken, clear-board bid attached. Basement has standing water approx 2 in, sump pump not working (power off). Bid for pump test after utilities on attached."
> 

## Quality checklist (before every submit)

- [ ]  Order number and address match the photos
- [ ]  Every ordered task is marked completed or not completed with a reason
- [ ]  Every required question answered and consistent with photos
- [ ]  Photo QC marked passed by the QC Specialist
- [ ]  Photos in the correct slots
- [ ]  Bids attached where damage or overage exists
- [ ]  Comments factual, no guesses, no opinions
- [ ]  Completion date inside the due date, or extension documented
- [ ]  Screenshot saved after submit
- [ ]  Tracker updated

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Accepting orders outside coverage | Check county and ZIP against the coverage list at intake |
| Copying the crew's text straight into comments | Rewrite into clear, factual sentences that match the photos |
| Submitting before QC passed | QC sign-off is required on every order, every time |
| Forgetting the "not completed" reason | Every task must have a status |
| Asking for an extension after the due date | Ask before, with a reason and a new date |
| Working rejections once a day | Three planned checks per shift |
| Using another person's portal login | Use only your own credentials |

## Escalation rules

| Situation | Escalate to | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order at risk of missing due date | Team Lead, then client coordinator for extension | As soon as you know, never after the due date |
| House possibly occupied or hostile occupant | Team Lead and client | Same hour |
| Safety hazard reported by crew | Team Lead and client | Immediately |
| Crew asks you to reuse or change photos | Team Lead | Same day, in writing |
| Client instruction conflicts with the guideline | Team Lead | Before acting |
| Portal down or login locked | Team Lead and campaign support contact | Within 15 minutes |
| Same rejection reason three times in a week | Team Lead and QC Specialist | End of shift report |

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never mark a task complete to "save" a due date, never submit photos that did not pass QC, and never share your portal login with anyone, including your teammates.

</div>

## Check yourself

<details class="toggle"><summary>1. You have three orders: an initial secure due in 3 days, a re-cut due today, and an inspection due tomorrow. Which do you update first?</summary>

The re-cut due today, then the inspection due tomorrow, then the initial secure. Work by due date.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>2. The crew texted &quot;all done&quot; but the photos show the rear yard not cut. What do you write in the update?</summary>

Mark the rear yard not completed with the reason (from crew or photos), or hold the update and ask the crew to return before the due date. Never mark it complete.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>3. When should you request an extension?</summary>

As soon as you know the due date is at risk, always before the due date, with a clear reason and a realistic new date.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>4. What do you save after every submit?</summary>

A screenshot of the submission confirmation, in the order folder.

</details>
