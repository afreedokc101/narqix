## A to C

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **ACH** | The US electronic system that moves money between bank accounts. Most advance payments are ACH debits. |
| **ACH debit** | A pull of money from an account by a company the owner authorised, such as a funder or a lender. |
| **Accounts receivable (AR)** | Money customers owe the business for work already done or goods delivered. |
| **AR aging report** | A list of unpaid invoices grouped by how long they have been unpaid (for example 0 to 30 days, 31 to 60 days). |
| **Advance** | Short for merchant cash advance. Money given now in exchange for a share of future sales. |
| **Advance proceeds** | The money a funder deposits into the merchant's account. Never counted as revenue. |
| **Affiliate** | Another business owned by the same owners. Some programs need its financials too. |
| **After-repair value (ARV)** | What a property should be worth after renovation. Used in Fix and Flip. |
| **APR** | Annual percentage rate. A yearly cost measure required in some state disclosures. You never quote it. |
| **Application** | The signed form with business and owner details and permission to share the file with funders. |
| **Auto-stop flag** | A fact that stops a file until the desk lead reviews it: open bankruptcy, active tax lien, recent judgment. |
| **Average daily balance (ADB)** | The average of each day's ending balance in a period. Shows how much cash usually sits in the account. |
| **Balance sheet** | A report of what a business owns, what it owes, and the difference (equity) on a given date. |
| **Bank letter** | A letter from the bank confirming the account and routing numbers. Used when there is no voided check. |
| **Bank-original PDF** | A statement downloaded directly from the bank's website or app, not a photo, scan or edited file. |
| **Bank-verified connection** | A secure, read-only link that pulls statements directly from the bank. |
| **Bankruptcy (open or discharged)** | A legal process for people or businesses that cannot pay debts. Open means still in progress; discharged means finished. |
| **Bridge loan** | A short-term first-lien loan that covers a gap in time until longer financing or a sale. Not a gap loan. |
| **Broker** | A company that connects merchants with funders and is usually paid by the funder when a deal funds. |
| **Business debt schedule** | A list of every business debt: lender, balance, payment, and end date. |
| **Buy rate** | The funder's price to the broker before any broker compensation. Internal only; never discussed with merchants. |
| **Card processing statement** | A monthly report from the company that processes the business's card sales. |
| **Cash-out refinance** | A new real estate loan larger than the old one, giving the owner cash from their equity. |
| **Chargeback** | A card payment reversed after a customer dispute. |
| **Closer** | The person (or founder) who presents offers to merchants. VAs do not present offers. |
| **Collateral** | An asset that secures a loan, such as equipment or property. |
| **Commercial financing disclosure** | A form some states require funders to give merchants before signing, showing cost and terms. |
| **Comparable sales (comps)** | Recent sales of similar nearby properties, used to support value. |
| **Concentration** | When one customer pays a large share of a business's revenue. A risk flag. |
| **Confession of judgment** | A contract clause where the borrower agrees in advance to a court judgment if they default. Banned in several states. |
| **Consent (prior express written consent)** | A person's signed or digital agreement to receive automated calls or marketing texts. |
| **Consolidation** | Paying off several advances or debts with one new funding. |
| **Contingency** | Extra money in a construction or rehab budget for surprises. |
| **Credit band** | A score range, such as 620 to 659. We record the band the merchant reports. |
| **Credit repair** | A service that disputes inaccurate, outdated or unverifiable credit items. |

## D to L

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **DBA** | "Doing business as". The name customers see, when it differs from the legal name. |
| **Debt-to-income (DTI)** | Monthly debt payments divided by monthly income. Used in residential mortgages. |
| **Decline reason code** | The standard reason recorded when a file is declined. |
| **Default** | Failing to meet the terms of a funding contract, such as missing payments. Escalate any mention of it. |
| **Desk lead** | The person who approves funder matches and file decisions on the funding desk. |
| **Disposition** | The status you set on a call or lead after contact, such as "qualified" or "callback". |
| **Do-not-call list (DNC)** | National and internal lists of numbers that must not receive telemarketing calls. |
| **Draw** | Money released in stages as construction or rehab work is completed. |
| **DSCR** | Debt service coverage ratio. Income from a property divided by its debt payments. 1.20 means 20% more income than payment. |
| **EIN** | Employer identification number. A US business's tax ID. |
| **Entity documents** | Papers that show a business legally exists: articles of organization or incorporation, operating agreement. |
| **Equity injection** | Cash the buyer puts into a business purchase or project from their own funds. |
| **Factor rate** | A multiplier that shows total payback on an advance (for example, 1.3 means 130 repaid for each 100 received). You never quote it. |
| **Factoring** | Selling unpaid invoices to a company that pays most of the value now. |
| **Funded** | The merchant received the money. |
| **Funder** | The company that provides an advance or other non-loan funding. |
| **Funder matrix** | The internal list of funders and what each one accepts: credit, time in business, revenue, positions, industries, states. |
| **Gap loan** | A second or third lien loan placed behind an existing loan. Narqix does not place these. |
| **General contractor (GC)** | The licensed company that manages a construction project. |
| **Gross deposits** | Every deposit in the account in a period, before removing non-revenue items. |
| **Ground up** | New construction starting from land. |
| **Hard inquiry (hard pull)** | A credit check that can lower the score and that other lenders can see. |
| **Holdback** | The share of sales, or fixed payment, that goes to a funder. |
| **Holdback burden** | Monthly advance payments divided by monthly true revenue. |
| **Interest reserve** | Money set aside inside a construction loan to cover interest payments during the build. |
| **ISO** | Independent sales organization. Another name for a funding broker. |
| **Lender** | A company that makes loans (as opposed to buying future sales). |
| **Letter of intent (LOI)** | A signed early agreement on the main terms of a business purchase. |
| **Lien** | A legal claim on an asset until a debt is paid. |
| **Line of credit (LOC)** | A revolving limit a business can draw from, repay and draw again. |
| **LTC** | Loan-to-cost. The loan amount divided by the total project cost. |
| **LTV** | Loan-to-value. The loan amount divided by the property value. |

## M to R

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **MCA** | Merchant cash advance. A funder buys a share of future sales. Not a loan. |
| **MCA Bailout** | A program for merchants struggling under existing advances, through payoff or restructured payments. |
| **Merchant** | The business owner seeking funding. |
| **Month-to-date (MTD)** | Bank activity from the first of the current month to today. |
| **Negative day** | A day that ends with the account balance below zero. |
| **Net funding** | The amount actually deposited after any payoffs or fees deducted by the funder. |
| **NOI** | Net operating income. A property's income minus operating expenses, before debt payments. |
| **NSF** | Non-sufficient funds. A payment returned because the account did not have enough money. |
| **Nurture** | Ongoing helpful, low-pressure contact with a lead who is not ready yet. |
| **Offer** | A funder's written proposal of amount and terms. Presented only by a closer or founder. |
| **Opt-out** | A person's request to stop calls, texts or emails. Honored immediately. |
| **Overdraft** | When the bank pays an item even though it takes the balance below zero, usually with a fee. |
| **Payment burden (total)** | All monthly debt payments divided by monthly true revenue. |
| **Payoff letter** | A funder's or lender's written statement of the amount needed to close an account. |
| **Personal financial statement (PFS)** | A form listing an owner's personal assets, debts and net worth. |
| **Personal guarantee** | A promise by the owner to be personally responsible if the business does not pay. |
| **PITI** | Principal, interest, taxes and insurance: the full monthly payment on a property loan. |
| **Position** | One open advance. The first advance is the first position, the next is the second position. |
| **Position map** | The list of every position: name, amount, frequency, first and last date seen. |
| **Pre-qualification** | An early check against screening minimums. It is not an approval and must never be called one. |
| **Profit and loss statement (P&L)** | A report of revenue, costs and profit for a period. |
| **Purchase order (PO)** | A buyer's official order for goods at an agreed price. |
| **PO factoring** | Funding to pay a supplier to fill a confirmed purchase order. |
| **Reconciliation** | Adjusting advance payments to match actual sales, when a contract allows it. |
| **Renewal** | Additional funding for an existing funded client. |
| **Rent roll** | A list of a property's units, tenants, rents and lease dates. |
| **Restricted industry** | An industry some funders avoid or limit. Listed in the funder matrix. |
| **Revenue based financing (RBF)** | Funding repaid as a share of revenue, rising and falling with sales. |
| **Reverse consolidation** | A funder deposits money regularly so the merchant can keep paying existing advances, then the merchant repays the new funder over a longer period. Often raises total cost. |
| **Rehab** | Renovation of a property. |

## S to Z

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **SBA 7(a)** | A US government-backed small business loan program made through approved lenders. |
| **Schedule of real estate owned (REO schedule)** | A list of properties an investor owns, with values, loans and income. |
| **Scope of work (SOW)** | A line-by-line list of renovation work and costs. |
| **Screening minimums** | Narqix's internal rules for starting a file for a product. Not promises. |
| **Scrub** | The Narqix 20 point review of bank statements before submission. |
| **Scrub sheet** | The record of the scrub, with the 3-line summary at the top. |
| **Seasonality** | Regular busy and slow periods during the year. |
| **Seller note** | Part of a business purchase price that the seller lets the buyer pay over time. |
| **Shotgunning** | Sending a file to many funders at once without matching. Banned at Narqix. |
| **Soft inquiry (soft pull)** | A credit check that does not affect the score and is not seen by other lenders. |
| **Speed to lead** | The time between a lead's request and your first contact. |
| **Stacking** | Taking several advances at the same time, or applying for several credit cards at once. |
| **Stips** | Stipulations. Items a funder requires before it will fund. |
| **Stop payment** | An instruction to the bank not to pay a specific item. A stop on a funder payment is a serious flag. |
| **Submission** | Sending a complete, scrubbed file to an approved funder. |
| **T-12** | The trailing 12 months of a property's income and expenses. |
| **Tax lien** | A government claim on property for unpaid taxes. An auto-stop flag. |
| **Term loan** | A lump sum repaid with fixed payments over a set period. |
| **Time in business (TIB)** | How long the business has been operating, usually from its start date. |
| **True revenue** | Deposits from real sales, after removing transfers, advance proceeds, refunds and other non-revenue items. |
| **UCC-1** | A public filing that records a lender's or funder's claim on business assets. |
| **UCC-3** | A filing that changes or ends a UCC-1, for example after payoff. |
| **Underwriter** | The person or system at a funder that reviews and decides on a file. |
| **Underwriting** | The review of a file to decide whether and how to fund. |
| **Use of funds** | What the merchant will do with the money. |
| **Voided check** | A blank check marked "VOID" that shows the account and routing numbers. |
| **White label** | A service delivered under one company's brand while partners work behind it. |
| **Working capital** | Money used for day-to-day operations: payroll, inventory, rent. |
