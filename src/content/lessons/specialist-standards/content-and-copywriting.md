<div class="callout callout-intro">

For Narqix content writers and copywriters producing web copy, ad copy, emails, articles, scripts and sales materials. Use this page as your working checklist to deliver writing to the Narqix standard.

Track: Specialist  ·  Role: Content Writer, Copywriter  ·  Time to complete: 10 min

</div>

## Definition of done

- [ ]  The piece does the job in the brief, for the audience in the brief, with one clear call to action
- [ ]  Every fact, number and claim is accurate and sourced in the working document
- [ ]  It matches the client's voice and style guide
- [ ]  It is edited, proofread and formatted for where it will be published
- [ ]  Search requirements met where relevant (topic, structure, title, description, internal links)
- [ ]  The client approved it in writing

## Quality checklist

<details class="toggle"><summary>Before writing</summary>

- [ ]  Brief confirmed: goal, audience, the one action, format, length, deadline, approver
- [ ]  You know the reader's problem, what they already believe, and what would change their mind
- [ ]  Outline or angle approved for long pieces before the full draft
- [ ]  Sources gathered: client interviews, client data, primary sources

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>Substance</summary>

- [ ]  Main point in the first two sentences
- [ ]  Specific over general: real numbers, real examples, real steps
- [ ]  Every claim can be proven. No guaranteed results, income, savings or approvals
- [ ]  Customer quotes and testimonials are real and approved by the customer
- [ ]  Industry rules respected (financial, legal, health and housing content often needs disclaimers or expert review)
- [ ]  Says something useful that the top search results do not

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>Style</summary>

- [ ]  Short sentences, plain words, active voice
- [ ]  Spelling and date format match the client's country
- [ ]  Headings and short paragraphs so it can be scanned
- [ ]  No filler phrases, no buzzwords, no hype
- [ ]  Consistent terms: one name for each product, service and feature

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>Final edit</summary>

- [ ]  Read aloud once, slowly
- [ ]  Names, numbers, dates, prices and links checked against the source
- [ ]  Plagiarism check passed; nothing copied from other sites
- [ ]  Formatting checked in the real publishing view, not only in the draft

</details>

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ]  Final approved in writing by the named approver
- [ ]  Title, description and preview text written
- [ ]  Links tested and pointing to the right pages, with campaign tracking tags where used
- [ ]  Images have alt text and are licensed
- [ ]  Required disclaimers present
- [ ]  Publishing date and time set in the client's zone
- [ ]  For emails: test sent to two inboxes, checked on mobile, unsubscribe link working, sending list confirmed by the client

<div class="callout callout-coach">

Coach's note: the best source of copy is the client's own customers. Ask for five recent sales call notes, reviews or support tickets. The exact words customers use for their problem will beat anything you invent, and they are the words people type into search.

</div>

## Reporting KPIs

| KPI | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Pieces delivered on schedule | Against the content calendar |
| Revision rounds | Within the agreed rounds |
| Factual errors found after publishing | Zero |
| Organic traffic to published pieces | At 30, 90 and 180 days |
| Conversions from content | Leads, sign-ups or sales the piece started or assisted |
| Email open and click rates | For email copy, compared with the client's average |
| Ad copy performance | Click-through rate and conversion rate, shared by the ads manager |

## Access and security rules

- Draft in the client's shared documents or content system, not in personal files.
- Never paste confidential client material, customer data or unpublished financial numbers into an AI tool unless it is an approved business account and the client allowed it in writing.
- AI may help with research and first drafts only if the client allows it. You own every word that ships: check every fact and edit every line.
- Publishing access is used only for approved pieces. Never publish a draft to "test" it on a live site.

## Common mistakes

<details class="toggle"><summary>Open the list</summary>

- Writing about the client ("we are passionate leaders") instead of the reader's problem.
- Long introductions before the useful part.
- Vague claims with no numbers or proof.
- Copying structure and phrases from a competitor's page.
- Publishing AI drafts with invented facts, quotes or sources.
- One call to action buried at the end of a long page.
- Ignoring the client's legal limits on what can be promised.

</details>

## Red flags to escalate

| Red flag | Level | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client asks for fake reviews, invented testimonials or made-up statistics | P2 | Decline, inform Delivery Lead |
| Client asks for claims that guarantee results, income, savings or approvals | P2 | Offer compliant wording, inform Delivery Lead |
| Published content contains a wrong price, legal claim or someone's private data | P1 | Alert client and Delivery Lead immediately |
| Content would copy or closely rewrite another company's material | P2 | Stop and inform Delivery Lead |
| Health, legal, financial or housing content without expert review | P3 | Request review before publishing |

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never do this: never invent a statistic, quote, source or customer story. If you cannot prove it, cut it.

</div>
