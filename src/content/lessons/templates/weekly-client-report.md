<div class="callout callout-intro">

For every Narqix team member who reports weekly to a lead or client. After reading this page you will be able to write a weekly report in under 30 minutes that shows the pattern of the week, the numbers against target, the risks and the decisions the client must make.

Track: All tracks  ·  Role: Everyone  ·  Time to complete: 15 min

</div>

## When and where

- **When:** the last working day of the client's week (usually Friday), before the end of the client's day, in the client's time zone. Your Playbook may set another day.
- **Where:** the channel the client prefers, with detailed numbers in a linked sheet.
- **Length:** one screen. Two minutes to read.
- **Built from:** your SOD and EOD reports for the week. If they are accurate, the weekly report almost writes itself.

## The six-block client report

```
Weekly report, (CLIENT NAME), week ending (DATE)

Headline: (THE ONE THING TO KNOW THIS WEEK, IN ONE SENTENCE)

Numbers (this week / last week / target):
- (MEASURE): (THIS WEEK) / (LAST WEEK) / (TARGET)
- (MEASURE): (THIS WEEK) / (LAST WEEK) / (TARGET)
- (MEASURE): (THIS WEEK) / (LAST WEEK) / (TARGET)

Wins:
- (OUTCOME THAT IMPROVED OR GOT FINISHED, WITH A NUMBER)
- (OUTCOME)

Issues and risks:
- (WHAT WENT WRONG OR MIGHT). Done: (WHAT YOU DID). Next: (WHAT HAPPENS NEXT, BY WHEN)

Decisions needed:
1. (DECISION). My suggestion: (RECOMMENDATION AND ONE-LINE REASON)
2. (DECISION). My suggestion: (RECOMMENDATION AND ONE-LINE REASON)

Next week:
1. (PRIORITY), by (DAY)
2. (PRIORITY), by (DAY)
3. (PRIORITY), by (DAY)
```

## The short campaign report

```
Weekly report, week of (DATE)
Volume: (KEY COUNTS FOR THE WEEK)
KPIs vs target:
- (KPI): (ACTUAL) vs (TARGET)
- (KPI): (ACTUAL) vs (TARGET)
Wins: (ONE OR TWO SPECIFIC RESULTS)
Issues: (WHAT WENT WRONG, WHAT YOU DID ABOUT IT)
Ideas: (ONE IMPROVEMENT YOU SUGGEST)
Next week's focus: (TOP 3)
```

## Suggested measures by track

Pick the measures your client agreed at launch. These are the usual ones.

| Track | Usual weekly measures |
| --- | --- |
| Real Estate (calling) | Dials, live contacts, conversations, HOT and APPT leads, appointments shown, DNC coded same shift |
| Real Estate (acquisitions and dispositions) | Offers made, contracts signed, buyers contacted per deal, walkthroughs, assignments locked |
| Transaction Coordination | Use the TC Weekly Pipeline Report |
| Solar | Dials, conversations, SET, CONF, show rate, NS and CXL counts |
| HVAC and Home Services | Calls handled, booking rate, unbooked reason codes, memberships offered, reviews requested |
| Property Preservation | Orders received and accepted, updates submitted on time, rejections, past due, bids approved, invoices submitted |
| Strategic Funding | Qualified leads, applications sent, docs-pending conversion, files submitted, stips cleared |
| Back Office | Tasks completed, inbox processed, invoices entered, accuracy on QA sample, turnaround time |
| Customer Support | Tickets solved, first reply time, resolution time, QA score, customer satisfaction |

## Writing rules

1. **Numbers over adjectives.** Not "lots of calls", but "512 calls".
2. **Always compare.** Last week and target next to every number.
3. **Bad news early and plainly.** Every issue shows what you did and what is next.
4. **Every decision has your recommendation,** so the client can reply "yes to both".
5. **No surprises.** A serious problem goes up the Escalation Ladder the day it happens. The weekly report is never the first time the client hears of it.
6. **Honest numbers only.** If a number was not measured, write "not measured this week" and why.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Keep a "report notes" file open all week. Every time something good, bad or decision-worthy happens, add one line with the date. On Friday you copy, trim and send, and you never lose Monday's win.

</div>

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never invent, guess or "improve" a number. One made-up number makes every other number in the report worthless. Never include customer personal data; use IDs, initials or counts.

</div>

## Before you send

- [ ]  Headline is one sentence and the most important fact of the week
- [ ]  Every number checked against its source
- [ ]  Last week and target shown next to each number
- [ ]  Every issue has an action and a next step
- [ ]  Every decision has a recommendation
- [ ]  Client's spelling and date style
- [ ]  Fits on one screen

## Check yourself

2. Who uses the short campaign report, and who turns it into the client's report?
3. A serious data mistake happened on Tuesday. Is Friday's report the place to tell the client?
4. A number was not tracked this week. What do you write?
