For dispositions managers and assistants. After reading this page you will be able to build an accurate deal sheet that a cash buyer can decide on quickly, and keep a buyer activity summary your client can read at a glance.

Track: Real Estate  ·  Role: Dispositions  ·  Time to complete: 15 min

</div>

## What the deal sheet is for

The deal sheet is the one document a buyer reads before deciding to walk the property or make an offer. It must be **accurate**. Buyers check the ARV first, then the repairs, then the price. A team known for honest numbers sells deals in days.

The content rules are on the Dispositions Manager page and the Deal Presentation Call script.

## Before you send it

- [ ]  Client approval to market this property
- [ ]  Market Compliance Card checked for the state (some states regulate marketing a property you only have under contract)
- [ ]  ARV from the Comps and ARV Worksheet, with 3 or more comps
- [ ]  Repair tier and main items from the Repair Tier Worksheet
- [ ]  Price, deposit and deadlines confirmed with the client and the TC
- [ ]  Access rules confirmed with the TC (lockbox, showing times, tenant notice)
- [ ]  Buyer list filtered to the matching buy box

## The template

```

Price: (ASSIGNMENT PRICE OR RESALE PRICE)
Deal structure: (ASSIGNMENT OF CONTRACT OR DOUBLE CLOSE)
Earnest money: (EMD AMOUNT), due within (EMD DEADLINE) of signing, (EMD REFUND TERMS)
Funding: (CASH OR HARD MONEY ONLY, or AS APPROVED BY CLIENT)
Condition: sold as-is
Closing costs: (WHO PAYS WHICH COSTS)
Closing by: (CLOSING DATE)    Title company: (TITLE COMPANY)

PROPERTY FACTS
Type and style: (TYPE AND STYLE)
Beds / baths: (BEDS) / (BATHS)    Living area: (SQFT)    Year built: (YEAR)
Lot: (LOT SIZE)    Garage: (DETAIL)    Basement: (DETAIL)
Occupancy: (VACANT, OWNER UNTIL CLOSING, OR TENANT WITH LEASE END AND RENT)

NUMBERS
ARV: (ARV)
Comps (renovated, sold):
1. (ADDRESS) · sold (DATE) · (SQFT) sqft · (PRICE)
2. (ADDRESS) · sold (DATE) · (SQFT) sqft · (PRICE)
3. (ADDRESS) · sold (DATE) · (SQFT) sqft · (PRICE)
Repair estimate: (AMOUNT), tier (TIER)
Main items: (LIST OF MAIN REPAIRS)
Known issues: (LIST, or "none known")
Rent estimate (landlord buyers): (MONTHLY RENT, or NOT PROVIDED)

PHOTOS AND ACCESS

```

## Buyer activity summary (for the client)

Update this on the deal record after every buyer call, so the client sees interest without asking.

```
BUYER ACTIVITY · (PROPERTY ADDRESS) · Deal ID (DEAL ID) · updated (DATE, TIME AND ZONE)
Matching buyers contacted: (NUMBER) (A: (NUMBER), B: (NUMBER), C: (NUMBER))
Calls made: (NUMBER)
Walkthroughs booked: (NUMBER)    Walkthroughs held: (NUMBER)
Offers received: (NUMBER)    Highest offer: (AMOUNT), from buyer rated (RATING)
Main reasons for passing: (PRICE, ARV, REPAIRS, LOCATION, CONDITION, TIMING), with counts
Buyer must be locked by: (DATE)
Recommendation: (HOLD PRICE, ADJUST PRICE WITH CLIENT, OR EXTEND MARKETING), because (REASON)
```

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Lead with facts, not excitement. "3/2 ranch, ARV 250K on three renovated sales within half a mile, Full Cosmetic at 50K, price 118K" sells better to a serious buyer than any adjective. Serious buyers do their own math in ten seconds.

</div>

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never send a deal before the client approves marketing and the state's Market Compliance Card is checked. Never put the seller's personal situation or contact details on a deal sheet, and never include the original seller contract or its price unless the client approves it in writing. Never defend an ARV you cannot support with comps.

</div>

## Check yourself

1. What does a buyer check first on a deal sheet?
2. Name four items in the Price and Terms block.
3. Which seller information stays off a deal sheet?
4. What does the buyer activity summary tell the client?

<details class="toggle toggle-answers"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. The ARV and its comps.
2. Any four of: price, deal structure, earnest money and deadline, funding allowed, as-is condition, who pays closing costs, closing date, title company.
3. The seller's personal situation and contact details. The original seller contract and its price only appear if the client approves in writing.
4. How many matching buyers were contacted, walkthroughs, offers, the highest offer, why buyers passed, the lock deadline and your recommendation.

</details>
