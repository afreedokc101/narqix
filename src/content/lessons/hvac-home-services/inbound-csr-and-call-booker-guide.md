For Narqix team members who answer inbound calls, texts and web leads for a home services client. After this page you will know what you own, the numbers you are measured on, your daily rhythm, and the standards that make a Narqix CSR the best voice a homeowner hears all week.

## What you own

You are the first voice of the company. The homeowner does not know the technicians yet. They decide whether to trust the company based on the 3 minutes they spend with you.

You own:

- Answering every inbound call fast and warmly.
- Screening every relevant call for safety.
- Booking service, maintenance and sales appointments.
- Classifying every call, booked or not.
- Returning missed calls and contacting new web leads.
- Mentioning the maintenance plan to eligible callers.
- Clean, complete records for every interaction.

You do not own: diagnosing, pricing repairs, approving discounts, deciding warranty coverage, or deciding whether a return visit is free. You capture the facts and route those decisions to the right person.

## KPIs that matter

| KPI | Narqix standard | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Booking rate (real leads) | 80 percent or higher. Your client's baseline sets the starting goal | The single biggest driver of client revenue from your seat |
| Speed to answer | Within 3 rings | Callers in a hurry hang up and call the next company |
| Hold time | Avoid holds. If needed, ask permission and come back within 20 seconds | Long holds cause hang-ups |
| Abandon rate (team) | Under 5 percent | Shows whether the team is covering the phones |
| Missed call callback | Within 30 minutes, 100 percent | A missed caller is often still deciding |
| Web lead first contact | Within 5 minutes | The first company to respond usually wins |
| Average call length | 2 to 4 minutes for a booking | Shorter often means steps skipped; longer often means diagnosing |
| Membership mention rate | 100 percent of eligible calls | You cannot sell what you do not mention |
| Membership close rate | 10 percent or more of eligible calls | Recurring revenue for the client |
| Reason code accuracy | 100 percent on audit | Clients use this data to fix problems |
| Note quality (QA audit) | 95 percent or higher | Technicians and dispatch rely on your notes |
| QA call score | 90 percent or higher on 5 recorded calls a week | Overall call quality |

### Know your number

Top teams turn the client's revenue goal into a daily booking target. Example: the client wants USD 100,000 a month in service revenue and the average ticket is USD 2,500. That is 40 jobs. At an 80 percent booking rate, the team needs 50 real leads. At 50 percent booking, it needs 80 leads, which costs far more in marketing. Ask your team lead what your daily target is and track it yourself.

## Your daily rhythm

### Before your shift (15 minutes)

1. Check in through your campaign's check-in method.
2. Read the handoff from the previous shift and the overnight log: emergencies, callbacks owed, open issues.
3. Listen to any voicemails left since the last shift and list callbacks.
4. Check the weather for the client's area. A heat advisory or freeze warning changes your triage.
5. Check today's board capacity: which windows are open, which are full, any technician out sick.
6. Check the team channel for changes: fees, promotions, service area, special instructions.

### During your shift

- **Live calls first.** Answer within 3 rings. Follow the Booking Standard.

<details class="toggle"><summary><strong>Between calls</strong>, in this order:</summary>

1. Missed calls and voicemails (30-minute rule).
2. New web leads and text inquiries (5-minute rule).
3. Confirmations for tomorrow's jobs if your role includes them.
4. Tune-up bookings and outbound lists if your client assigns them.

</details>

- **Every hour**, glance at the board for same-day openings you can offer to people on the cancellation list.

### End of shift (15 minutes)

1. Every call from your shift is classified. Every unbooked call has a reason code and next step.
2. Every callback you promised is either done or handed over in writing.
3. Write the shift handoff: emergencies, open callbacks, unusual calls, system issues.
4. Check out through your campaign's check-out method.

## Quality checklist

- [ ]  Greeting under 10 seconds with company name and my name
- [ ]  Asked the caller's name and used it at least twice
- [ ]  Located the record and checked history, membership, open estimates, recalls
- [ ]  Asked five or more discovery questions without diagnosing
- [ ]  Ran the safety screen on every relevant call
- [ ]  Reassured the caller
- [ ]  Disclosed all fees clearly
- [ ]  Offered two specific arrival windows
- [ ]  Mentioned the maintenance plan if eligible
- [ ]  Restated the booking and explained the next steps
- [ ]  Note in standard format, all fields filled
- [ ]  Unbooked calls coded with a reason and next date

## Common mistakes

1. **Sounding rushed when the queue is busy.** The caller cannot see your queue. Each caller deserves your full calm attention.
2. **Diagnosing to be helpful.** "It's probably your capacitor" sets an expectation the technician may not meet, and it can make the customer try a dangerous fix.
3. **Quoting from memory.** Fees and promotions change. Use the current fee sheet.
4. **Skipping the safety screen on "simple" calls.** A no-heat call can also be a CO call.
5. **Giving up after the first objection.** Acknowledge, explain value once, offer the slot again. Then code it honestly.
6. **Letting the missed-call list grow.** Thirty minutes is the rule, even on busy days.
7. **Going off script on location questions.** If asked where you are, answer with the client's company and market, using the client's approved wording. Never mention your own country. See the Objection Handbook.

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never argue with a caller, never hang up on a caller who is upset (escalate instead), never make promises about price, warranty, timing or refunds that are not in your instructions, and never mark a real lead as "not a lead".

</div>

## Escalation rules

| Situation | Escalate to | How fast |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Any safety protocol call | On-call manager or dispatcher | Within 2 minutes |
| After-hours P1 or P2 | On-call ladder | Immediately |
| Angry customer asking for a manager | Office manager (warm transfer if available, or callback) | Same call or within 1 hour |
| Complaint about a technician, damage or billing | Office manager | Same day |
| Legal threat, lawyer, media, regulator, or "I'll report you" | Office manager and your Narqix team lead | Immediately |
| Request outside policy (discount, free visit, fee waiver) | Office manager | Before you promise anything |
| Customer wants to stop all calls or texts | Record opt-out immediately, inform team lead | Same day, same shift |
| System down, phones not working | Your Narqix team lead and the client contact | Immediately |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** A useful habit: before you transfer or escalate, summarise in one sentence for the person receiving it. "This is (CUSTOMER NAME) at (SERVICE ADDRESS), member, technician came Monday for no cooling and it's not cooling again, they're upset about paying twice." Nobody makes the customer repeat their story. That is what premium service feels like.

</div>

## Check yourself

1. What are the time standards for answering, missed-call callbacks and web leads?
2. Name three decisions a CSR never makes alone.
3. Your call took 7 minutes and did not book. What two things might have gone wrong?
4. Who gets a caller who says they want to replace their 20-year-old furnace?
5. What goes in your end-of-shift handoff?

<details class="toggle toggle-answers"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Answer within 3 rings, missed calls back within 30 minutes, web leads within 5 minutes.
2. Any three of: diagnosis, repair pricing, discounts, warranty coverage, whether a return visit is free, fee waivers.
3. You may have been diagnosing or explaining too much, or you did not offer two specific windows and ask for the booking.
4. A comfort advisor. Book a replacement consultation per your client's process.
5. Emergencies and their status, callbacks still owed, unusual calls, complaints, system issues and anything the next shift must act on.

</details>
