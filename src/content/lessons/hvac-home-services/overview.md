## Why this track matters

When a US homeowner's air conditioner stops on a 98°F afternoon, or water is coming through the kitchen ceiling, they call the first company they find. The company that answers fast, sounds calm and books a clear time usually wins the job. The company that lets the phone ring, or puts the caller on a long hold, loses that job and often loses that customer for life.

That is what our clients buy from Narqix. Not "someone to answer phones", but three outcomes:

1. **Responsiveness.** Calls answered in three rings, missed calls returned in 30 minutes, web leads contacted in 5 minutes.
2. **Booked revenue.** A high share of real callers leave the call with an appointment.
3. **Clean records.** Every call, text and job has notes that a technician, dispatcher or owner can act on without calling you.

Everything in this track trains you toward those three outcomes, while keeping every customer safe and every action honest and legal.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** In this industry, one missed call is not one missed call. A repair might be USD 300 to 500, a new system can be USD 8,000 to 15,000, and a happy customer may join a maintenance plan and stay for ten years. When you treat every ring as valuable, owners notice within the first week.

## How a US home services company runs

Most of our clients are small to mid-size, family-owned businesses with 5 to 150 employees. They often serve one metro area and a ring of nearby towns. Many do more than one trade (for example HVAC plus plumbing, or plumbing plus electrical).

### The people you will work with

| Person | What they do | How you work with them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner or general manager | Sets prices, service area, rules and goals | Their rules override anything in this track |
| Office or operations manager | Runs the office team day to day | Your main point of contact and first escalation |
| CSR (customer service representative) or call booker | Answers calls, books jobs, handles basic questions | Often this is you |
| Dispatcher | Assigns jobs to technicians, manages the daily board, updates customers | Often this is you, or you hand jobs to them |
| Service technician | Goes to the home, diagnoses, quotes and repairs | You give them clean job notes; they give you outcomes |
| Install crew | Replaces full systems, water heaters, panels, roofs | Long jobs, booked in full days |
| Comfort advisor (also called sales advisor or replacement consultant) | Visits homes to quote new equipment | You book their appointments and follow up on their estimates |
| On-call technician or on-call manager | Covers nights, weekends and emergencies | You page them using the after-hours ladder |
| Roofing sales rep or project manager | Inspects roofs, meets adjusters, sells retail roofs | You qualify and book their inspections |

### The life of a typical job

1. **Demand.** A homeowner calls, fills in a web form, sends a text, or comes from a third-party lead source. Some calls come from existing customers and members.
2. **Booking.** The CSR runs a safety screen, captures the details, discloses the fee and books an arrival window.
3. **Dispatch.** The dispatcher assigns the job to the right technician based on skill, location and priority.
4. **Confirmation.** The customer gets a confirmation the day before and an "on the way" message on the day.
5. **The visit.** The technician diagnoses, explains options with prices, and gets approval before any work.
6. **Outcome.** The job is completed, or the customer gets an estimate (repair or replacement), or the job goes on hold (waiting for parts, waiting for a decision).
7. **Follow-up.** Open estimates are followed up. Every completed customer gets a review request. Non-members hear about the maintenance plan.
8. **Recurring care.** Members get seasonal tune-ups, priority service and discounts, which keeps them loyal.

### How the money works

- **Service calls.** The customer usually pays a diagnostic fee (also called a trip fee or service call fee) for the technician's visit and diagnosis. Repairs are priced upfront from a flat-rate price list, called a pricebook. Many clients credit the fee toward the repair; follow your client's rule.
- **Replacements and installs.** The biggest tickets. New systems, water heaters, electrical panels and roofs. These usually start as a lead for a comfort advisor or sales rep.
- **Memberships.** Also called maintenance agreements, service plans or comfort clubs. The customer pays monthly or yearly for tune-ups, priority scheduling and repair discounts. This recurring revenue keeps the business stable in slow months.
- **Financing.** Many clients offer financing through a partner lender for big purchases. You only ever say that options exist.

### The seasons

| Trade | Busy | Slower | What it means for you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HVAC | Summer (cooling) and winter (heating) | Spring and fall ("shoulder seasons") | Peak season is about fast booking and triage. Shoulder season is about tune-ups, memberships and estimate follow-up |
| Plumbing | Steady all year, spikes in hard freezes and holidays | Fewer swings | Freeze events bring burst pipes and no-hot-water calls |
| Electrical | Steady, spikes after storms and outages | Fewer swings | Generator, surge and panel work after storms |
| Roofing | Spring and summer, plus 1 to 10 days after hail or wind storms | Winter in cold states | Lead volume can multiply overnight after a storm |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Check the weather for your client's city before every shift. A heat advisory, a freeze warning or a hailstorm changes how many calls you get and how you prioritize them. The best CSRs know the forecast before the first call comes in.

</div>

## Where Narqix fits

Narqix team members work remotely inside the client's own systems: the field service management system (where customers, jobs and the dispatch board live), the phone system or dialer, the texting platform, email and the team channel. We follow the client's scripts, fees, service area and emergency rules. When the client's rules and this track disagree, the client's rules win, except where safety, honesty or the law is involved. In those cases you stop and escalate.

| Dispatcher | Build and run the daily board, keep customers informed | Dispatcher Guide |
| Outbound Follow-Up and Membership Rep | Estimate follow-up, tune-up campaigns, memberships and renewals | Outbound Follow-Up and Membership Rep Guide |
| Review and Reputation Assistant | Review requests, review reply drafts, complaint routing | Review and Reputation Assistant Guide |
| Roofing Lead Qualifier | Qualify storm and retail roofing leads, book inspections | Roofing Lead Qualifier Guide |

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never diagnose a problem, promise a price the technician has not given, promise an exact arrival minute, or tell a caller anything is "safe". You are not on site and you are not licensed. Your job is to keep people safe, capture the facts and get the right expert there.

### Your first two weeks

| Days | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 2 | Read the core four. Pass the Check yourself quizzes. Learn your client's service area, fees and emergency rules |
| 3 to 4 | Read your role guide and the Glossary. Shadow live calls or review recorded calls with your team lead |

## The Narqix home services promise

- We answer like it is our own company, and we are always honest about who we are.
- We put safety before booking, every time.
- We never guess at prices, diagnoses or insurance coverage.
