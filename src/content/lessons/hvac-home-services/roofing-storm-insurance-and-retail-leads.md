|  | Storm restoration (insurance) | Retail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why the homeowner calls | Hail or wind damaged the roof | Old roof, leaks, selling the home, insurance company asked for a new roof |

## Part 1: Storm and insurance leads

### The claim process in simple words

You need to understand this process so you can schedule and track it. You do not explain insurance to homeowners. When they ask insurance questions, you send them to their insurance agent or company.

1. **Storm.** Hail or strong wind hits the area. The client learns the date and the affected ZIP codes (the storm footprint).
2. **Inspection.** The roofing company's rep inspects the roof for free and takes photos. If there is no real damage, a good company says so.
3. **Homeowner decides.** If there is damage, the homeowner decides whether to file a claim with their insurance company. The homeowner files it, not us.
4. **Adjuster visit.** The insurance company sends an adjuster to inspect. The homeowner may ask the roofing rep to be there too. You may schedule this meeting.
5. **Scope of loss.** The insurer sends a written list of what it will pay for and how much.
6. **Supplements.** If the insurer missed items (for example damaged decking found during tear-off, or code-required parts), the roofing company sends a supplement request with proof.
7. **Build.** The crew replaces or repairs the roof.
8. **Final paperwork.** The company sends completion documents. If the policy pays in two parts, the second payment is released.

### Money words (understand them, do not explain them)

- **Deductible:** the part the homeowner pays on each claim. It can be a flat amount (for example 1,000 USD) or, in many hail-prone states, a percentage of the home's insured value (often 1 to 2 percent). On a home insured for 400,000 USD, a 2 percent deductible is 8,000 USD.
- **ACV (actual cash value) policy:** pays the value of the old roof after wear and age are taken off. The amount taken off is not paid back.
- **RCV (replacement cost value) policy:** pays the ACV amount first, then pays the rest (the **recoverable depreciation**) after the work is completed and documented.
- **Example (RCV):** roof replacement 20,000 USD. Depreciation 8,000. Deductible 2,000. The first payment is 10,000 (20,000 minus 8,000 minus 2,000). After completion, the insurer releases the 8,000. The homeowner pays the 2,000 deductible.
- **Upgrades** (for example a better shingle than the old one) are the homeowner's cost unless the insurer agrees otherwise.
- **Mortgage company:** insurance checks are often made out to both the homeowner and their mortgage company. This can add time. The rep or office handles it.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** If a homeowner asks "So how much will insurance pay?" the honest answer is always: "That's the insurance company's decision after their adjuster sees it. What we do is document everything clearly so they have the full picture." Say it calmly. It builds more trust than any promise.

</div>

### Deductible law: the hard line

Many US states (at least 28) make it illegal for a contractor to waive, pay, rebate or absorb a homeowner's insurance deductible, or to offer gifts or rewards for an inspection or a claim. Some examples:

- **Texas** bans waiving, absorbing or rebating any part of a deductible, with fines and possible jail time.
- **Colorado** bans paying, waiving or rebating a deductible, and gives homeowners a right to cancel a roofing contract within a set number of hours after signing and after a claim is denied.
- **Florida** bans gifts, cash or deductible waivers for allowing an inspection or filing a claim, bans interpreting policies unless you are a licensed public adjuster, and holds the contractor responsible for anyone soliciting on its behalf.

Rules change and differ by state. **Narqix does not rely on state differences.** Our rule is the same in all 50 states.

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this (in any state):**

Never offer, suggest or hint at waiving, covering, rebating or "taking care of" a deductible.

Never offer gifts, cash, gift cards or discounts for an inspection, a claim, or a referral, unless the client's legal team has approved a referral program in writing for that state.

Never say "free roof" or "insurance will cover it".

Never interpret a policy, predict a claim result, or promise approval.

Never file, start or talk to the insurer about a claim on the homeowner's behalf.

Never tell a homeowner to claim damage that is not there or to change the storm date.

</div>

### The deductible line (say it on every storm appointment)

> **You:** Just so you know upfront, if you do decide to file a claim, your deductible is your responsibility under your policy. We aren't able to waive or cover it. What we can do is make sure your inspection is documented clearly.
> 

Then note in the record: "Deductible responsibility explained: yes."

### Storm qualification checklist

1. **Owner?** "Are you the homeowner?" Who else is on the title or involved in decisions?
2. **Address and footprint.** Confirm the address. Is it inside the area the client is working for this storm?
3. **Storm date.** "Do you remember when the storm hit your home?" Use only the dates the client has confirmed.
4. **Signs of damage.** Missing or creased shingles, ceiling stains, dented gutters, vents or downspouts, damaged screens or siding, dented cars, neighbours getting roofs.
5. **Active leak?** If water is coming in now, route an emergency tarp request per client policy.
6. **Roof age and type.** Shingle, metal, tile, flat. Approximate age.
7. **Claim status.** Already filed? Has an adjuster or another contractor been out? Claim number if they have one.
8. **Insurance company name.** Only to schedule and track, never to give advice.
9. **Availability.** Can the decision-makers be there for the inspection or the review of photos afterwards?

### After the inspection (if your client assigns tracking)

- If the homeowner files, record the claim number, insurance company and adjuster contact.
- Schedule the adjuster meeting and confirm the rep will attend.
- Create tasks for scope received, supplement sent, supplement approved, build date, completion documents sent and depreciation released.
- Note every contact with the homeowner. Never contact the insurer yourself unless the client has a legal process and authority for it.

## Part 2: Retail roofing leads

### Retail qualification checklist

1. **Owner and decision-makers.** Can everyone involved sit down together for 60 to 90 minutes?
2. **Why now?** Age, leaks, curling or missing shingles, granules in gutters, selling the home, insurance non-renewal letter.
3. **Timeline.** Emergency, this season, or planning for next year?
4. **Other bids.** Have they had other quotes?
5. **Financing interest.** Only whether they want to hear about options. No rates.
6. **HOA.** Is there a homeowners association with rules on roof colour or material?
7. **Material preferences.** Shingle, metal, other.

**Who to skip (per client rules):** renters (unless the owner joins), roofs replaced very recently, addresses outside the service area.

### The decision-maker line

> **You:** So we can have the right person come out, is anyone else involved in the decision, like a spouse or co-owner? It works best when everyone can see the options at the same time, so nobody has to repeat anything.
> 

### Retail objections

<details class="toggle"><summary>&quot;Just give me a ballpark price.&quot;</summary>

> **You:** I wish I could, but roofs vary a lot with size, steepness, layers and materials, so any number I gave you now would be a guess. The inspection is free, and you'll get a written, itemized price.
