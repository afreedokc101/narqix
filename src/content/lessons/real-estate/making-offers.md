## How an offer is built

An offer is not a guess. It is three numbers combined in a fixed order:

1. **ARV:** what the home is worth after repairs (see Underwriting 101).
2. **Repairs:** the planning estimate from the Narqix rehab table (see Repair Estimating).
3. **The margin:** what the end buyer needs for costs and profit, plus the client's fee.

Work in this order every time: **repairs, then ARV, then MAO, then the offer range.**

## The buyer's number first

Our client usually does not keep the house. A cash buyer (a flipper or a landlord) does. So we start by asking: **what is the most a typical cash buyer will pay?**

The common rule in US investing:

**Buyer's maximum = (ARV × 70%) − repairs**

Why 70%? The remaining 30% of the ARV covers the buyer's purchase and sale closing costs, agent fees when they resell, loan costs, insurance, taxes and utilities while they hold the house, and their profit for the risk.

### Choosing the percentage

| Market and deal | Percentage to use |
| --- | --- |
| Normal market, normal rehab | **70%** |
| Slow market, heavy rehab, lower-value area, or low ARV confidence | **65%** (safer) |
| Fast market, high-value area, light rehab, strong buyer demand | **75%**, and only with the client's approval |

## The MAO (our walk-away number)

**MAO = (ARV × percentage) − repairs − the client's fee**

The **client's fee** is the assignment fee (or the spread on a double close). It is set by the client. When the client has not set one, Narqix plans with **10K to 15K** as a starting assumption and confirms with the client before any offer.

The MAO is the **highest** price we can agree with the seller. It is not the opening offer.

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never go above the MAO without written approval from the client. Never reduce the repair estimate or raise the ARV to "make the numbers work". If the numbers do not work, the deal does not work at that price, and we tell the seller honestly.

</div>

## Assignment fee: how to think about it

- In an **assignment**, the client signs a purchase agreement with the seller, then assigns (transfers) its rights in that contract to the end buyer. The buyer pays the client an **assignment fee**. On most settlement statements, this fee is visible to all parties.
- In a **double close**, the client buys the house from the seller and resells it to the end buyer, usually on the same day, often using short-term transactional funding (commonly about 1% to 3% of the price). The client's profit is the difference between the two prices, minus both sets of costs.
- A fair fee rewards the client for finding, negotiating and managing the deal. A fee so large that no buyer will pay the price kills the deal.
- Many states now require clear written disclosure to the seller that the contract may be assigned or that the buyer intends to resell for more. See Calling Compliance for Real Estate.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** If a seller asks "How much will you make?", be honest about the structure: "Our company is paid a fee when we pass the contract to the investor who does the repairs. It shows on the closing paperwork." Sellers respect a straight answer much more than a dodge.

</div>

## The offer range

Do not open at the MAO. Leave room for honest negotiation. Narqix plans three numbers:

| Number | How to set it | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Opening offer** | About 10% to 15% below the MAO | Where the conversation starts |
| **Target** | About 5% to 7% below the MAO | Where most deals should land |
| **Walk-away (MAO)** | The MAO | Not one dollar higher without client approval |

When the seller is clearly motivated and the price they asked is already close to the target, it is fine to offer at or near the target. **Lowballing a fair seller to "test" them damages trust.**

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Full Cosmetic ranch (standard method)

From the Underwriting 101 and Repair Estimating pages: 1,450 sqft, ARV **250,000**, Full Cosmetic repairs **50,000**, normal market, client fee **15,000**.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer's maximum | 250,000 × 70% − 50,000 | 125,000 |
| MAO | 125,000 − 15,000 | **110,000** |
| Opening offer | About 13% below the MAO | 95,000 |
| Target | About 6% below the MAO | 103,000 |
| Walk-away | MAO | 110,000 |

The seller asked 165,000. The acquisitions manager explains the numbers, opens at 95,000, and the parties agree at 103,000. The client assigns the contract to a buyer at 118,000. The client's fee is 15,000; the buyer is at 118,000, which is under their 125,000 maximum.

### Example 2: Easy house in a slower, lower-value area

900 sqft, ARV **140,000**, Easy repairs **10,000**, slow C-class market, client fee **10,000**.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer's maximum (65%) | 140,000 × 65% − 10,000 | 81,000 |
| MAO | 81,000 − 10,000 | **71,000** |

### Example 3: Full Gut, larger house

2,200 sqft, ARV **380,000**, Full Gut repairs **100K to 120K** (seller clear and specific, so use the middle: **110,000**), normal market, client fee **15,000**.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer's maximum | 380,000 × 70% − 110,000 | 156,000 |
| MAO | 156,000 − 15,000 | **141,000** |

## The rental check (for landlord buyers)

In B and C class areas, the end buyer is often a landlord who thinks in rent, not ARV. Use this check alongside the 70% method, and use the **lower** result unless the client says otherwise.

**Class rent factor:** A = 0.005, B = 0.01, C = 0.025

**Total investment a landlord will accept = monthly market rent ÷ class rent factor**

**Contract price = total investment − repairs − the client's fee**

Market rent is the average of 3 similar rentals nearby.

| Example | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B-class, rent 1,450 per month, repairs 30,000, fee 15,000 | 1,450 ÷ 0.01 = 145,000; then 145,000 − 30,000 − 15,000 | **100,000** |
| C-class, rent 1,100 per month | 1,100 ÷ 0.025 | 44,000 total investment |

The C-class result shows why landlords pay much less in lower-value areas: they take more risk with tenants, repairs and vacancies, so they need the house to cost far less compared with the rent.

## When cash is not enough

If the seller needs more than the MAO, there may still be a solution. Never invent one; escalate to the client, who decides whether to offer:

- **Retail Partnership Option** (novation-style): the client partners with a licensed agent to sell the home to a retail buyer, often after repairs, and the seller nets an agreed amount. Longer timeline (often 60 to 120 days), access needed. Best for good-condition homes in good areas.
- **Seller financing:** the seller is paid over time instead of all at once. Usually for owners with no mortgage.
- **Subject-to:** the buyer takes over payments on the existing mortgage, which stays in the seller's name. Carries real risks for the seller and must be fully explained and reviewed by the client's counsel.

These are explained in the Real Estate Glossary. The client's counsel and title company must approve the paperwork.

## Writing the offer email

Send a written offer after the offer conversation (never instead of it, unless the seller asked for email). Keep it short, warm and factual. Edit every template to fit the real situation, and replace every purple placeholder with the real details before sending.

## Check yourself

1. Write the MAO formula.
2. When would you use 65% instead of 70%?
3. ARV 200,000, repairs 40,000 (Needs Work), fee 12,000, normal market. What is the MAO?
4. What are the three numbers in the Narqix offer range?
5. A B-class rental would rent for 1,200 per month. Repairs 20,000, fee 10,000. What contract price does the rental check give?

<details class="toggle toggle-answers"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. MAO = (ARV × percentage) − repairs − the client's fee.
2. Slow market, heavy rehab, lower-value area, or low ARV confidence.
3. 200,000 × 70% = 140,000; minus 40,000 = 100,000; minus 12,000 = **88,000**.
4. Opening offer, target, walk-away (MAO).
5. 1,200 ÷ 0.01 = 120,000; minus 20,000 minus 10,000 = **90,000**.
