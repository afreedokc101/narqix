## The big picture in one minute

A deal is a relay race. Each person runs one part of the track, then passes the baton. The baton is the lead record in the CRM. If the baton is dropped (missing notes, wrong stage, no next action), the race is lost, even if every runner was fast.

Three rules hold the whole pipeline together:

1. **Every lead has one owner.** One named person is responsible for the next step.
2. **Every lead has one next action with a date.** "Call back Tuesday 4 PM seller time" is a next action. "Follow up" is not.
3. **Every stage change has a note.** The next person should never need to call you to understand the lead.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Clients judge a Narqix team by their CRM before they judge it by their phone calls. A clean CRM with honest notes makes the client trust every number you report. A messy CRM makes them doubt even your good work.

</div>

## The pipeline at a glance

| Stage | What it means | Owner | Exit when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. New Lead | Record loaded from a scrubbed list or an inbound source. Nobody has spoken to the owner yet. | Data team loads it, Cold Caller or SMS Agent works it | First attempt made |
| 2. Attempting Contact | We are calling or texting but have not reached the owner. | Cold Caller or SMS Agent | Live conversation, or dead (wrong, DNC, sold) |
| 3. Contacted / Qualifying | We spoke to the owner and are learning the Four Pillars. | Cold Caller | Lead is rated Hot, Warm, Nurture or Dead |
| 4. Warm Follow-Up | Some motivation, but timeline or price is not ready yet. | Lead Manager | Becomes Hot, or moves to Nurture |
| 5. Hot / Appointment Set | Motivation, timeline, condition and price discussed. An offer call or walkthrough is booked. | Lead Manager | Acquisitions holds the appointment |
| 6. Offer Made | Acquisitions presented a number or range. | Acquisitions | Seller accepts, counters or declines |
| 7. Negotiating | Seller countered, or needs time, or another decision maker must agree. | Acquisitions | Agreement reached, or lead returns to follow-up |
| 8. Under Contract | Seller signed the purchase agreement. All state disclosures completed. | Acquisitions hands to TC and Dispositions | File opened with title and buyer marketing starts |
| 9. Marketing to Buyers | Dispositions is presenting the deal to cash buyers. | Dispositions | A buyer signs and pays the deposit |
| 10. Assigned / Buyer Under Contract | Buyer signed the assignment or the second purchase contract and paid the deposit. | Dispositions and TC | Title clears all items |
| 11. Clear to Close | Title has no open items. Closing appointments set. | TC | Closing funds and records |
| 12. Closed / Fee Received | Deal closed and the client's fee arrived. | TC | File archived |
| Nurture | Not ready now, but not a "no". Long-term follow-up in 90 to 180 days. | Lead Manager or SMS Agent | Seller re-engages, or asks to stop |
| Dead | Lead will not move forward. Always with a reason code. | Whoever closes it | Never deleted, only closed with a reason |

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never skip a stage to make your numbers look better. Never move a lead to "Hot" because the seller was friendly. Hot has a definition (below). A wrong stage wastes the time of the most expensive person on the team, the acquisitions manager.

</div>

## Lead temperature: one definition for everyone

We judge every conversation using **the Four Pillars plus Three Checks**.

**The Four Pillars**

1. **Motivation:** Why would they sell? Is there a real problem the sale solves?
2. **Timeline:** When do they want or need to sell?
3. **Condition:** What does the property need?
4. **Price:** What number do they have in mind, and is it anywhere near workable?

**The Three Checks**

1. **Ownership and decision makers:** Are we talking to the owner? Who else is on the deed or must agree?
2. **Money owed:** Mortgage, liens, back taxes, HOA dues.
3. **Occupancy and listing status:** Owner living there, tenant, vacant? Listed with an agent?

| Temperature | Definition | Disposition code | Who gets it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 🔥 Hot | Clear motivation, timeline under 90 days, condition discussed, price discussed (even if high), owner is a decision maker | HOT or APPT | Same-day handoff to Lead Manager or Acquisitions |
| 🌤 Warm | Motivation is present but timeline is 3 to 12 months, or price is far off, or a decision maker is missing | FU-WARM | Lead Manager follow-up in 7 to 30 days |
| 🌱 Nurture | No motivation now, but polite and open to a future call | NI | Long-term nurture, 90 to 180 days |
| ❌ Dead | Sold, wrong number, not the owner, or asked not to be contacted | SOLD, WRONG, DNC | Suppressed with a reason |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** A high asking price does not make a lead cold. Many sellers start high and come down once they hear the real numbers. Motivation and timeline decide temperature. Price decides how hard the negotiation will be.

</div>

## Disposition codes (the Narqix standard set)

Use **one code per call**. Pick the code that describes the outcome of this call, not your hopes for the next one. Your client's CRM may use different labels; map them to these meanings.

| Code | Meaning | What happens next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NA / VM | No answer, or reached voicemail | Retry by the campaign cadence |
| BUSY / DISC | Line busy, or number disconnected | DISC twice: remove the number |
| WRONG | Wrong number, or the person is not the owner | Suppress this number for this owner |
| DNC | The person asked not to be called or texted, in any words | Internal do-not-call list **immediately**, across every campaign and client. Never call back. |
| NI | Not interested, but did not ask to stop | Long-term nurture in 90 to 180 days |
| CB | Asked for a callback at a specific time | Task set in the owner's local time |
| FU-WARM | Some motivation, no timeline yet | Lead Manager follow-up in 7 to 30 days |
| HOT | Motivation, timeline, condition and price discussed | Same-day handoff |
| APPT | Offer call or walkthrough booked | Calendar invite sent, stage moved to Appointment Set |
| LISTED / AGENT | Property listed with an agent, or the caller is an agent | Suppress or route by client policy |
| SOLD | Already sold | Suppress |
| LIT-FLAG | Hostile legal language, threats to sue, or classic pre-lawsuit questions | Internal DNC plus same-day escalation to your team lead |

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never code a stop request as "NI". "Not interested" and "do not call me" are different. If a person says anything like "stop calling", "take me off your list", "remove my number" or "don't contact me again", the code is **DNC**, every time, in every language.

</div>

<details class="toggle"><summary>Practice: pick the right code</summary>

1. "Yeah, this is my house, but I'm not selling. Maybe in a couple of years." → **NI** (polite, future possible)
2. "Who is this? Never call this number again." → **DNC**
3. "That's my mother's house. She passed last year. My brother handles it." → **WRONG** for this number, and add a note about the brother and the estate so the data team can research the correct contact
4. "I might sell if the price is right. We're moving for my job in about two months. The kitchen is old. I'd want around 300." → **HOT**
5. "I want to sell next year after my daughter finishes school. The roof is fine. Not sure on price." → **FU-WARM**
6. "Is this call recorded? What's your company's full legal name? Send me your do-not-call policy." → **LIT-FLAG**, add to internal DNC, escalate the same day
7. "Call me Thursday after 5." → **CB** with Thursday 5 PM in the owner's time zone

</details>

## Handoffs: how to pass the baton

A handoff is complete only when **three things** are true:

1. The CRM stage is updated.
2. The handoff note is written in the standard format.
3. The receiving person is notified in the way your campaign uses (CRM task, team channel message, or both).

### The standard handoff note

Write it in this order, every time. Short phrases are fine. Facts only. Put the seller's own words in quotes when they matter.

- **Lead:** (SELLER NAME), (PROPERTY ADDRESS)
- **Temperature and code:** HOT, APPT, or FU-WARM
- **Motivation:** the reason, in the seller's words
- **Timeline:** when they want or need to sell
- **Condition:** roof age, HVAC age, kitchen and baths, known problems, your suggested repair tier
- **Price:** their number, and how they arrived at it
- **Money owed:** mortgage, liens, back taxes, if known
- **Occupancy and listing:** owner, tenant or vacant; listed or not
- **Decision makers:** everyone on the deed or who must agree
- **Best contact:** phone, email, best time (in their time zone), and whether they agreed to receive texts
- **Promised next step:** exactly what you told the seller would happen, and when

<details class="toggle"><summary>Example of a strong handoff note</summary>

- **Lead:** (SELLER NAME), (PROPERTY ADDRESS)
- **Temperature and code:** HOT
- **Motivation:** Inherited from her father 8 months ago. Lives 4 hours away. "I can't keep driving up there to check on it."
- **Timeline:** Wants it done "before winter". Flexible on closing date.
- **Condition:** 3 bed, 1.5 bath, about 1,300 sqft. Roof replaced around 2015. Furnace "very old". Kitchen and baths original (1960s). Some water stains in one bedroom ceiling. Suggested tier: Full Cosmetic.
- **Price:** Asked 165K. Said a neighbor's house sold for that. Open to hearing an offer.
- **Money owed:** No mortgage. Not sure about property taxes this year.
- **Occupancy and listing:** Vacant. Not listed.
- **Decision makers:** She and her brother are both heirs. Brother "will go along with whatever I decide" but is on the estate.
- **Best contact:** Mobile, evenings after 6 PM Eastern. Agreed to receive texts.
- **Promised next step:** Told her someone from our acquisitions team will call within 24 hours to discuss numbers.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>Example of a weak handoff note (and what is wrong)</summary>

"Seller interested, call back. Wants good price. House needs work."

- No motivation. Why are they selling?
- No timeline.
- "Needs work" tells the acquisitions manager nothing.
- No price, no decision makers, no contact time.
- No record of what was promised.

The acquisitions manager now has to repeat the whole first call. The seller feels nobody listened. Trust drops before the offer is even made.

</details>

### Who passes to whom

| From | To | Trigger | Time limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Data team | Cold Callers and SMS Agents | Scrubbed, de-duplicated list loaded and tagged | Before the shift starts |
| Cold Caller | Lead Manager | FU-WARM or HOT where the seller needs more qualifying | Same shift |
| Cold Caller | Acquisitions | HOT with appointment booked (when the campaign allows direct booking) | Same shift, with calendar invite |
| SMS Agent | Cold Caller or Lead Manager | Seller replied with interest and agreed to a call | Within the shift, ideally within minutes |
| Lead Manager | Acquisitions | Appointment set with all Four Pillars covered | At least 2 hours before the appointment |
| Acquisitions | TC and Dispositions | Signed purchase agreement and completed state disclosures | Same day as signing |
| Dispositions | TC | Signed assignment or second contract plus buyer deposit | Same day |
| TC | Client and whole team | Closed and fee received | Same day |

## Dead leads still matter

Never delete a lead. Close it with a reason code so the client learns from the data:

- **Dead: Sold** (to whom, if they told you)
- **Dead: Listed** (with an agent, and the listing date if known)
- **Dead: Price** (seller's number is far above any workable offer, and they will not move)
- **Dead: No motivation**
- **Dead: Not the owner / no authority**
- **Dead: DNC** (also on the internal do-not-call list)
- **Dead: Unreachable** (all numbers tried by the full cadence)

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** "Dead: Price" leads are often the best leads 60 to 120 days later. Life changes, listings expire, repairs get worse. If the seller did not ask to stop, ask your Lead Manager whether to move the lead to Nurture instead of Dead.

</div>

## Check yourself

1. A seller says, "I'd sell in about two months if the money is right. I want 250. The roof leaks." Which stage and code?
2. What three things make a handoff complete?
3. A seller says "please don't call me anymore" in a friendly voice. Which code, and what must happen to the number?
4. Who owns a lead in the "Under Contract" stage, and which two roles receive it?
5. Why do we never delete a dead lead?

<details class="toggle toggle-answers"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Contacted / Qualifying moving to Hot, code **HOT** (motivation, timeline under 90 days, condition and price discussed).
2. Stage updated, standard handoff note written, receiving person notified.
3. **DNC.** The number goes on the internal do-not-call list immediately, across every campaign and client. Tone does not matter.
4. Acquisitions hands it to the TC and Dispositions on the same day.
5. Dead leads with reason codes show the client which lists, markets and messages work. Some dead leads become live again later.

</details>
