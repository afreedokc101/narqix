## Why people sell to an investor

An investor's cash offer is almost always lower than what a fully repaired house would sell for on the open market. So why would anyone accept it? Because for some owners, **certainty, speed and simplicity are worth more than the last dollar**.

An investor sale usually means:

- No repairs, cleaning or showings
- No agent commission
- A closing date the owner chooses
- No risk of a buyer's loan falling through
- A clean end to a stressful situation

Our job is to find the owners for whom these things truly matter, understand their situation, and present an honest option. **We find the problem, make its cost clear, and solve it.**

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never discourage a seller from talking to family, an agent or a lawyer. If a seller would clearly be better served by listing with an agent, it is fine to say so.

</div>

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** On the offer call, address the pain and amplify it, then solve it. Use the seller's own words: "You told me the roof leaks into the back bedroom and the tenant stopped paying in March. Every month that goes on, it costs you." Then show how the sale ends it, give the number, and stop talking.

</div>

## The motivation scale

Motivation decides almost everything. Listen for it in every conversation.

| Level | What you hear | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 3: Curious | "Just seeing what it's worth." "Not really planning to sell." | Nurture. Stay friendly. |
| 4 to 6: Open | "I might, if the price is right." "Maybe next year." | Warm. Learn the real reason. |
| 7 to 8: Motivated | "I need to sell this year." "I'm tired of dealing with it." | Hot when timeline and condition are clear |
| 9 to 10: Urgent | "Foreclosure sale is next month." "I'm moving in 3 weeks." | Hot. Same-day handoff. Handle with extra care. |

A helpful question: "On a scale of 1 to 10, where 10 means you would sell tomorrow, where are you?" Then: "What would make it a 10?" The second answer often reveals the real motivation.

## How to show empathy (the Narqix way)

Empathy is not a script trick. It is showing the seller you understand what they told you.

1. **Listen fully.** Do not plan your next question while they talk.
2. **Reflect.** Repeat the key point in your own words. "So the house has been empty since your tenant left in March."
3. **Name the feeling.** "That sounds exhausting." "That's a lot to handle on your own."
4. **Ask, don't assume.** "How has that been for you?"
5. **Respect the pace.** Some sellers need a second or third call before they are ready.
6. **Offer, don't push.** "If it would help, we can look at a simple option. If not, no problem at all."

## Seller situations and how to approach each

<details class="toggle"><summary>⚖️ Probate and inherited property</summary>

**What is happening:** The owner has died. Heirs or an estate representative now control the house. Often several siblings share it, some live far away, and the house may be full of belongings.

**What they may feel:** Grief, guilt about selling a family home, stress about siblings agreeing, overwhelm about the clean-out.

**What they need:** Patience, simplicity, help with belongings, and respect for the family.

**How to talk:**

- Start with sympathy: "I'm sorry for your loss."
- Ask gently: "How are you and the family doing with everything?"
- Learn who decides: "Is there an executor or personal representative for the estate?" "Are other family members involved in the decision?"
- Mention practical help only if it fits: buying with belongings inside, flexible dates.

**Avoid:** Rushing, talking about price in the first minutes, or treating the house as "just a property".

**Note for acquisitions and TC:** No one may sign until a court-appointed representative exists (or a small-estate process applies). Never pressure heirs to sign before the legal steps are done.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>🏦 Pre-foreclosure</summary>

**What is happening:** The owner is behind on mortgage payments and the lender has started foreclosure.

**What they may feel:** Fear, shame, denial, anger at the lender, and tiredness from many calls.

**What they need:** Clear facts, respect, and real options, including options that do not involve us.

**How to talk:**

- Never open with the foreclosure. Let them bring it up, or ask softly: "Is there anything about your situation that makes timing important?"
- Show respect: "A lot of good people go through this. You're not alone."
- Ask about the timeline: "Do you know if a sale date has been set?"
- Mention honestly that they can also speak with their lender or a HUD-approved housing counselor.

**Avoid:** Promising to "stop the foreclosure", giving legal advice, or creating panic. Some states have special rules for buying homes in foreclosure; follow your client's instructions.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>💔 Divorce or separation</summary>

**What is happening:** A couple must divide the property. One may still live there.

**What they may feel:** Emotional pain, conflict, and a strong wish for "a clean break".

**What they need:** Neutrality, fairness, and a process both owners trust.

**How to talk:**

- Stay neutral. Never take sides.
- Confirm decision makers: "Will both owners be part of the decision?"
- Offer to communicate with both, or through their attorneys if they prefer.

**Avoid:** Sharing what one spouse said with the other, or pushing one spouse to sign alone.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>🔑 Tired landlord</summary>

**What is happening:** A landlord is worn out by tenants, repairs, late rent or evictions.

**What they may feel:** Frustration, fatigue, regret about being a landlord.

**What they need:** A way out without more tenant stress.

**How to talk:**

- Ask: "How long have you owned it as a rental?" "What's been the hardest part?"
- Ask about the tenant: lease end date, rent paid on time, condition.
- Explain that some buyers purchase with tenants in place.

**Avoid:** Asking them to evict anyone for us, or making promises about what the buyer will do with the tenants.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>🏚️ Vacant property</summary>

**What is happening:** The house is empty. It still costs taxes, insurance and utilities, and risks break-ins, damage and city fines.

**What they may feel:** Worry, guilt, "I keep meaning to deal with it."

**What they need:** A simple plan and a clear finish.

**How to talk:**

- "How long has it been empty?"
- "What's your plan for it if you don't sell?"
- "When were you last able to check on it?"

**Avoid:** Exaggerating risks. State real facts gently, only if they fit.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>🧾 Tax delinquent</summary>

**What is happening:** The owner is behind on property taxes. Penalties grow and, eventually, the county may sell the tax debt or the property.

**What they may feel:** Embarrassment, money stress.

**What they need:** Respect and facts.

**How to talk:**

- Do not lead with the debt. Let the conversation open naturally.
- If they mention it: "That must be stressful. Do you know the deadline the county gave you?"

**Avoid:** Quoting public record details in a way that feels like you are watching them ("I see you owe 4,312 in taxes").

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>🚧 Code violations</summary>

**What is happening:** The city has cited the property (tall grass, damaged roof, unsafe structure, trash). Fines may be growing.

**What they may feel:** Pressure, annoyance with the city, overwhelm about repairs.

**What they need:** Relief from the repair burden.

**How to talk:**

- "Has the city asked for any repairs?"
- "Are there any deadlines or fines you're dealing with?"
- Explain that some buyers take on the repairs and city process.

**Avoid:** Promising that open violations or fines "disappear". They often must be paid or transferred at closing; the title company decides.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>📋 Expired or withdrawn listing</summary>

**What is happening:** The home was listed with an agent but did not sell.

**What they may feel:** Disappointment, doubt about the price, fatigue with showings.

**What they need:** A different path, without blaming their agent.

**How to talk:**

- Always ask first: "Is the property currently listed with an agent?" If yes, respect the listing; do not interfere with the agreement.
- If expired: "What do you think got in the way of it selling?"

**Avoid:** Criticising their agent. Never suggest breaking a current listing agreement.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>✈️ Out-of-state or absentee owner</summary>

**What is happening:** The owner lives far away. Managing repairs, tenants or vacancy is hard.

**What they need:** A process they can complete remotely.

**How to talk:**

- "How do you manage it from where you live?"
- Explain that closings can often be done with a mobile notary or by mail, as the title company arranges.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>📦 Relocation, job change or downsizing</summary>

**What is happening:** The owner must move by a certain date, or the home no longer fits their life (children moved out, health, retirement).

**What they need:** Timing that matches their move, and help with belongings.

**How to talk:**

- "When do you need to be in your new place?"
- "Would a flexible closing date help?"

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>🩺 Health, aging and seniors</summary>

**What is happening:** An owner may be moving to assisted living, or family members are managing the decision.

**What they need:** Extra patience, clarity, and involvement of trusted family.

**How to talk:**

- Speak slowly and clearly. Repeat key points.
- Ask: "Is there a family member who usually helps you with decisions like this?"

**Narqix rule:** If an owner seems confused, forgetful or unable to follow the conversation, stop qualifying. Note it, end the call kindly, and escalate. We never contract with someone who may not understand the agreement.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>🔥 Fire, flood or major damage</summary>

**What is happening:** A disaster has damaged the house. Insurance may or may not cover it.

**What they may feel:** Shock, loss, exhaustion with insurance processes.

**How to talk:**

- "I'm really sorry that happened. Is everyone safe?"
- "Has insurance been settled, or is that still in process?"
- Flag the lead as a red flag repair case for acquisitions.

</details>

<details class="toggle"><summary>💸 Financial hardship or bankruptcy</summary>

**What is happening:** Job loss, medical bills or debts. In bankruptcy, the court or trustee may need to approve a sale.

**How to talk:** Respectfully and factually. If bankruptcy is mentioned, note it clearly; acquisitions and the title company must handle the legal steps.

**Avoid:** Any comment about the owner's finances beyond what they share.

</details>

## Communication styles

People also differ in **how** they like to talk. Read the style in the first minute and adjust your pace. Note it in the handoff.

| Style | How they sound | What they want | How to adapt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct** | Short answers, fast, "just give me a number" | Results, speed | Be brief. Get to the point. Respect their time. |
| **Social** | Friendly, chatty, tells stories | Connection, being liked | Build rapport. Let them talk. Then guide gently. |
| **Steady** | Calm, slow, careful, avoids risk | Safety, no surprises | Explain the process step by step. Reassure with facts. |
| **Analytical** | Asks for details, skeptical, wants proof | Logic, data | Show comps, explain the math, send things in writing. |

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Match their pace, not their mood. If a seller is angry, stay calm. If a seller is grieving, slow down. If a seller is in a rush, get to the point. People trust people who feel easy to talk to.

</div>

## Words that build trust, and words that break it

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| "That makes sense." | "Calm down." |
| "Take your time." | "This offer expires today." (unless true and client-set) |
| "Here is how we got that number." | "That's the best anyone will pay." |
| "Who else should be part of this decision?" | "You don't need to ask anyone." |
| "If it's not a fit, that's completely fine." | "You'd be crazy to say no." |
| "I don't know, but I'll find out." | Guessing an answer |

## Check yourself

1. Why would an owner accept less than full market value from an investor?
2. What should you do if an owner seems confused or unable to follow the conversation?
3. How should you open a conversation with an heir of a probate property?
4. Name the four communication styles and one way to adapt to each.
5. A pre-foreclosure owner asks, "Can you stop the foreclosure?" How do you respond?

<details class="toggle toggle-answers"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Certainty, speed and simplicity: no repairs, no showings, no commission, a chosen closing date, and an end to a stressful situation.
2. Stop qualifying, end the call kindly, note it, and escalate. Never contract with someone who may not understand.
3. With sympathy ("I'm sorry for your loss") and patience, then gently learn who the decision makers and the estate representative are.
4. Direct (be brief), Social (build rapport), Steady (explain step by step), Analytical (show the data).
5. Honestly: you cannot promise that and cannot give legal advice. Explain what your client can offer, and mention they can also speak with their lender or a HUD-approved housing counselor.

</details>
