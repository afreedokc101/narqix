## What this role is

Most sellers do not sell on the first call. Industry experience shows that many deals come from the fourth, sixth or tenth touch, after the seller's situation changes. The Lead Manager is the person who makes sure that, when that moment comes, the seller is talking to us.

You are the **bridge** between the cold caller's first conversation and the acquisitions manager's offer. You deepen the relationship, fill the gaps in qualification, and book appointments that are worth the acquisitions manager's time.

## What you own

- **Every Warm lead and every Hot lead** that has not yet reached acquisitions.
- **Re-qualification:** confirming and completing the Four Pillars and Three Checks.
- **Follow-up cadences:** calls, texts (only where the seller agreed) and emails on schedule.
- **Appointments:** booking offer calls or walkthroughs, confirming them, and preparing the acquisitions manager.
- **CRM hygiene for your pipeline:** every lead has an owner, a stage, a next action and a date.
- **Reactivation:** working old Nurture and "Dead: Price" leads when the client asks.

You do **not** own: setting offer prices, negotiating terms, or sending contracts. If a seller asks "what would you pay?", you explain that the acquisitions team prepares offers after reviewing the property, and you book that conversation.

## Your tools (generic)

- **CRM** with tasks, stages and follow-up sequences.
- **Phone and dialer**, usually single-line for warm follow-up.
- **Texting platform** (only for sellers who agreed to texts, inside a registered campaign).
- **Email** for written follow-up and appointment confirmations.
- **Calendar** shared with acquisitions.
- **Scripts:** the Warm Follow-Up, Appointment Setting and Reactivation scripts in the Script Library.

## Your daily rhythm

| Block | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Start of shift | Check in with your campaign's method, start your tracker. Review new handoffs from callers and SMS. Review today's appointments. |
| Speed-to-lead block | Call every brand new Hot handoff first. The fastest response usually wins the seller. |
| Appointment confirmations | Confirm today's and tomorrow's appointments with sellers. Send reminders through the agreed channel. |
| Due follow-ups | Work every follow-up task due today, oldest first. |
| Acquisitions prep | For each booked appointment, send acquisitions a complete brief at least 2 hours before. |
| Reactivation block (if assigned) | Work aged Nurture and "Dead: Price" leads with a fresh reason to call. |
| End of shift | Every lead touched has a next action and date. Send your end-of-shift report. Check out. |

## Follow-up cadence (a Narqix starting point)

Your client may have its own cadence. If not, use this.

| Lead type | Days 1 to 7 | Weeks 2 to 4 | Months 2 to 6 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hot, not yet booked | Call within the hour of handoff. Then daily attempts, varying time of day, for up to 5 days | Twice a week | Move to Warm if no contact |
| Warm (FU-WARM) | One call on day 1 or 2, then one more that week | Weekly call or text (if allowed) | Every 2 to 4 weeks |
| Nurture (NI) | None | None | Every 60 to 90 days, with a reason to call |

**Limits that override every cadence:** no more than 3 attempts per owner in any 24 hours across all numbers and channels, calls only inside the owner's permitted window, and any stop request ends all contact. See Calling Compliance for Real Estate.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

**Coach's note:** Every follow-up needs a reason, not just "checking in". Use what the seller told you. "Last time you mentioned the tenant's lease ends in March. I wanted to see how that is going." Sellers remember the person who remembered them.

</div>

## Booking a strong appointment

An appointment is **ready** for acquisitions when you can answer yes to all of these:

- [ ]  Motivation is clear, in the seller's own words.
- [ ]  Timeline is known.
- [ ]  Condition is described well enough to pick a repair tier (Easy, Needs Work, Full Cosmetic, Full Gut).
- [ ]  The seller has shared a price, or at least agreed to hear an offer.
- [ ]  All decision makers will be on the call or at the walkthrough.
- [ ]  Mortgage or liens are known, or the seller agreed to share them on the call.
- [ ]  The seller confirmed the date and time, and knows who will call and why.

### The acquisitions brief

Send it with the calendar invite. Use the standard handoff note (see How a Deal Moves) and add:

- **Your read of the seller:** communication style (direct, social, steady or analytical; see Seller Psychology and Motivation), emotional state, what they care about most.
- **Objections already heard** and how the seller reacted.
- **Anything you promised.**
- **Comps or property details** the client asks you to attach.

## KPIs that matter

**Narqix starter targets.** Recalibrate per client after 2 to 4 weeks.

| Metric | Starter target |
| --- | --- |
| Speed to lead on new Hot handoffs | First attempt within 15 minutes during your shift |
| Follow-up tasks completed on their due date | 95% or higher |
| Leads with an owner, stage and dated next action | 100% |
| Qualified lead to booked appointment | 25% to 40% of Hot and strong Warm leads |
| Appointment show rate (seller attends) | 70% or higher |
| Briefs sent to acquisitions at least 2 hours before | 100% |
| Reactivated leads per week (if assigned) | Set by your team lead per list |
| Compliance exceptions | Zero |

<div class="callout callout-never">

**Never do this:** Never book an appointment the seller did not clearly agree to. Never tell a seller "we'll pay your price" or hint at a number to get them on the calendar. A no-show or an angry seller costs more than an empty slot.

</div>

## Handoffs

| You receive from | What |
| --- | --- |
| Cold Callers | HOT and FU-WARM leads with standard notes |
| SMS Agents | Sellers who replied with interest |
| Acquisitions | Leads that need longer follow-up after an offer ("not now", "too low for now") |

| You pass to | What | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acquisitions | Booked appointments with a full brief | At least 2 hours before |
| SMS Agent | Sellers who prefer texting, for light nurture inside the registered campaign | When the seller agrees |
| Data and List team | Contact info corrections, new decision-maker numbers | Same day |
| Team lead | Complaints, DNC disputes, LIT-FLAG cases | Immediately |

## Quality checklist

- [ ]  Every new Hot handoff was attempted within 15 minutes of arriving during my shift.
- [ ]  Every follow-up has a reason based on the seller's own words.
- [ ]  Every lead I touched has a next action and date.
- [ ]  Every appointment has all decision makers confirmed.
- [ ]  Every brief went out at least 2 hours before the appointment.
- [ ]  I respected the 3 attempts per 24 hours limit and every calling window.
- [ ]  Every stop request became DNC immediately.

## Common mistakes (and the fix)

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Letting leads sit with no next action | End every touch by setting the next task before you close the record |
| "Just checking in" calls | Bring a reason: their timeline, their tenant, the season, a repair they mentioned |
| Booking without decision makers | Ask early: "Who else would want to be part of this decision?" |
| Over-following up | Respect the cadence and the 3 per 24 hours limit. More is not better. |
| Re-asking everything the caller already learned | Read the notes first. Confirm, do not repeat. |
| Hiding bad news from acquisitions | If the seller is shaky, say so in the brief. Surprises waste the call. |

## Escalation rules

- **Immediately:** legal threats or pre-lawsuit questions (LIT-FLAG), any stop request dispute, a seller who seems confused or vulnerable, an emergency or safety issue.
- **Same day:** a seller angry about the client, a no-show pattern on a lead, a seller who says another company made promises in the client's name.
- **Weekly with your team lead:** cadence results, which lists produce appointments, and suggestions.
