## Title company or closing attorney?

In the US, a neutral third party runs the closing. Depending on the state, that is a **title company**, an **escrow company** or a **closing attorney**. Some states require an attorney to be part of the closing. You do not need to know every state's rule: the client and the closing office will tell you. On this page, "title" means whichever one runs the closing.

Title does five things for the deal:

1. **Holds money.** Deposits and closing funds go into their escrow account, not to the seller or buyer directly.
2. **Searches the title.** They check public records for the owner, liens, judgments and other problems.
3. **Clears problems.** They tell you what must be fixed, and they pay off liens from the sale proceeds.
4. **Insures the title.** They issue title insurance so the new owner and any lender are protected.
5. **Closes and records.** They prepare the settlement statement, collect signatures, disburse the money and record the deed.

## Choosing a title company

The client usually has preferred title companies. Your job is to use them, and to vet any new one before you send a file.

### Why vetting matters

Not every title company will close investor deals. Some refuse assignments. Some will not show an assignment fee on the settlement statement. Some will not allow a double close funded by the end buyer's money. Some will not touch subject-to. Discovering this two days before closing kills deals.

### Vetting checklist for a new title company

Call the office and speak to a closer or the escrow officer. Ask:

- [ ]  Do you close **assignments of contract**, and how do you show the assignment fee on the settlement statement?
- [ ]  Do you close **double closings**? Can the second closing's funds be used for the first, or must the first closing be funded separately?
- [ ]  Do you close **subject-to** transactions? (Ask only if the client does these.)
- [ ]  Can you prepare **separate buyer and seller settlement statements**?
- [ ]  Do you order the **municipal lien search** automatically, or must we request it?
- [ ]  What is your usual turnaround for the **title commitment**?
- [ ]  Do you offer **mobile notaries** for sellers, and **mail away** or **remote online** closings where allowed?
- [ ]  How do you verify **wiring instructions**? (The right answer includes a phone call to a known number.)
- [ ]  Who is our day to day contact, and who is the backup when they are out?
- [ ]  What fees do you charge for an assignment or a double close?

Record the answers in the title company's contact record in the CRM, with the date and the person you spoke to. Send the answers to the client and wait for approval before sending a file.

<div class="callout callout-coach">

The best title relationship is built on clean files. A TC who always sends complete packages, uses clear subject lines and answers questions within hours gets their files handled first. Closers remember who makes their job easy.

</div>

## The opening package

Everything title needs to start work, sent in one email, on Day 0.

### Always include

| Item | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Fully signed purchase agreement | Every page, every initial, every signature |
| All signed addenda and counteroffers | Name each file clearly so title can see the final version |
| Contact sheet | Seller, buyer, lender, agents, JV partner, client contact, you. Name, role, phone, email |
| Target closing date | From the contract |
| Deposit details | Amount, who is sending it, deadline |
| Deal type | Assignment, double close, Retail Partnership, subject-to, client purchase, JV |
| Request to order searches | Title search plus municipal, city, county and state lien searches |
| Request for written confirmation | Receipt of the file today, and a receipt for the deposit when it arrives |

### Include when it applies

| Item | When |
| --- | --- |
| Assignment agreement | Assignment files, once the end buyer has signed. If not yet signed, tell title it will follow. |
| B to C contract | Double close files |
| JV agreement | JV files. Tell title how the fee is split and between which entities. |
| Buyer entity documents | First file with a new title company, or when the entity is new. Usually: articles of organization, operating agreement, EIN letter, certificate of good standing and a signing authority resolution if the operating agreement requires one. |
| Seller disclosures required by the state | When the client's process requires title to hold copies |
| Known issues | Anything acquisitions learned: owner deceased, divorce, back taxes, second mortgage, tenant in place |
| Lender contact and loan details | Client purchases with financing, Retail Partnership files with a buyer's lender |
| Subject-to documents | The seller's signed acknowledgments, loan information and authorization, as the client's documents require |

Send entity documents in a **separate email** from the contract. Title often files them in a different place, and it keeps the main thread clean.

<div class="callout callout-never">

Never forward the original seller contract to an end buyer, a buyer's agent or a JV partner unless the client has approved it in writing. Title receives the full file. Everyone else receives only what they need.

</div>

## Intake checklist for the opening email

Run this before you press send.

- [ ]  Subject line format: (PROPERTY ADDRESS) · New File · (DEAL TYPE) · Close (CLOSING DATE)
- [ ]  Every attachment opens, is the final signed version and is named per page 11.
- [ ]  The seller name matches the contract exactly.
- [ ]  The buyer entity name matches the contract exactly.
- [ ]  Contract price, deposit amount and deadline in the email match the contract.
- [ ]  You asked title to order all searches.
- [ ]  You asked for written confirmation of receipt today.
- [ ]  You copied the people the client wants copied (and nobody else).
- [ ]  You set a reminder to call title in 4 business hours if there is no reply.

## Working with title day to day

### Know your people

| Title role | What they do | When you contact them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Escrow officer or closer | Runs the file and the closing | Most of your contact: status, documents, scheduling |
| Processor or assistant | Orders searches, chases payoffs, prepares documents | Document requests, payoff status |
| Title examiner | Reads the search and writes the commitment | Through the closer, for questions about requirements |
| Funder or disburser | Releases the money after closing | Through the closer, on closing day |
| Notary | Witnesses and notarizes signatures | Scheduled by the closer, confirmed by you |

### How to ask for things

- **One file, one thread.** Keep each file in its own email thread with the address in the subject.
- **Number your requests.** "1. Please confirm the deposit. 2. Please send the commitment. 3. Is the payoff ordered?" Numbered lists get numbered answers.
- **Give a reason and a date.** "We need the commitment by Thursday so the client can decide before the inspection period ends on Friday."
- **Call for urgent items, then email to confirm.** A call gets action. An email creates the record.
- **Be warm and short.** Thank them by name. Closers handle dozens of TCs; the polite, organized one wins.

### Cadence with title

- At least twice a week on every open file.
- Every business day during closing week.
- Within 1 hour when anything changes: new addendum, new date, new party, new issue.

## Seller information sheet and payoff authorization

Most title companies send the seller a short form asking for their full legal name, marital status, forwarding address, Social Security number for tax reporting, and details of every loan or lien on the property. They also ask the seller to sign an authorization so title can request mortgage payoff figures.

Your job:

1. Tell the seller on your first call that this form is coming and why. "The title company needs this to pay off your loan and send your money to you."
2. Help the seller understand each question. Do not fill in the form for them, and never ask them to send their Social Security number to you. It goes directly to title through title's secure method.
3. Follow up every 2 business days until title confirms it is back.
4. If the seller is stuck, offer a short call with the closer.

<div class="callout callout-never">

Sellers' Social Security numbers, bank details and ID copies go directly to title through title's secure method. Never ask for them by text, never store them in the CRM and never forward them by plain email.

</div>

## When title does not respond

| Time since your email | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 4 business hours | Call the closer. Leave a voicemail with the property address and the one thing you need. |
| End of the next business day | Call the main office and ask for the backup closer. Escalate Yellow to your team lead. |
| 2 business days with a deadline approaching | Escalate to the client. The client may call the title owner or choose to move the file. |
